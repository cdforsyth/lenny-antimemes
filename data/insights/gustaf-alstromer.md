## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Ninety Percent of Potential Customers Won't Engage

**INSIGHT:** "Most people aren't early adopters—approximately 90% of potential customers won't engage with new solutions. Founders must reach roughly ten prospects to find one genuinely interested early adopter."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / Psychological

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Good products find customers.
- **Psychological Antimeme:** Rejection means the product is wrong.
- **Social Antimeme:** Successful founders have high hit rates.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- 90% rejection rate is demoralizing
- We interpret rejection as signal
- Success stories don't mention the 9 nos
- The math is uncomfortable

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that good products sell themselves
- The belief that rejection signals product failure
- The comfort of expecting higher conversion

**Verification:**
> "Approximately 90% of potential customers won't engage with new solutions. Founders must reach roughly ten prospects to find one genuinely interested early adopter."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Expect 90% rejection** - Calibrate outreach volume accordingly
2. **Ask "what's holding you back from moving faster?"** - One question focus
3. **Use peer pressure for accountability** - Group goal-sharing works

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Strategy is post-PMF** - Execution only before that
2. **90% won't engage** - Early adopters are 1 in 10
3. **Peer competition drives progress** - Not intrinsic motivation alone

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Gustaf's antimemes share a structure: **early-stage startup success requires calibrating expectations and tactics to uncomfortable realities—most customers won't engage (expect 10 nos per yes), strategy is premature before PMF (execution only), and peer pressure drives accountability better than intrinsic motivation or professional structures**. Expect rejection. Skip strategy. Use peer pressure. The pattern reveals that YC's effectiveness comes from brutal realism about conversion rates, timing of strategic thinking, and motivation mechanisms—where conventional professional frameworks (strategy, intrinsic motivation, comprehensive reviews) actually slow progress at the stage where speed is everything.

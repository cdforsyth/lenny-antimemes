## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. The "Better Trap"

**INSIGHT:** Companies frequently fail by attempting to out-execute rivals in established categories. Even with massive distribution advantages and brand strength (like Threads), offering the same solution to an existing problem leads to failure. The trap: "better" feels like a winning strategy but isn't.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** "Better" is the universal product strategy.
- **Social Antimeme:** Competitive analysis focuses on beating rivals.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Improvement seems like the safe bet.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "Build a better mousetrap" is foundational advice
- Competitive benchmarking is standard practice
- Improvement requires less creativity than category creation
- The failure pattern (Threads) is explained away

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that better execution wins markets
- The belief that distribution advantages guarantee success
- The comfort of incremental improvement strategies

**Verification:**
> "Companies frequently fail by attempting to out-execute rivals in established categories. Threads exemplifies this—despite massive distribution advantages and brand strength, it 'cratered.'"

### 2. Categories Make Products, Not the Reverse

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 3
- Recognition: 2

- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Product-market fit is startup gospel.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Products feel like the primary variable.
- **Social Antimeme:** Founders want to talk about their product.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Product-market fit is universally taught
- Products are visible; categories are abstract
- "Build a great product" is actionable advice
- Challenging PMF feels heretical

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that product quality determines success
- The belief that PMF is the goal
- The comfort of product-centric strategy

**Verification:**
> "This concept assumes the best product wins. Actually, 'categories make products, not the other way around'—problem definition precedes solution viability."

- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Competitors are visible; status quo is invisible.

### 4. Backcasting Over Forecasting

**INSIGHT:** "Imagine success five years forward, then work backward to identify necessary actions—rejecting present constraints." Backcasting inverts normal planning: instead of extrapolating from current state, you define the desired end state and identify the path backward, deliberately ignoring today's limitations.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**

- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Planning feels like forecasting from now.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Backcasting requires rejecting current constraints.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Forecasting is the standard planning methodology.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Forecasting is embedded in business planning
- "Where are we going?" starts from where we are

**What's lost by accepting it:**

**Verification:**

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Compete against status quo** - Different opponent than competitive analysis suggests
2. **Lightning strikes over peanut butter** - Concentrate marketing in 1-2 bursts
3. **Backcast from desired future** - Plan by working backward, ignoring current constraints

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **The Better Trap** - Out-executing in existing categories fails even with advantages
2. **Categories make products** - Product-market fit inverts the actual causation
3. **Status quo is the real competitor** - Not other companies

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Lochhead's antimemes share a structure: **the standard competitive playbook (build better products, beat competitors, forecast from present, spread marketing evenly) fails because it accepts the existing category frame**. The Better Trap catches companies trying to win within existing rules. Categories make products inverts the assumed causation. Status quo competition changes the opponent. The pattern reveals that category design isn't a marketing tactic - it's a complete inversion of competitive strategy, where the frame matters more than execution within the frame.

## Stage 1: Popular vs. Rare Concepts

### Popular Takeaways (80-90% of listeners would mention)

1. **Hire when you've closed ~10 customers** - Sales hiring signal
2. **Hire two reps, not one** - A/B testing requirement
3. **50/50 base-to-bonus compensation** - Early-stage structure
4. **VP of Sales after two quota-hitting reps** - Critical timing
5. **"Sell me this pen" in interviews** - Demonstration requirement
6. **VPs must spend 20-30 hours selling** - Hands-on requirement

### Rare/Antimemetic Insights (1-5% would notice)

1. **Founders wrongly assume they can avoid sales teams** - Default misconception
2. **Hire people you'd personally buy from** - Not resume credentials
3. **100% commission for initial reps** - Build confidence first
4. **VP of Sales before two reps = ~100% failure** - Near certainty
5. **Give sales leadership quarterly feature budget** - Forces prioritization
6. **Sales consuming 20% of time is the signal** - Not revenue milestone

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Hire people you'd buy from** - Not resume credentials
2. **Wait for two quota-hitting reps before VP** - ~100% failure otherwise
3. **Give sales a feature budget** - Forces prioritization

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Most founders wrongly assume they can avoid sales** - Default is wrong
2. **"Would I buy from them?" beats resumes** - Credential bias fails
3. **VP before two reps = ~100% failure** - Near certainty

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Jason's antimemes share a structure: **B2B sales success requires abandoning the comfortable assumptions that technical founders hold—most founders wrongly assume they can avoid sales teams, hiring people you'd personally buy from matters more than credentials despite resume bias, VP of Sales before two quota-hitting reps has ~100% failure rate despite seeming logical, and giving sales leadership feature budgets forces prioritization**. Accept you need sales. Hire sellers, not resumes. Wait for proof before VPs. Give sales product influence. The pattern reveals that SaaStr's advice works by confronting the specific biases technical founders have about sales—where the instincts to avoid sales, trust credentials, hire senior early, and protect product from sales requests all lead to predictable, avoidable failure.

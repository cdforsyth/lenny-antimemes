## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. The "Bad Signal" Hiring Strategy

**INSIGHT:** "Companies like Palantir intentionally repel certain candidates to attract the right ones. 'A lot of the best recruiters put out this distinctive bad signal and it has to turn some people off.' This filters for mission alignment rather than broad appeal."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Attract as many candidates as possible.
- **Social Antimeme:** Broad appeal is good.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Don't turn anyone off.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Broad appeal is valued
- More candidates seems better
- Turning people off feels wrong
- The filtering mechanism is hidden

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that more candidates is better
- The belief that broad appeal helps
- The comfort of not repelling

**Verification:**
> Companies like Palantir intentionally repel certain candidates to attract the right ones. Nabeel notes: "A lot of the best recruiters...put out this distinctive bad signal and it has to turn some people off." This filters for mission alignment rather than broad appeal.

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Intentionally repel some candidates** - Filters for alignment
2. **Recognize gatekeeping incentives** - Technical isn't the blocker
3. **Grow PMs from field roles** - External hires fail

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **The bad signal hiring strategy** - Repelling filters for mission
2. **The no-titles paradox** - Changes what people compete for
3. **Data gatekeeping as job security** - Not technical, social

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Nabeel's antimemes share a structure: **building enterprise software companies requires accepting that human incentives override stated intentions—the bad signal hiring strategy works because repelling filters for mission alignment, the no-titles paradox shows you can't eliminate hierarchy only change what people compete for, data gatekeeping as job security means technical barriers aren't the real blocker, and PMs must come from BD because external hires lack customer empathy built through field experience**. Repel some candidates. Expect new status games. Recognize gatekeeping incentives. Grow PMs from the field. The pattern reveals that Palantir taught that enterprise success comes from understanding hidden incentives—where repelling beats attracting, new games replace old ones, social beats technical, and internal beats external.

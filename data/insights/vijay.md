**Analyzed:** 2026-01-20

## Stage 1: Popular vs. Rare Concepts

### Popular Takeaways (80-90% of listeners would mention)

1. **Focus on core product** - Don't spread thin
2. **Engineers need alignment** - Get buy-in
3. **Prioritization frameworks help** - Use RICE or similar
4. **Data tracking matters** - Understand user behavior
5. **Product expansion is important** - Grow into adjacencies
6. **Speed and quality balance** - Ship fast but well

### Rare/Antimemetic Insights (1-5% would notice)

1. **Invest profits, not people, for expansion** - Don't redirect best talent
2. **Being third-best is worthless** - Adjacent products drain resources
3. **Engineer bias toward no is a feature, not a bug** - Skepticism tests ideas
4. **Server-side tracking beats SDKs despite industry norm** - Eliminates event loss
5. **Speed and design are sequential, not parallel** - Separate phases
6. **RICE framework kills innovation prematurely** - Remove C and E temporarily

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Invest Profits, Not People, for Expansion

**INSIGHT:** "Most companies expand by redirecting their best talent to new ventures. The opposite: Invest the profits that come out of that core into the next venture. Invest profits and not people. Taking engineers away from your core leaves you vulnerable to competitors who can out-invest you in what made you successful."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**

- Recognition: 2

- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Organizational / Strategic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Organizational Antimeme:** Best people should tackle new challenges.
- **Strategic Antimeme:** Talent drives new venture success.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Reassign to expand.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Best people are moved
- Talent is the lever
- Reassignment is standard
- The core vulnerability is missed

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that best people should move
- The belief that talent drives new ventures
- The comfort of reassignment strategies

**Verification:**
> "Invest the profits that come out of that core into the next venture. Invest profits and not people."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Invest profits not people for expansion** - Don't weaken core
2. **Only build category-leading products** - Third-best drains resources
3. **Use RI not RICE for moonshots** - Remove confidence and effort

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Invest profits, not people** - Best talent stays on core
2. **Being third-best is worthless** - Adjacent products are net negative
3. **Engineer skepticism is a feature** - Rigorous testing before rejection

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Vijay's antimemes share a structure: **product expansion requires accepting counterintuitive truths about talent, positioning, and prioritization—invest profits not people for expansion because redirecting best talent weakens your core, being third-best is worthless because adjacent products drain resources despite appearing to add revenue, engineer bias toward no is a feature not a bug because rigorous testing beats forced alignment, and RICE framework kills innovation prematurely because confidence and effort cause premature deprioritization**. Keep best people on core. Only build to lead. Value skepticism. Use RI not RICE. The pattern reveals that building Amplitude taught that product success comes from inversions that feel limiting—where profits beat people, mediocrity is worse than nothing, skepticism helps, and frameworks hurt.

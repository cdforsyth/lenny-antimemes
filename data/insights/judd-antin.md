## Stage 1: Popular vs. Rare Concepts

### Popular Takeaways (80-90% of listeners would mention)

1. **User-centered performance** - Checking boxes rather than seeking truth
2. **Middle-range research is the problem** - Interesting but not impactful
3. **Researchers need business fluency** - OKRs, competitive analysis, funnels
4. **Integration over service model** - Partner from inception through completion
5. **Five essential research skills** - Including SQL and applied statistics
6. **Hire researchers from earliest stages** - Drive velocity, not slow it

### Rare/Antimemetic Insights (1-5% would notice)

1. **"We don't validate, we falsify"** - Looking to be wrong
2. **Multimillion dollar button** - 7 characters = millions in revenue
3. **Quick validation studies at project's end = checking boxes** - Not real research
4. **NPS is scientifically unsound** - Strong opposition from survey science
5. **AB tests reveal what, not why** - Limited without research
6. **"A researcher makes you go faster, not slower"** - Opposite of common belief

### 1. We Don't Validate, We Falsify

**INSIGHT:** "We don't validate, we falsify. We are looking to be wrong." The research mindset should be seeking disconfirmation, not confirmation.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Research validates ideas.
- **System Antimeme:** Validation reduces risk.
- **Social Antimeme:** Confirmation is progress.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Validation sounds productive
- Falsification sounds negative
- We want confirmation
- "Being wrong" feels like failure

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that research validates
- The belief that confirmation is the goal
- The comfort of validating intuitions

**Verification:**
> "We don't validate, we falsify. We are looking to be wrong."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Seek falsification, not validation** - Look to be wrong
2. **Involve researchers from project start** - Not just end validation
3. **Test small changes seriously** - 7 characters can = millions

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Falsify, don't validate** - Opposite research mindset
2. **Late validation is performance** - Not real research
3. **NPS is scientifically flawed** - Industry standard is wrong

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Judd's antimemes share a structure: **user research effectiveness requires inverting common practices—seeking falsification rather than validation, involving researchers at project inception rather than end, testing small changes that can generate millions, and abandoning scientifically flawed metrics like NPS that have become industry standard**. Look to be wrong. Start with research. Small changes matter. NPS is broken. The pattern reveals that research fails when it becomes performance—validating predetermined conclusions, arriving too late to change decisions, focusing on big initiatives over small high-leverage changes, and using metrics that provide comfort rather than insight.

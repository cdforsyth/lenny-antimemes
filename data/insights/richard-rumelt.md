**Source:** Lenny's Podcast - Richard Rumelt Interview (Strategy Legend, author of Good Strategy Bad Strategy)

## Stage 1: Popular vs. Rare Concepts

### Popular Takeaways (80-90% of listeners would mention)

1. **Strategy = diagnosis + guiding policy + coherent actions** - The kernel
2. **Goals are not strategy** - Ambition isn't design
3. **Bad strategy is word salad** - Vague language hides absence
4. **The Crux** - The hardest part you must overcome
5. **Focus ruthlessly (2-3 priorities max)** - Not a laundry list
6. **Look for "value denial"** - Things customers cannot buy

### Rare/Antimemetic Insights (1-5% would notice)

1. **90 out of 100 major company strategies lack merit** - Shocking prevalence
2. **Conflicting departmental interests are the real obstacle** - Not capability
3. **Call it "action agenda," not strategy** - Language matters
4. **Matrix organizations diffuse accountability** - Structural failure mode
5. **Conviction + flexibility, not commitment alone** - The balance

**INSIGHT:** "He estimates '90 out of 100' major company strategies lack merit." Most strategies aren't strategies at all—they're goals, aspirations, or word salad.

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** We have a strategy.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Strategy is what companies do.
- **Identity Antimeme:** Our strategy is thoughtful.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Every company claims to have strategy
- Strategy documents exist everywhere

**Verification:**

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Call it action agenda** - Force clearer thinking
2. **Address departmental conflicts** - The real execution barrier
3. **Advocate clear hierarchical decision-making** - Counter matrix diffusion

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **90% of strategies aren't strategies** - Most companies are faking it
2. **Politics, not capability, blocks execution** - Wrong diagnosis
3. **Matrix diffuses accountability** - Structural failure mode

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Richard's antimemes share a structure: **the obstacles to good strategy are hidden in plain sight—bad strategies masquerading as strategies, political conflicts disguised as alignment, accountability diffused through matrix structures—and the standard organizational vocabulary and structures enable this hiding**. Most strategies are word salad. Real barriers are political. Matrix enables diffusion. The pattern reveals that strategic failure is more about organizational self-deception than capability gaps—and that fixing it requires naming things honestly and making authority clear.

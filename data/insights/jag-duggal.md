## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Clarity Over Perfection in Strategy

**INSIGHT:** Kevin Systrom's insight: "We may not be right, but at least we are clear." Effective strategy requires specificity rather than hedged, vague aspirations that accommodate all possibilities.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Strategy should be right.
- **Social Antimeme:** Wrong strategy is worse than vague strategy.
- **System Antimeme:** Good strategy requires more analysis.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- We want to be right
- Clarity exposes us to being wrong
- Vagueness provides cover
- "More analysis" delays commitment

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that strategy should wait for certainty
- The belief that vagueness is safer than wrongness
- The comfort of hedged positioning

**Verification:**
> "We may not be right, but at least we are clear."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Be clear, even if wrong** - Clarity enables learning; vagueness doesn't
2. **Operationalize values with metrics** - 50% "very disappointed" threshold
3. **Invert industry sequences** - Credit-first banking worked

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Clarity over perfection** - Wrong and clear beats vague and safe
2. **Category design through inversion** - Fundamentally different entry points
3. **Setbacks as repositioning** - Failure became the pathway

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Jag's antimemes share a structure: **breakthrough results come from commitments that conventional wisdom avoids—clarity that risks being wrong, category inversions that defy industry logic, operationalized values that demand real measurement, and setbacks that become repositioning opportunities**. Be clear. Invert the sequence. Measure love. Failures reposition. The pattern reveals that Nubank's success came from making uncomfortable commitments: clarity over safety, inversion over imitation, measurement over aspiration, and persistence through failure.

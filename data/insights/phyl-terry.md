## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Narrowing Your Search Expands Opportunities

**INSIGHT:** "When you're looking for a job, you need a spear and not a net. Broad statements cause people to forget you, while narrow positioning makes people think of you for adjacent opportunities too. The paradox is that specificity generates more leads than breadth."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Cast a wide net for more options.
- **System Antimeme:** Breadth increases chances.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Be flexible about roles.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Wide nets feel safer
- Breadth seems logical
- Flexibility is valued
- The specificity paradox is hidden

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that breadth increases chances
- The belief that flexibility helps
- The comfort of keeping options open

**Verification:**
> "When you're looking for a job, you need a spear and not a net" because broad statements cause people to forget you, while narrow positioning makes people think of you for adjacent opportunities.

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Phyl's antimemes share a structure: **job search success requires accepting counterintuitive truths about positioning, help, and negotiation—narrowing your search expands opportunities because specificity makes you memorable for adjacent roles, asking for help demonstrates strength because it creates investment from others, negotiation success comes from collaborative problem-solving not adversarial tactics, and broad positioning makes you forgettable because openness prevents recall**. Use a spear. Ask for help. Present a mission. Get specific. The pattern reveals that coaching thousands of job seekers taught that career success comes from inversions that feel risky—where narrow beats broad, asking beats independence, collaboration beats adversarial, and specificity beats flexibility.

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. 40% Employee Turnover as Success Signal

**INSIGHT:** McCabe implemented changes that resulted in "approximately 40% employee turnover." This massive turnover was a feature, not a bug - it indicated the culture shift was real.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 3
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Organizational

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** High turnover indicates problems.
- **Organizational Antimeme:** Retain employees to preserve knowledge.
- **Identity Antimeme:** Good leaders minimize turnover.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Turnover is universally seen as negative
- 40% sounds catastrophic
- Employee retention is a celebrated metric
- The framing is deeply uncomfortable

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that turnover indicates failure
- The belief that retention is always good
- The comfort of low-turnover as success metric

**Verification:**
> "Resulting in approximately 40% employee turnover."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Price for value delivery, not capture** - Outcome-based pricing
2. **Do personal work to lead transformation** - Therapy as leadership tool
3. **Accept turnover as transformation signal** - Not retention at all costs

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **40% turnover as success indicator** - Inverts retention metrics
2. **Consensus was the disease** - Comfort caused stagnation
3. **AI disruption isn't optional** - "You don't have a choice"

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Eoghan's antimemes share a structure: **company transformation requires embracing what looks like organizational failure—40% turnover signals successful culture change rather than dysfunction, comfortable consensus caused stagnation rather than alignment, outcome-based pricing prioritizes value delivery over capture, and personal therapy work enables rather than distracts from business leadership**. Let people leave. Kill consensus. Give up pricing control. Do your internal work. The pattern reveals that Intercom's dramatic turnaround came from rejecting the metrics and approaches that comfortable companies celebrate—where low turnover, consensus, and complex pricing were actually symptoms of the decline they were experiencing.

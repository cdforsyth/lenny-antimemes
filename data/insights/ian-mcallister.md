## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Great Prioritization Generates 5X Impact

**INSIGHT:** "A product manager with great prioritization ability generates 5X impact compared to peers lacking this skill." The multiplier isn't 10-20% better - it's 5X. Prioritization is the highest-leverage PM skill.

- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**

- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** All PM skills seem equally important.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** 5X seems like exaggeration.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Prioritization is less visible than shipping.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Shipping gets celebrated, not prioritizing
- 5X multiplier sounds hyperbolic
- Prioritization is private work
- The impact attribution is delayed

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that PM skills contribute equally
- The belief that execution matters most
- The comfort of visible work over prioritization

**Verification:**
> "A product manager with great prioritization ability generates 5X impact."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Avoid pantry ingredients trap** - Start from customer problems
2. **Build trust through expectations met** - Set and deliver repeatedly
3. **Use PR/FAQ as thinking tool** - Not document template

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **5X impact from prioritization** - Highest-leverage skill
2. **Trust as leadership currency** - Underrated vs visibility/politics
3. **Impact over promotion focus** - Simpler guiding light

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Ian's antimemes share a structure: **the visible, celebrated PM activities (shipping features, gaining visibility, writing documents) matter less than the invisible foundations (prioritization, trust-building, thinking mechanisms)**. The 5X multiplier on prioritization captures it: the skill that creates the most impact isn't the skill that gets the most attention. The pattern reveals that PM effectiveness is more about what you choose NOT to do (wrong problems, wrong features, wrong focus) than what you ship.

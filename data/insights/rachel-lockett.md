## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Coaching Depletes When Misapplied

**INSIGHT:** "Most leaders assume providing answers builds capability, but the opposite occurs. When you try to advise and have the answer all the time, you're not actually equipping your team to go solve the hard problems. You're training them to become dependent rather than self-sufficient problem-solvers."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Identity / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Identity Antimeme:** Good leaders have answers.
- **System Antimeme:** Expertise should be shared.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Guide with solutions.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Having answers feels like leadership
- Sharing expertise is valued
- Guiding is standard
- The dependency mechanism is hidden

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that answers build capability
- The belief that sharing expertise helps
- The comfort of providing solutions

**Verification:**
> "When you try to advise and have the answer all the time, you're not actually equipping your team to go solve the hard problems."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Ask questions instead of providing answers** - Builds capability not dependency
2. **Own your career navigation** - Manager owns performance, you own career
3. **Seek understanding not victory** - The actual goal of conflict

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Coaching depletes when misapplied** - Answers create dependency
2. **Manager isn't responsible for career satisfaction** - It's your job
3. **Conflict isn't a problem to win** - It's data to understand

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Rachel's antimemes share a structure: **effective leadership and career development requires accepting counterintuitive truths about helping, responsibility, and conflict—coaching depletes when misapplied because providing answers trains dependency not capability, your manager isn't responsible for career satisfaction because that's your job to navigate, conflict isn't a problem to win because the goal is mutual understanding, and burnout signals misalignment not overwork requiring role redesign**. Ask don't answer. Own your career. Seek understanding. Redesign for strengths. The pattern reveals that executive coaching taught that professional effectiveness comes from inversions that feel unhelpful—where questions beat answers, self-ownership beats manager-blame, understanding beats winning, and redesign beats rest.

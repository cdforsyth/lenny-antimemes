## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Budgeting Tools Show Zero Impact Despite User Demand

**INSIGHT:** "A fintech app's comprehensive budgeting tool showed zero impact on spending behavior when tested across 10,000 users. Reason: budgeting requires too many decision points and cognitive load."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** User requests indicate value.
- **System Antimeme:** Features users want get built.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Budgeting should help spending.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Users explicitly request budgeting
- The feature seems obviously useful
- Zero impact contradicts intuition
- Cognitive load explanation isn't intuitive

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that requested features deliver value
- The belief that budgeting helps spending
- The comfort of building what users ask for

**Verification:**
> "Comprehensive budgeting tool showed zero impact on spending behavior when tested across 10,000 users."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Add engagement questions to signup** - 133% conversion increase possible
2. **Simple confirmation popups work** - 24% behavior change from "Are you sure?"
3. **Map 200+ steps before designing** - Find where interventions matter

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Budgeting tools don't change behavior** - Zero impact despite user demand
2. **Friction can increase conversion** - Adding questions helps
3. **"Right for wrong" motivation works** - Donuts increase voting

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Kristen's antimemes share a structure: **behavioral science reveals that product intuitions (less friction, build what users request, complex problems need complex solutions, pure motivation) often produce worse outcomes than counterintuitive alternatives (strategic friction, ignore stated preferences, simple interventions, indirect motivation)**. Budgeting tools do nothing. Adding questions helps conversion. Donuts increase voting. The pattern reveals that effective behavior change requires abandoning product common sense in favor of tested behavioral principles.

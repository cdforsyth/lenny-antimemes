## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Evals Are PRDs, Not Tests

**INSIGHT:** "If the model is the product, then the eval is the product requirement document." This reframes AI evaluation from quality assurance to product specification. Evals don't just measure whether a model works - they define what "working" means. This makes eval creation a product management function, not a QA function.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Testing comes after building; requirements come before.
- **System Antimeme:** QA and product management are separate functions/teams.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Evaluation sounds like judgment of existing work, not definition of future work.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "Eval" linguistically sounds like testing/measurement
- The AI development process feels like: build → test → ship
- PRD-writing is a different skill than test-writing
- Organizations separate these functions

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that AI development has the same structure as software development
- The belief that evals are downstream of product decisions
- The comfort of testing-as-quality-assurance framing

**Verification:**
> "If the model is the product, then the eval is the product requirement document."

### 2. AI Needs Experts to Know What Success Looks Like

**INSIGHT:** AI models can generate outputs but cannot evaluate them in novel domains. Determining whether a legal brief is good, a medical diagnosis is accurate, or a codebase is well-architected requires domain expertise that can't be bootstrapped from the model itself. This creates an irreducible role for human expertise in AI development.

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** AI improvement is assumed to be increasingly automated.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** "AI will do everything" narrative obscures human dependencies.
- **Social Antimeme:** Experts fear displacement and don't see the opportunity.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "AI will replace experts" is the dominant narrative
- The dependency on human judgment is less visible than AI outputs
- Experts are defensive rather than entrepreneurial about this
- The opportunity is hidden behind the threat narrative

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that AI development becomes fully automated
- The belief that AI replaces expertise rather than requiring it differently
- The comfort of simple "AI takes jobs" framing

**Verification:**
> "AI development requires human expertise to define success criteria, create quality benchmarks, and guide model improvement—a need that's creating substantial economic opportunity rather than purely displacing workers."

Mercor's growth ($1M to $500M in 17 months) demonstrates massive demand for expert human labor in AI development. The market isn't just automating existing jobs - it's creating new high-value roles for domain expertise.

### 4. Every Domain Requires Domain-Specific Evals

**INSIGHT:** There is no universal AI evaluation - each domain you want to automate requires experts from that domain to define what good looks like. This means the eval layer scales with the breadth of AI applications, creating demand for expertise across every field AI touches. The eval bottleneck is domain expertise, not evaluation methodology.

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Implementation

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Companies underinvest in domain expert hiring for AI projects.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** General AI feels like it should have general evaluation.
- **System Antimeme:** AI teams are often siloed from domain experts.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "General purpose AI" language implies general evaluation
- AI teams hire AI experts, not domain experts
- The scale of domain expertise needed isn't understood
- Cross-functional collaboration is organizationally hard

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that AI evaluation is a single methodology
- The belief that AI teams can evaluate AI without domain experts
- The comfort of centralized AI competency

**Verification:**
> "Models will need evals to measure success across every domain they want to automate."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Evals are PRDs** - Treat eval creation as product specification, not testing
2. **Every domain needs its experts** - AI projects need domain expertise at the eval layer
3. **Expert opportunity in AI development** - Position domain expertise as AI-enabling, not AI-threatened

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Creation, not pure displacement** - Reframe the AI/labor narrative from threat to opportunity
2. **AI needs experts to know what success looks like** - Irreducible human role in AI development
3. **Evals are the new PRDs** - Fundamental reframe of what evaluation means in AI

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Brendan's antimemes share a structure: **the bottleneck in AI development is human judgment, not compute or algorithms**. Evals require human definition. Domains require human expertise. Success requires human standards. The dominant narrative (AI replaces humans) obscures the countervailing dynamic (AI development requires humans). This pattern suggests that as AI capabilities expand, the demand for specific types of human expertise (evaluation, domain knowledge, quality definition) may increase rather than decrease - but in roles that don't yet have clear names or career paths.

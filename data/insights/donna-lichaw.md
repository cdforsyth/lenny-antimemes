## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Validate internal narratives externally** - Check self-perception against reality
2. **Calibrate limitations, don't eliminate** - Find the functional purpose
3. **Channel imposter syndrome** - Use it for learning, not eliminate it

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Limitations serve functions** - Dyslexia, perfectionism have benefits
2. **Imposter syndrome is functional** - Triggers productive behavior
3. **Self-stories are most effective** - Internal, not external narrative

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Donna's antimemes share a structure: **leadership development requires reframing what we consider problems—apparent limitations serve functional purposes requiring calibration rather than elimination, internal narratives we tell ourselves matter more than external communication, imposter syndrome triggers productive behavior when channeled rather than fought, and self-perception of weaknesses often contradicts how others actually see us**. Calibrate, don't eliminate. Check your self-perception. Channel the doubt. Tell yourself the right story. The pattern reveals that executive effectiveness comes from working with psychological patterns rather than against them—where the "problems" of limitations, self-doubt, and internal narratives become assets when understood functionally rather than fought against.

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Prioritize competence over likability** - The uncomfortable tradeoff
2. **Cultivate weak ties** - Breadth over depth in networking
3. **Pursue power deliberately** - Good people must have it to use it

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Success excuses everything** - Past rewritten by present
2. **Likability undermines success** - Competence over being liked
3. **Good people must pursue power** - Abstention is abdication

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Jeffrey's antimemes share a structure: **career advancement requires accepting uncomfortable truths that violate social norms—success rewrites history (past doesn't matter), likability trades off against competence (being liked isn't the goal), and good people must pursue power rather than avoid it (abstention enables bad outcomes)**. Be competent over liked. Build weak ties. Get power. The pattern reveals that career advice often fails because it optimizes for social comfort rather than effectiveness—where the virtuous-seeming paths (avoiding power, prioritizing likability, deepening existing relationships) actually reduce impact.

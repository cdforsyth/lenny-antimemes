## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. R&D Teams Need Separation, Not Integration

**INSIGHT:** "Most companies assume R&D should be embedded within product teams. I deliberately isolated GitHub Next as a separate unit, allocating only 5-10% of capacity to moonshots while reserving 60% for incremental improvements. Don't expect anything out of them that is going to turn into a money maker. They need space to create and experiment."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Organizational / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Organizational Antimeme:** Embed R&D in product teams.
- **System Antimeme:** Integration enables innovation.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Keep researchers close to product.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Integration is standard
- Embedding is valued
- Closeness is taught
- The separation benefit is hidden

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that integration enables innovation
- The belief that embedding helps
- The comfort of integrated teams

**Verification:**
> "Don't expect anything out of them that is going to turn into a money maker...They need space to create and experiment."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Isolate R&D teams deliberately** - Space to experiment
2. **Use simple metaphors for ethics** - Pair programmer clarifies behavior
3. **Optimize latency over features** - 200ms beats new capabilities

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **R&D teams need separation, not integration** - Isolation enables experimentation
2. **Scaling requires more PMs than engineers** - Dialogue is the constraint
3. **Latency precision matters more than features** - Micro-optimization trumps breadth

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Ryan's antimemes share a structure: **AI product development requires accepting counterintuitive truths about organization, ethics, and scaling—R&D teams need separation not integration because space enables experimentation, the pair programmer metaphor solves ethics by making abstract principles concrete, scaling requires more PMs than engineers because dialogue is the actual constraint, and latency precision matters more than features because flow beats capabilities**. Separate R&D. Use simple metaphors. Invest in dialogue. Optimize latency. The pattern reveals that building GitHub Copilot taught that AI product success comes from inversions that feel backwards—where separation beats integration, metaphors beat frameworks, PMs beat engineers, and latency beats features.

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. 10 Units Input for 1 Unit Output

**INSIGHT:** When Artifact was failing, Krieger noticed "10 units of input for one unit of output" - putting blood and tears into the product, launching things they were proud of, and metrics barely moving. This energy ratio is the diagnostic signal for when to quit.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Psychological / Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Psychological Antimeme:** Founders are trained to persist. "10 units in, 1 out" sounds like giving up.
- **Narrative Antimeme:** Success stories emphasize perseverance through hard times, not reading energy signals.
- **Temporal Antimeme:** Effort/output ratio requires reflection over time. In the moment, you're just working.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "The energy is not present in this product, in this system" is abstract
- Founders fear calling it too early more than too late
- The opposite (grinding through) is celebrated in startup culture
- No precise threshold for when the ratio becomes unacceptable

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The hope that more effort will change things
- The identity of the persevering founder
- The external narrative of "we're building something"

**Verification:**
> "It's like 10 units of input in for one unit of output versus the other way around. If we put blood and tears into the product and launch something we were proud of and metrics would barely move, the energy is not present in this product, in this system."

### 2. Metrics Obsession Signals Product Isn't Working

**INSIGHT:** You know when your product is really serving people. Metrics obsession happens when you're trying to convince yourself that it is when it's not. The metrics are rationalization, not discovery.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Psychological

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Organizational Antimeme:** Data-driven decision making is the dominant ideology. Questioning it sounds anti-intellectual.
- **Psychological Antimeme:** Metrics provide cover. "The data says..." protects against intuition-based failure.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** How do you operationalize "you know when it's working"? It sounds vague.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "Trust your gut over metrics" is dangerous advice to generalize
- Boards and investors want quantitative evidence
- The distinction between healthy metrics usage and obsession is fuzzy
- Admitting you're rationalizing requires uncomfortable self-honesty

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The cover that metrics provide
- The hope that you'll find the right metric that proves success
- The comfortable delay of facing the truth

**Verification:**
> "I think you know when your product is really serving people and it's doing a good job of doing that, and I think so much of when you get really metrics obsessed is when you're trying to convince yourself that it is when it's not."

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Psychological / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Psychological Antimeme:** New joiners are told to "listen first." Acting fast feels arrogant.
- **Social Antimeme:** Making big changes early alienates people who built the current system.

### 4. Claude Has No Chill

**INSIGHT:** If you put Claude in a Slack channel, it will chime in either way too much or too little. The conversational calibration problem - knowing when to engage vs. when to stay silent - is unsolved. Models need to learn conversational skills in the true collaborator sense.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Technical Antimeme:** Most AI development focuses on quality of response, not timing of response.
- **Framing Antimeme:** "Claude has no chill" is a funny phrase that hides a deep problem.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Conversational calibration is hard to benchmark or train for.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Elicitation (asking follow-up questions) feels like good UX until it's annoying
- "Just go and do it" vs. "engage me in dialogue" depends on user state
- The solution (train conversational skills) is vague
- No clear metric for "appropriate engagement"

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that better responses = better AI
- The simplicity of optimizing one thing (quality)
- The comfortable belief that more interaction is always better

**Verification:**
> "Claude has no chill. If you put Claude in a Slack channel, it will chime in either way too much or too little. How do we train conversational skills into these models? Not in a chatbot sense, but in a true collaborator sense."

### 5. Incumbents' Disadvantage from User Expectations

**INSIGHT:** Startups can try completely different form factors for AI interaction because users don't have existing assumptions. Existing products with hundreds of millions of users have people who assume how to use things - that's a disadvantage for radical innovation.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We think of distribution and brand recognition as pure advantages.
- **Strategic Antimeme:** "Leverage your installed base" is standard advice. This inverts it.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** "Do something very weird at the beginning" isn't actionable advice.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- ChatGPT's distribution looks like an insurmountable advantage
- "Users have assumptions" sounds like a manageable problem
- The advantage is only realized by startups that survive to scale
- "Weird and out there" products mostly fail

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that network effects compound forever
- The belief that existing users are pure asset
- The comfort of being the incumbent

**Verification:**
> "Do something that feels very advanced user, very power user, very weird and out there at the beginning, but could become huge if the models make that easy. And it's hard for existing incumbents to adapt to because people already have an existing assumption about how to use their products."

### 6. Everything Should Be an MCP, Not a Feature

**INSIGHT:** Krieger caught himself in a product review saying "we're building this whole feature - this shouldn't be a feature, this should just be an MCP we're exposing." The shift from features to exposed primitives changes how AI products should be built.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Frontier → Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Product thinking is feature-centric. "Expose primitives" is an architectural mindset.
- **Organizational Antimeme:** Teams are structured around features, not primitives.
- **Career Antimeme:** "I shipped a feature" is visible. "I exposed an MCP" isn't as celebrated.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Features are concrete; primitives are abstract
- MCPs require a different mental model of what "product" means
- The wife-who-couldn't-add-to-project-knowledge example makes it concrete, but most people haven't had that moment

**What's lost by accepting it:**

- The satisfaction of shipping discrete features
- The traditional PM job description

**Verification:**
> "I've started saying things like, 'Guys, we're building this whole feature. This shouldn't be a feature that we're building. This should just be an MCP that we're exposing.'"

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **10 units input for 1 unit output** - Use energy ratio as a diagnostic for when to quit
2. **Made changes too slowly** - Some organizational changes should happen faster than instinct suggests
3. **TypeScript PR in an hour** - Language barriers to contribution are collapsing; try crossing them

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Metrics obsession signals product isn't working** - You know when it works; metrics rationalize doubt
2. **Everything should be an MCP, not a feature** - Primitives over features
3. **Incumbents' disadvantage from user expectations** - Distribution can constrain innovation

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Mike's antimemes share a structure: **the invisible system dynamics matter more than the visible artifacts**. Energy flow (10 units in, 1 out) is invisible but diagnostic. Metrics obsession is a symptom of doubt, not cause of clarity. User expectations are a hidden constraint on incumbents. Conversational calibration is unsolved but unnoticed. The quiet 3 AM moments don't show up in dashboards. The antimemetic quality comes from the gap between what's measured and what matters - product culture celebrates visible metrics, shipped features, and user growth, but the underlying dynamics (energy, doubt, expectations, timing) determine outcomes. Mike's consistent move is to name the invisible system: the energy not present, the assumptions constraining, the calibration missing. The deeper pattern: legibility and importance are inversely correlated in product development.

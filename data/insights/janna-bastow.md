## Stage 1: Popular vs. Rare Concepts

### Popular Takeaways (80-90% of listeners would mention)

1. **Now, Next, Later framework** - Three-column roadmap replacing timelines
2. **Roadmaps as strategy prototypes** - Testing assumptions, not delivery plans
3. **Gantt-style roadmaps create false certainty** - Uncertainty increases with distance
4. **Separate soft launch from hard launch** - Development vs. marketing coordination
5. **Product vision using Geoffrey Moore's template** - Target, need, category, differentiators
6. **High-performing teams practice discovery** - Psychological safety, retrospectives

### Rare/Antimemetic Insights (1-5% would notice)

1. **"The value isn't in your roadmap, the value is in the roadmapping process"** - Process over artifact
2. **Dates are optional additions, not defaults** - Only when genuinely needed
3. **Events are "extremely risky and expensive"** - Community building hidden costs
4. **Consistency beats scale for community building** - Monthly ProductTank events
5. **Reduced silos lead to healthier roadmapping** - Organizational structure affects process
6. **Don't wait to feel fully prepared** - Founders learn by doing

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. The Value Is in the Process, Not the Artifact

**INSIGHT:** "The value isn't in your roadmap, the value is in the roadmapping process." The document is a byproduct; the thinking and alignment it forces is the actual output.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Deliverables are the work.
- **System Antimeme:** Documents communicate decisions.
- **Social Antimeme:** Stakeholders need the roadmap.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Artifacts are tangible, process isn't
- We're evaluated on outputs
- The process seems like overhead
- Documents feel like the product

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that roadmaps are for communication
- The belief that the document is the value
- The comfort of evaluating visible outputs

**Verification:**
> "The value isn't in your roadmap, the value is in the roadmapping process."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Make dates optional, not default** - Add only when genuinely needed
2. **Focus on process, not artifact** - The thinking is the value
3. **Fix structure to fix roadmapping** - Silos cause bad roadmaps

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Process over artifact** - The roadmap isn't the value
2. **Dates as exceptions** - Not the default planning frame
3. **Structure shapes process** - Org problems cause roadmap problems

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Janna's antimemes share a structure: **roadmapping pathologies come from misattributing where the value lies—the value is in the thinking process not the document, dates should be exceptions not defaults, org structure shapes roadmap quality more than frameworks do, and community building through events is riskier than success stories suggest**. Value the process. Default to no dates. Fix the structure. The pattern reveals that roadmapping problems are usually symptoms of deeper issues: treating artifacts as outputs, defaulting to false precision, and applying process fixes to structural problems.

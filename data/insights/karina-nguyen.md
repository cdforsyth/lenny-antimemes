## Stage 1: Popular vs. Rare Concepts

### Popular Takeaways (80-90% of listeners would mention)

1. **Soft skills become more valuable as AI handles technical work** - Creative thinking, listening, management
2. **Model training is more art than science** - Focus on data quality
3. **Synthetic data enables infinite task generation** - Beyond internet-scraped training
4. **Coding and design may be automated** - Technical skills at risk
5. **o1 chain-of-thought enables extended reasoning** - New model capabilities
6. **Anthropic vs. OpenAI cultural differences** - Careful crafting vs. creative freedom

### Rare/Antimemetic Insights (1-5% would notice)

2. **"Emotional intelligence and creativity are hardest for models to master"** - Not just technical tasks
3. **Asynchronous background agents replace synchronous chat** - Interface shift
4. **Computer-use agents struggle with pixel-based perception** - Technical limits
5. **Rapid iteration capability as key human skill** - Responsiveness over building

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Develop rapid iteration capability** - The skill, not just the process
2. **Invest in emotional intelligence** - Hardest for models to master
3. **Prepare for async agent workflows** - Chat is temporary

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Emotional intelligence hardest for AI** - Technical skills are more at risk
2. **Async agents replace chat** - Conversation isn't the paradigm
3. **Training contradictions confuse models** - More isn't always better

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Karina's antimemes share a structure: **the AI future inverts conventional tech career wisdom—emotional intelligence and creativity (traditionally "soft") are what AI struggles with most, rapid iteration (traditionally "rework") is a key capability, and the chat interface (currently dominant) is temporary as async agents emerge**. Build soft skills. Get good at iteration. Prepare for background agents. The pattern reveals that preparing for AI's impact requires developing the capabilities that seem least technical—where the "soft" skills become hard moats and the dominant interfaces are transitional.

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Even ChatGPT Needed Different Marketing

**INSIGHT:** "ChatGPT's massive awareness masked the real problem: 'I don't know what to use it for.' Everyone knew it, but didn't know how to apply it."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Viral products don't need marketing.
- **System Antimeme:** Awareness equals success.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Growth solves messaging.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Viral seems like enough
- Awareness is visible
- Growth hides problems
- The use-case gap is hidden

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that viral products don't need marketing
- The belief that awareness equals success
- The comfort of organic growth

**Verification:**
> "Everyone knew of ChatGPT, but when you clicked one zoom level further, the thing that came up was, 'I don't know what to use it for.'"

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Market viral products for use cases** - Awareness isn't enough
2. **Never compete on price** - Strategic suicide
3. **Distrust playbooks** - Context mismatch harms

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Even ChatGPT needed marketing** - For use cases, not awareness
2. **Playbooks are actively harmful** - False confidence
3. **AI makes taste more important** - Not less

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Krithika's antimemes share a structure: **marketing in the AI era requires rejecting comfortable assumptions—even ChatGPT's viral success masked the real problem of users not knowing how to use it, being cheaper is strategic suicide as capabilities commoditize, playbooks create false confidence without context understanding, and AI makes taste more important not less as drivel floods in**. Market use cases. Never compete on price. Distrust playbooks. Develop taste. The pattern reveals that first marketing hires at OpenAI and Stripe learned that marketing intuitions invert in AI—where viral isn't enough, cheap fails, playbooks harm, and human judgment matters more.

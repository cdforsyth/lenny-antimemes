## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. The "Missing Middle" Problem

**INSIGHT:** "Most organizations believe their issues stem from poor employee training. However, 99% of the time it's actually in the way that they're setting their goals and deploying strategy. Teams lack context connecting daily work to business objectives."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**

- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**

- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Organizational

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Training solves performance issues.
- **Organizational Antimeme:** Employees need upskilling.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Invest in learning and development.

**Why it resists spreading:**

- Employee skills are blamed

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that training solves problems
- The belief that employees need upskilling

**Verification:**

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Fix strategy deployment not training** - 99% is goal setting
2. **Extract existing strategy** - It lives in people's heads
3. **Prioritize 2-3 major movers** - Not scattered features

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **The missing middle problem** - Training isn't the fix
2. **Iteration applies to processes** - Methodologies deserve experimentation
3. **Strategy deployment is the failure** - Not strategy creation

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Melissa's antimemes share a structure: **product strategy effectiveness requires accepting that problems are in deployment and process adherence, not creation and training—the missing middle problem shows 99% of issues are goal setting not employee training, strategy exists but stays hidden in people's heads, strategic intent over feature count means ruthlessly prioritizing 2-3 movers, and iteration applies to processes because methodologies deserve experimentation not dogmatic adherence**. Fix deployment. Extract hidden strategy. Ruthlessly prioritize. Experiment on processes. The pattern reveals that product strategy expertise taught that effectiveness comes from the inversions that feel lazy—where deployment beats training, extraction beats creation, concentration beats spreading, and experimentation beats adherence.

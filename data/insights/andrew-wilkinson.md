# Antimeme Analysis: Andrew Wilkinson on Lenny's Podcast

**Source:** Lenny's Podcast - Andrew Wilkinson Interview (Co-founder/CEO of Tiny, Barista to Billionaire)
**Analyzed:** 2026-01-19

## Stage 1: Popular vs. Rare Concepts

### Popular Takeaways (80-90% of listeners would mention)

1. **Fish where the fish are** - Find underserved niches, not crowded markets
2. **Boring businesses are underrated** - Funeral homes, pest control, government form software beat cafes
3. **Baby weights first** - Start simple, don't try to deadlift 300 pounds on day one
4. **Moats matter** - Brand, network effects, switching costs create defensibility

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. CEOs Are Elephants, You're the Rider

**INSIGHT:** When Andrew hired a CEO who believed in enterprise sales, but Andrew wanted organic marketing, the CEO did enterprise sales anyway. CEOs will do what they believe in regardless of what you tell them. The only solution is to hire someone whose instincts already match what you need.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Admitting you can't direct your CEO undermines the investor/board narrative.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** "I hired them, so I can manage them" feels logical but isn't true.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Listening for instincts during interviews requires different skill than evaluating competence.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Boards want to believe they can guide executives
- "Coach them up" is the dominant hiring philosophy
- Admitting loss of control feels like failure
- Interview processes aren't designed for instinct detection

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that management can redirect senior leaders
- The belief that strategy alignment can be achieved post-hire
- The comfort of thinking you can change people

**Verification:**
> "Hiring CEOs is like they're an elephant and you're the rider. They're going to go wherever it is that they want to go."

### 2. Hiring for Potential Is Wrong

**INSIGHT:** The common advice is "hire for potential, coach them into what you need." Andrew has found the opposite: hire someone already fully formed who can do exactly what you need. Mentoring someone into being a good employee has never worked for him.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** "Develop your people" is culturally sacred; questioning it sounds callous.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Potential feels like upside; reality is potential often doesn't materialize.
- **System Antimeme:** HR processes are built around development, not selection.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Development narratives are optimistic and popular
- "Hire for potential" sounds meritocratic
- Admitting you can't change people feels pessimistic
- Training programs exist to develop people

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that people grow into roles
- The belief that coaching creates competence
- The comfort of giving people chances

**Verification:**
> "I think you need to hire someone who already is fully formed and can do what you need... I just haven't found that. I think you need to hire someone who already is fully formed."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Hire fully formed, not for potential** - Stop trying to coach people into competence
2. **Listen for CEO instincts in interviews** - They'll do what they believe regardless
3. **Get ADHD evaluated if you're an entrepreneur** - 30% vs 5% base rate; explains a lot

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Billionaires still compare to peers** - The hedonic treadmill has no exit
2. **Lifestyle vs venture is false dichotomy** - Bootstrap to $300M without ever burning cash
3. **The Palm Treo phase** - Current AI agents are pre-iPhone; the disruption hasn't really started

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Andrew's antimemes share a structure: **what looks like wisdom is often cope, and what looks like constraint is often freedom**. "Hire for potential" sounds wise but is cope. "You need VC to get big" sounds like a constraint but is actually a path to losing control. "Money will make you happy" sounds like wisdom but is cope. The antimemetic quality comes from inverting conventional startup narratives - the things everyone agrees on (raise money, develop people, success brings happiness) may be precisely wrong. The path that looks limiting (bootstrap, hire formed, diagnose yourself) may be the one that actually works.

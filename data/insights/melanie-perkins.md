## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. You Feel Completely Inadequate Before It

**INSIGHT:** A Crazy Big Goal should make you feel completely inadequate before it. If you don't feel inadequate, the goal isn't big enough. The inadequacy is the feature, not the bug.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Psychological

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Psychological Antimeme:** Inadequacy is aversive. We avoid goals that make us feel inadequate.
- **Goal-setting Antimeme:** SMART goals teach achievability. Inadequacy feels like failure before starting.
- **Social Antimeme:** "I feel completely inadequate" is not something people share publicly.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Inadequacy is an emotion people flee from
- Goal-setting frameworks optimize for achievability
- "If you feel inadequate, the goal is right" inverts normal advice
- The mechanism (inadequacy → motivation) isn't intuitive

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The comfort of achievable goals
- The confidence of knowing you can do it
- The protection from visible failure

**Verification:**
> "The thing that I love about a crazy big goal is that you feel completely inadequate before it. You want to work really hard to will it into existence."

### 2. I Thought the Investors Were Wrong

**INSIGHT:** After 100+ investor rejections, Melanie didn't question her vision - she thought the investors were wrong. She used their feedback to improve the pitch deck, but maintained conviction in the core idea.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 4
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Psychological / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Saying "they were wrong" after rejection sounds like denial or arrogance.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** 100 smart people disagreeing with you is strong evidence you're wrong.
- **Psychological Antimeme:** Rejection erodes confidence. Maintaining conviction requires extraordinary belief.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "100 investors can't all be wrong" is the intuitive response
- Distinguishing between "improve pitch" and "change vision" requires nuance
- Survivorship bias: we only hear this from winners
- The confidence required sounds like delusion until it works

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The humility of listening to experts
- The comfort of changing course based on feedback
- The protection of consensus

**Verification:**
> "It was really clear in my mind that it was the future and I thought the investors were wrong, frankly."

### 3. Taking Bricks from Someone Else's House

**INSIGHT:** When scaling, companies often import systems from other companies ("they've done it at a bigger company"). This rarely works. It's like taking bricks from someone else's house and sticking them in yours - they don't match.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**

- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Organizational Antimeme:** New executives are hired specifically to bring in outside systems.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Building authentic systems from scratch is harder than importing.
- **Social Antimeme:** "We do things differently" sounds like NIH syndrome.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Executives are valued for bringing experience from other companies
- "Best practices" is the language of importing systems

### 4. Timing Never Accurate (Which is Fine)

**INSIGHT:** Canva consistently achieves its Crazy Big Goals but almost never on the predicted timeline. Six months becomes two years. This is okay - the goal is achieved, just not when expected. The timeline is less important than the direction.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Organizational

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Organizational Antimeme:** Companies are managed by quarters and timelines. Missing deadlines feels like failure.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We conflate "achieve the goal" with "achieve it on time."
- **Planning Antimeme:** Goals without timelines feel incomplete. "We'll get there eventually" sounds vague.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Elon Musk gets criticized for timeline misses even though he achieves the goals
- Boards and investors want predictability
- "We don't know when" feels like lack of control
- The mechanism (direction more important than speed) contradicts urgency culture

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The accountability of deadlines
- The satisfaction of hitting milestones on time
- The external pressure that drives execution

**Verification:**
> "I think with a crazy big goal, the thing we have been really great at is achieving them. The timeframe that we achieve them on has not always been very reliable."

### 6. Systems Break at Every Doubling

**INSIGHT:** Every time the company doubles in size, almost all systems break. What worked stops working. This isn't failure - it's predictable. The constant is change, not stability.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**

- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Organizational Antimeme:** Companies want stable processes. "Everything breaks every time we grow" is unsettling.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We expect systems that work to keep working. Predictable breakage is counterintuitive.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Knowing systems will break doesn't tell you how to rebuild them.

**Why it resists spreading:**

**What's lost by accepting it:**

- The comfort of stable processes

**Verification:**

**INSIGHT:** When planning, think about what is terrible failure, not just what is wild success. The negative vision clarifies as much as the positive one.

**Why it resists spreading:**

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **You feel completely inadequate before it** - Use inadequacy as a signal the goal is right
2. **That first step is embarrassing** - Embrace the embarrassment, share early anyway
3. **What is terrible failure** - Plan by imagining both wild success and terrible failure

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **I thought the investors were wrong** - 100 rejections doesn't mean you're wrong
2. **Taking bricks from someone else's house** - Imported systems don't match
3. **Everything great was once imagined** - Imagination, not execution, is the bottleneck

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Melanie's antimemes share a structure: **the uncomfortable feeling is the signal that you're doing it right**. Inadequacy means the goal is big enough. Embarrassment means you're at the beginning. Investor rejection means you're seeing something they don't. Systems breaking means you're growing. The antimemetic quality comes from emotional valence - we flee from inadequacy, embarrassment, and rejection, but these are exactly the signs of progress. Melanie's consistent move is to reframe aversive signals as positive indicators. The deeper pattern: discomfort is information, not warning.

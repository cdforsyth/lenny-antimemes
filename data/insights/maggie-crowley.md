## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Most of Product Management Is Carrying Water

**INSIGHT:** "Most of product management is carrying water—doing the unglamorous, repetitive work that keeps projects moving. The role is less about big strategic moments and more about consistent execution of small tasks."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** PM role is glamorized as strategic.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Career advice focuses on big decisions.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** "Water carrying" sounds like grunt work.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- PM content focuses on frameworks and strategy
- "Carrying water" doesn't make good content
- The glamorous version gets more engagement
- Admitting the mundane is unsexy

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that PM is mainly strategic work
- The belief that frameworks are the hard part
- The comfort of focusing on the interesting bits

**Verification:**
> "Most of product management is carrying water."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Follow up consistently** - So rare it differentiates
2. **Embrace water carrying** - The unglamorous work is the job
3. **Decide before certainty** - Strong opinions from weak data

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **PM is mostly carrying water** - Not strategy, execution of small tasks
2. **Simplicity requires more work** - Not less
3. **Strong opinions precede data** - Certainty comes too late

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Maggie's antimemes share a structure: **the unglamorous, invisible, and simple aspects of product work (carrying water, follow-up, simplification) are actually the differentiating and difficult parts**. PM discourse focuses on frameworks and strategy; the real job is consistent execution of boring tasks. Follow-up is rare because it's boring. Simplicity is hard because it requires more work. The pattern reveals that PM excellence comes from embracing the mundane parts that most people skip.

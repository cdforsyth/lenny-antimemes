## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. 75% of Leaders Operate From Fear and Defensiveness

**INSIGHT:** "Approximately 75% of leaders operate reactively—from fear and defensiveness—rather than from openness and purpose." The default mode is reactive, not creative. Most leaders aren't leading from their best selves.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Leaders are assumed to lead well.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** 75% seems shockingly high.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Leadership = creative mode is the assumption.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Leaders don't want to admit reactivity
- The assessment is unflattering
- "Fear and defensiveness" sounds harsh
- The 75% challenges leadership narratives

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that leaders are in creative mode
- The belief that leadership training is effective
- The comfort of default leadership competence

**Verification:**
> "Approximately 75% of leaders operate reactively—from fear and defensiveness."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Identify your reactive posture** - Approval, rightness, or control
2. **Address environmental reinforcement** - Not just mindset
3. **Shift investment from technical to people** - As you advance

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **75% of leaders in reactive mode** - Default is fear-based
2. **Complete reboot required** - Not skill addition
3. **Imposter syndrome is environmentally reinforced** - Systemic, not personal

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Ken's antimemes share a structure: **leadership development fails when it treats symptoms (skills, confidence, control) rather than underlying system architecture (operating system, environmental reinforcement, reactive patterns)**. The 75% statistic anchors it: most leaders operate from fear, but the advice they receive assumes creative mode. The pattern reveals that effective leadership coaching must go deeper than skills or mindset - it requires system-level transformation that most development programs avoid.

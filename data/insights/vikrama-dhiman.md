The opposite reveals: some product managers whose product didn't have the impact really grew. Success depends on execution quality and visibility, not just working on important initiatives."

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Career Antimeme:** High-impact work drives advancement.
- **Narrative Antimeme:** Work on important things to succeed.
- **System Antimeme:** Impact equals recognition.

**Why it resists spreading:**

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Focus on execution quality over impact** - Especially early career
2. **Invest in artifact quality** - PRDs and work products matter
3. **Question your self-labels** - Stories limit behavior

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Impact doesn't equal career growth** - Execution and visibility matter
2. **Early career needs less strategic thinking** - Just get stuff done
3. **Self-perception actively sabotages growth** - Stories shape behavior

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Vikrama's antimemes share a structure: **career growth requires accepting counterintuitive truths about impact, strategy, and self-perception—impact doesn't equal career growth because execution quality and visibility matter more, early career success requires less strategic thinking because just getting stuff done builds credibility, artifacts matter more than outcomes because visibility through work products drives momentum, and your self-perception actively sabotages growth because stories you tell yourself shape limiting behavior**. Focus on execution. Get things done. Build quality artifacts. Question your stories. The pattern reveals that observing PM career paths taught that advancement comes from inversions that feel unambitious—where low-impact work can accelerate, strategy should wait, documents matter, and self-labels limit.

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Give explicit permission to think** - Don't assume it
2. **Build for best users first** - Not worst-case scenarios
3. **Add escape hatches to process** - For high performers

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Process brings high performers to average** - Variance reduction hurts the best
2. **Build for best users** - Counter to inclusive design
3. **Break the org for exceptional people** - Against consistent rules

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Eeke's antimemes share a structure: **organizational excellence requires accepting uncomfortable inequities—process variance reduction brings high performers down to average rather than helping everyone, permission to think must be explicitly granted rather than assumed through empowerment, building for best users rather than worst-case scenarios contradicts inclusive design, and breaking the organization for exceptional people conflicts with fairness norms**. Shield top performers from process. Give explicit permission. Build for the best. Break rules for exceptions. The pattern reveals that Retool and Stripe's product excellence came from deliberately unequal treatment—where the best people, best users, and exceptional situations get different rules than the standard, against the fairness instinct that says everyone should be treated the same.

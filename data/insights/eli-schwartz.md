## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Most SaaS Companies Shouldn't Invest in SEO

**INSIGHT:** "Most SaaS companies shouldn't invest in SEO. When sales teams drive deals and purchasing requires committee approval, organic search delivers minimal ROI compared to paid or brand channels."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Identity

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Every company needs SEO.
- **Identity Antimeme:** SEO experts recommend SEO investment.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Organic traffic is always valuable.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- SEO industry depends on universal applicability
- It's coming from an SEO expert (paradoxical)
- "Don't invest" is anti-growth messaging
- Most SEO advice assumes investment

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that SEO is universally valuable
- The belief that organic traffic equals business value
- The comfort of "we should be doing SEO"

**Verification:**
> "Most SaaS companies shouldn't invest in SEO."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Stop measuring traffic, measure conversions** - Zero business impact is possible
2. **Move SEO ownership to product** - It's a product question
3. **Assess whether you should invest at all** - Many SaaS shouldn't

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Most SaaS companies shouldn't invest in SEO** - From an SEO expert
2. **SEO is a product question, not marketing** - Ownership shift
3. **Traffic metrics mislead** - Dashboard vanity

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Eli's antimemes share a structure: **SEO effectiveness requires rejecting SEO industry orthodoxy—most SaaS companies shouldn't invest in SEO at all, traffic metrics mislead rather than indicate progress, product managers should own SEO decisions rather than marketers, and content built for ranking signals fails despite following "best practices."** Question whether to invest. Ignore traffic. Give it to product. Serve intent, not algorithms. The pattern reveals that an SEO expert's most valuable advice is often to not do SEO, to not measure what SEO practitioners measure, and to not follow what SEO guides recommend—because the industry's universal advice applies only to specific conditions most companies don't meet.

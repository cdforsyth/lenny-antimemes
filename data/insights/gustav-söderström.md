2. **Fault-tolerant UI design** - Weak algorithms need escape hatches
3. **Magic trick principle** - Great products feel magical through iteration

### Rare/Antimemetic Insights (1-5% would notice)

2. **Discovery is the real hard problem** - 1-in-10 hit rates for new genres vs. 9-in-10 within

4. **Amazon's model works because of hard APIs** - Mandated structure, not despite competition
5. **AI music won't replace artists** - Becomes instrument like DAWs; originality gets harder

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Don't redesign what works** - Spotify's homepage solved recall
2. **Build different UIs for different hit rates** - Discovery needs 5x options
3. **Actually create to understand creators** - Research isn't enough

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Discovery hit rates are fundamentally lower** - 1-in-10 vs. 9-in-10
2. **Decentralization requires mandated hard constraints** - Amazon's APIs
3. **AI makes originality harder, not easier** - Generic floods in

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Gustav's antimemes share a structure: **product leadership at scale requires accepting counterintuitive constraints—Spotify's homepage solved the hard problem so redesigning risks destruction, discovery hit rates are fundamentally lower than recommendation so UI paradigms must differ, creator empathy requires actually creating not just researching, and Amazon's decentralization works because of mandated hard APIs not despite constraints**. Don't fix what works. Build for different hit rates. Create to understand. Mandate structure for autonomy. The pattern reveals that 14 years at Spotify taught that scaling requires constraints that seem to contradict agile principles—where preservation beats iteration, different problems need different UIs, experience beats research, and mandates enable decentralization.

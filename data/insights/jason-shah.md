## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Reframe Pushback as Collaborative

**INSIGHT:** "Rather than 'pushback,' Jason advocates: 'How do I help the business actually succeed when I disagree?'" The framing shift changes the dynamic from confrontation to collaboration.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Disagreement is confrontational.
- **Social Antimeme:** "Pushback" is the common term.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** The reframe requires practice.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Pushback feels like the honest term
- Collaboration framing seems like spin
- The specific language matters
- Confrontation patterns are habitual

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that disagreement is confrontational
- The belief that pushback is honest
- The comfort of direct opposition

**Verification:**
> "How do I help the business actually succeed when I disagree?"

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Reframe pushback as "help succeed when I disagree"** - Changes dynamic
2. **Eliminate "great" and vague descriptors** - Forces clarity
3. **Write actual customer quotes** - Authentic perspective test

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Adjust roles to candidate strengths** - Not rigid job descriptions
2. **Collaborative framing for disagreement** - Not confrontational
3. **5-10 year rounds in 30-50 year career** - Deep commitment with flexibility

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Jason's antimemes share a structure: **the rigid, confrontational, and vague patterns in product work (job descriptions, pushback, marketing language) can be replaced with flexible, collaborative, and concrete alternatives that produce better outcomes**. Reframe pushback as collaboration. Adapt roles to candidates. Force concrete language. The pattern reveals that product leadership effectiveness comes from deliberate language and process choices that counteract default rigidity.

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Demonstrate Competence Instead of Defending Reputation

**INSIGHT:** "Rather than explaining away mistakes, she advocates: 'What is one thing that I can do, small, that will demonstrate the opposite of what I'm afraid this person thinks of me?'" Action over explanation.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Identity / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Identity Antimeme:** My reputation needs defending.
- **Social Antimeme:** Explanations show accountability.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** People need to understand what happened.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Defending feels proactive
- Explanation seems like taking responsibility
- We focus on perception management
- Action without explanation feels incomplete

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that reputation is defended with words
- The belief that explanation = accountability
- The comfort of managing perceptions through communication

**Verification:**
> "What is one thing that I can do, small, that will demonstrate the opposite of what I'm afraid this person thinks of me?"

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Demonstrate, don't defend** - One small action beats explanation
2. **Start with 5-10 minutes** - Low threshold breaks spirals
3. **Design immediate emotional rewards** - Delayed gratification doesn't build habits

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Action over explanation** - Reputation is built through demonstration
2. **Mental models over decisions** - Transfer the thinking, not the choices
3. **Immediate reward builds habits** - Not delayed gratification

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Hilary's antimemes share a structure: **professional resilience and habit formation work opposite to how we're taught—action beats explanation, tiny starts beat waiting for motivation, understanding how leaders think beats memorizing their decisions, and immediate emotional reward beats delayed gratification**. Demonstrate don't defend. Start in 5 minutes. Get the reward now. The pattern reveals that effectiveness principles often invert conventional wisdom about discipline, accountability, and learning—where the direct, immediate, small approach outperforms the careful, delayed, substantial one.

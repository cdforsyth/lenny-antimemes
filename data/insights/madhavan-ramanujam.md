## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Pricing Paralysis Is Internal, Not External

**INSIGHT:** "Your reluctance to do a price increase is often internal and emotional and it's not external and logical. Most founders blame market resistance when resistance is psychological self-sabotage."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Psychological / Identity

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Psychological Antimeme:** Market sets limits.
- **Identity Antimeme:** I'm being rational.
- **System Antimeme:** Customers resist price increases.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- External blame is comfortable
- Rationality is assumed
- Customer resistance seems real
- The internal cause is uncomfortable

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that market sets limits
- The belief that resistance is external
- The comfort of blaming customers

**Verification:**
> "If you're holding hands to do a price increase, you have a terrible business."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Check if pricing paralysis is internal** - Often emotional self-sabotage
2. **Select non-churners at acquisition** - Not reactive retention
3. **Charge for POCs** - Filters tire-kickers

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Pricing resistance is internal** - Not market-driven
2. **Stop churn by selecting non-churners** - Flips retention playbook
3. **AI taps labor budgets (10X larger)** - SaaS pricing fails

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Madhavan's antimemes share a structure: **pricing strategy requires accepting uncomfortable truths about internal psychology and market structure—pricing paralysis is internal emotional self-sabotage not external market resistance, stopping churn requires selecting non-churners at acquisition not reactive retention, AI products tap labor budgets 10X larger than software budgets making SaaS pricing wrong, and charging for POCs filters tire-kickers rather than creating friction**. Check your psychology. Select loyal customers. Price for labor budgets. Charge for POCs. The pattern reveals that pricing expertise taught that monetization failures come from internal not external sources—where psychology blocks pricing, selection beats retention, AI needs new models, and charging beats free.

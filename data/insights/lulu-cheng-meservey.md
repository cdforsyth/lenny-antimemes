## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Shape Message to Fit Worldview, Not Change It

**INSIGHT:** "Connect your message to what audiences already care about rather than trying to change their worldview. 'It's a light lift to take the thing you want to talk about and shape it to fit into their worldview.'"

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We want to change minds.
- **Social Antimeme:** "Thought leadership" implies new thinking.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Persuasion is assumed to require change.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Communications often aims to convince
- Shaping to fit feels like compromise
- "Change their mind" is the goal assumed
- The adaptation framing is counterintuitive

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that communication changes worldviews
- The belief that new ideas need to convince
- The comfort of originality over adaptation

**Verification:**
> "It's a light lift to take the thing you want to talk about and shape it to fit into their worldview."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Shape to worldview, don't change it** - Light lift adaptation
2. **Build independent channels early** - Defense before crisis
3. **Narrow audience for pressure** - Force ÷ Area

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Commission over omission** - Visible mistakes beat invisible ones
2. **People over institutions for trust** - Human voice wins
3. **Adaptation over persuasion** - Fit into existing worldview

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Lulu's antimemes share a structure: **effective communications works with existing structures (worldviews, trust in people, narrow audiences) rather than against them (changing minds, corporate voice, broad reach)**. Commission over omission captures the meta-pattern: action with visible risk beats inaction with invisible cost. The pattern reveals that communications effectiveness comes from leveraging existing dynamics, not fighting them.

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Models Have Gotten So Good That Generalists Are No Longer Needed

**INSIGHT:** "The models have gotten so good that the generalists are no longer needed. What they really need is experts." AI training now requires domain expertise, not general labeling.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**

- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Industry

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Data labeling is low-skill work.
- **Industry Antimeme:** Scale Turk-style workforces for AI training.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** More data beats better data.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- The old data labeling model is well-known
- Expert recruitment seems expensive
- Scale.ai model dominates mental models
- The shift happened quickly

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that data labeling is commoditized
- The belief that crowd-sourced labeling scales
- The comfort of the established industry structure

**Verification:**
> "The models have gotten so good that the generalists are no longer needed. What they really need is experts."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **CEO leads new venture directly** - 80%+ focus, not delegation
2. **Separate all operations** - Independent teams for everything
3. **Acknowledge the chaos explicitly** - Transparent difficulty, demanding pace

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Expert-only AI training** - Generalists no longer needed
2. **CEO operational involvement** - Not oversight, hands-on leadership
3. **Separation over integration** - Complete independence beats shared services

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Garrett's antimemes share a structure: **launching a transformative new business within an established company requires concentration of resources and attention that feels inefficient—CEO focus that seems excessive, operational separation that seems duplicative, and explicit acknowledgment of chaos that seems unprofessional**. CEO drives it. Separate everything. Admit it's chaos. The pattern reveals that the "efficient" approaches to internal ventures (delegation, shared services, stability messaging) actually slow execution, and breakthrough speed requires uncomfortable concentration.

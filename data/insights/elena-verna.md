## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Rebrands Never Produce Good Performance Results

**INSIGHT:** "Never ever once have I seen a rebrand or redesign...produce good performance results." Expect initial performance drops requiring 6+ months of optimization post-launch. Rebrands feel strategic but hurt metrics.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**

- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Rebrands are prestigious projects.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** "Refreshing the brand" feels strategic.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** New looks should perform better.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Rebrands are career-making projects
- Agencies profit from rebrand work
- The performance drop is attributed to other factors
- "Never" is hard to accept for popular initiatives

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that rebrands are growth initiatives
- The belief that fresh design improves performance
- The comfort of high-visibility strategic projects

**Verification:**
> "Never ever once have I seen a rebrand or redesign...produce good performance results."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Plan new growth engines every 18 months** - Don't just optimize current channels
2. **Don't over-experiment** - Use pre/post when testing conditions aren't met
3. **Consult experts, don't reinvent** - Your problem isn't unique

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Rebrands hurt performance** - Never produce good results
2. **Over-experimentation is a problem** - Inverts data-driven culture
3. **Career optionality over titles** - Different success metric

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Elena's antimemes share a structure: **the practices that signal growth sophistication (rebrands, A/B testing everything, unique solutions, title progression) often harm growth or limit career options**. Rebrands are prestigious but hurt metrics. Testing everything sounds rigorous but wastes resources. Solving unique problems feels innovative but ignores available patterns. The pattern reveals that growth expertise often means knowing what not to do - and the "not to do" list includes many popular practices.

# Antimeme Analysis: Nan Yu on Lenny's Podcast

**Source:** Lenny's Podcast - Nan Yu Interview (Head of Product at Linear)
**Analyzed:** 2026-01-20

## Stage 1: Popular vs. Rare Concepts

### Popular Takeaways (80-90% of listeners would mention)

1. **Quality matters** - Don't compromise on polish
2. **Speed is important** - Ship fast and iterate
3. **Customer focus** - Listen to user feedback
4. **Simplicity wins** - Avoid feature bloat
5. **PM-Sales collaboration** - Work across functions
6. **Deadlines require commitment** - Treat them seriously

### Rare/Antimemetic Insights (1-5% would notice)

1. **Speed signals mastery not sloppiness** - Fast + high quality
2. **Extreme testing reveals hidden solutions** - Build extremes to find middle
3. **Feature requests hide misaligned incentives** - Refuse entire categories
4. **B2B software teaches workflows** - Not just solving problems
5. **PM is a go-to-market discipline** - Sit on sales calls
6. **Deadlines only work if treated as P0** - Everything else stops

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Speed Signals Mastery, Not Sloppiness

**INSIGHT:** "You can basically tell how good the output is by how fast they're going. Exceptional performers work faster with higher quality. Competence enables both velocity and excellence."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Speed means carelessness.
- **System Antimeme:** Quality requires time.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Slow down for better work.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Speed and quality seem opposed
- Taking time is valued
- Slowness implies care
- The mastery signal is missed

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that speed hurts quality
- The belief that time ensures quality
- The comfort of taking it slow

**Verification:**
> The most overlooked insight: exceptional performers work faster with higher quality. Nan notes that "you can basically tell how good the output is by how fast they're going." Most teams conflate speed with carelessness, missing that competence enables both velocity and excellence.

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Use speed as quality signal** - Fast people produce better work
2. **Test extremes to find solutions** - Not reasonable options
3. **Refuse feature categories** - Requests hide incentive problems

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Speed signals mastery** - Not sloppiness
2. **Feature requests hide misaligned incentives** - Refuse entire categories
3. **Deadlines require P0 treatment** - Everything else stops

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Nan's antimemes share a structure: **building exceptional products requires accepting counterintuitive truths about speed, options, and requests—speed signals mastery not sloppiness because competence enables both velocity and excellence, extreme testing reveals hidden solutions by expanding beyond reasonable options, feature requests hide misaligned incentives between managers and ICs, and deadlines only work if treated as P0 with everything else stopping**. Trust speed. Test extremes. Refuse categories. Commit fully. The pattern reveals that building Linear taught that product excellence comes from inversions that feel wrong—where fast means good, extremes reveal answers, requests are refused, and commitment means stopping everything else.

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Rebuilt Entire Onboarding After Launch Success

**INSIGHT:** "His team rebuilt their entire onboarding after initial launch success plateaued, demonstrating willingness to 'bet the company' on fundamentals."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Success means you're doing it right.
- **System Antimeme:** Build on what's working.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Optimize, don't rebuild.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Launch success feels like validation
- "Bet the company" on fundamentals sounds risky
- Rebuilding working features seems wasteful
- Plateaus are expected after launch

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that initial success validates approach
- The belief that you should build on working foundations
- The comfort of not touching what's working

**Verification:**
> "Rebuilt their entire onboarding after initial launch success plateaued."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Spread influencer budget across 40+ creators** - Not concentrated
2. **Personally onboard creators as founder** - Authenticity premium
3. **Be willing to rebuild after success** - Bet on fundamentals

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Rebuild working features** - Success doesn't validate approach
2. **Wrapper framing is wrong** - Orchestration is the value
3. **Founders should onboard personally** - Time investment worth it

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Grant's antimemes share a structure: **AI-native product success requires doing things that seem to not scale—rebuilding working onboarding despite launch success, personally onboarding creators as a founder, spreading budget across 40+ creators monthly rather than concentrating, and building deep workflow orchestration rather than accepting "wrapper" limitations**. Rebuild what works. Do creator work personally. Spread wide. Build beyond the wrapper. The pattern reveals that Gamma's growth came from explicitly choosing approaches that seem inefficient or risky—where the willingness to not optimize, to not scale creator programs, and to not concentrate bets created advantages that optimization would have destroyed.

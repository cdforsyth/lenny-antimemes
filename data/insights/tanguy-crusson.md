**INSIGHT:** "Frame incubations with a 70% failure rate expectation rather than optimism. Things are going to fail, let's not drag the rest of the company into it. This creates necessary scarcity, keeps the broader organization from interfering, and provides psychological safety for the team."

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Organizational Antimeme:** Communicate optimism about new initiatives.
- **Social Antimeme:** Confidence attracts support.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Pitch success to get resources.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Optimism is expected
- Confidence is valued

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Frame incubations with 70% failure rate** - Creates scarcity and safety
2. **Violate processes to protect innovation** - Distance prevents interference
3. **Ignore competition, review customer interviews** - Icebergs hide research

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Position new bets as likely to fail** - Pessimism protects initiatives
2. **Break rules to protect innovation** - Process violation enables zero-to-one
3. **Build detached from platform initially** - Extract before integrating

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Tanguy's antimemes share a structure: **corporate innovation requires accepting counterintuitive truths about framing, rules, and competition—position new bets as likely to fail because 70% failure expectation creates scarcity and safety, break rules to protect innovation because process violation enables autonomy, build detached from your platform initially because extraction enables radical experimentation, and ignore competition because you only see shipped features not the research behind them**. Frame for failure. Break rules. Extract first. Ignore competitors. The pattern reveals that building Jira Product Discovery taught that corporate innovation success comes from inversions that feel disloyal—where pessimism protects, rules are violated, platforms are extracted from, and competition is ignored.

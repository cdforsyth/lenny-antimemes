## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Product Ops as a Sign of Inefficiency (Paradoxically Valuable)

**INSIGHT:** "Most assume operations roles indicate problems. However, product ops can be proactive optimization—concentrating PMs on strategic work rather than just firefighting."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Ops roles mean problems.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Operations is overhead.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Minimize non-building roles.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Ops seems like overhead
- Problems prompt ops hires
- Non-building roles are minimized
- The proactive value is hidden

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that ops means problems
- The belief that operations is overhead
- The comfort of lean structures

**Verification:**
> Most assume operations roles indicate problems. However, Melissa notes that "do you want them concentrating on strategic work majority of the time" through product ops infrastructure shows ops can be proactive optimization, not just firefighting.

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Treat product ops as proactive optimization** - Not problem indicator
2. **Include customer insights in ops** - The overlooked pillar
3. **Require product ops at scale** - Non-negotiable for CPOs

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Product ops as paradoxically valuable** - Inefficiency enables efficiency
2. **PMs self-select out when trained** - Understanding causes departure
3. **Customer insights as ops work** - The squishy middle is overlooked

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Melissa and Denise's antimemes share a structure: **product operations requires accepting counterintuitive truths about efficiency, scope, and retention—product ops as inefficiency is paradoxically valuable because it enables strategic focus, the squishy middle research pillar is overlooked because people don't see customer insights as ops, PMs self-select out when they understand the role because attraction stems from misconceptions, and CPOs should never join product ops-less companies because it's non-negotiable at scale**. Embrace ops overhead. Include research in ops. Expect self-selection. Require ops infrastructure. The pattern reveals that product operations expertise taught that ops effectiveness comes from accepting what seems inefficient—where overhead enables focus, research is ops, training causes departure, and infrastructure is non-negotiable.

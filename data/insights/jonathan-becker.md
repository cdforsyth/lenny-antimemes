## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Disclosed Vulnerability, Got Hired Instead of Sued

**INSIGHT:** Becker was exploiting Uber's referral program loophole through paid search. He disclosed this vulnerability to Garrett Camp, expecting consequences. "Rather than legal action, they hired Thrive to solve the problem, launching a 10-year partnership."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Don't admit to exploiting systems.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Disclosure leads to punishment.
- **System Antimeme:** Companies protect against exploiters.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Self-incrimination seems foolish
- Legal risk is real
- The outcome was unusual
- Most wouldn't try it

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that disclosure leads to punishment
- The belief that companies punish exploiters
- The comfort of hiding discovered vulnerabilities

**Verification:**
> "Rather than legal action, they hired Thrive to solve the problem."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Hire for composure under uncertainty** - Emotional quality matters
2. **Budget heavily for TikTok creative** - Not just another channel
3. **Disclose vulnerabilities you find** - Might become opportunity

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Disclosure became 10-year partnership** - Opposite of expected
2. **AI created more strategic jobs** - Not fewer people
3. **Composure as hiring criterion** - Emotional over technical

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Jonathan's antimemes share a structure: **performance marketing success often comes from counterintuitive moves—disclosing exploits rather than hiding them, recognizing that AI changes work type rather than eliminating jobs, hiring for emotional composure rather than just technical skills, and investing heavily in platforms that seem effortless**. Disclose the vulnerability. AI changes work, doesn't eliminate it. Hire for composure. TikTok requires production. The pattern reveals that marketing effectiveness requires seeing through surface appearances—where the risky-seeming disclosure creates relationships, the scary AI creates strategic roles, and the casual-seeming platform requires heavy investment.

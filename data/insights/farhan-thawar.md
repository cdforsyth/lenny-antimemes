## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Interviews Poorly Predict Job Performance

**INSIGHT:** "Interviews poorly predict job performance. Instead, Shopify emphasizes job trials: Real work product over 30-90 days beats interview performance."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Organizational

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Interviews are the hiring mechanism.
- **Organizational Antimeme:** Better interviews = better hires.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Interview performance indicates job performance.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Entire hiring industry built on interviews
- Job trials are operationally complex
- The statement is direct but rarely acted on
- Interview optimization is a huge industry

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that interviews predict performance
- The belief that interview skill matters
- The comfort of scalable hiring processes

**Verification:**
> "Interviews poorly predict job performance."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Create a Delete Code Club** - Institutionalize removal
2. **Accept pair programming productivity cost** - 20% for knowledge transfer
3. **Use job trials over interviews** - Real work over performance

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Interviews poorly predict performance** - Direct statement
2. **Deletion as regular practice** - Millions of lines yearly
3. **Infrastructure-first delays features** - Months of invisible work

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Farhan's antimemes share a structure: **engineering excellence at scale requires accepting visible losses for invisible gains—interviews don't predict performance so use trials instead, pair programming costs 20% but builds knowledge, millions of lines of code should be deleted yearly, and infrastructure work delays features by months but enables leverage**. Don't trust interviews. Accept pairing cost. Delete aggressively. Build platforms first. The pattern reveals that Shopify's engineering culture explicitly names and accepts the tradeoffs that most organizations avoid discussing—where the uncomfortable explicit costs (trial periods, pairing overhead, deleted code, delayed features) create the capabilities that differentiate the organization.

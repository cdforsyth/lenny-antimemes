## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Sugar-Coated Broccoli Strategy

**INSIGHT:** "People don't want to eat the broccoli but people like sugar, so give them the sugar then hide the broccoli inside of it." Hide your vision inside something people actually want.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Identity / Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Identity Antimeme:** Founders should pursue their vision directly.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Great products sell themselves.
- **Social Antimeme:** Vision compromise is selling out.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Direct vision pursuit is celebrated
- Hiding feels like compromise
- The broccoli/sugar frame is unusual
- Indirect paths feel like failure

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that vision should be direct
- The belief that customers want your vision
- The comfort of pure vision pursuit

**Verification:**
> "People don't want to eat the broccoli... so give them the sugar then hide the broccoli inside of it."

- **System Antimeme:** Sales accelerates growth.
- **Social Antimeme:** Hire sales when you have product.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** $10M without sales seems impossible.

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Hide the vision in what people want** - Sugar-coated broccoli
2. **Delay sales hiring as long as possible** - Product-market fit constraint
3. **Be willing to completely reset** - Wrong foundations aren't fixable

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **$10M ARR without a salesperson** - Extreme product-led success
2. **First PM at 50 employees** - Role skepticism at scale
3. **Sugar-coated broccoli** - Indirect vision delivery

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Ivan's antimemes share a structure: **Notion's success came from extreme constraints and indirect approaches that look like mistakes—no sales until $10M, no PM until 50, complete rewrites, hiding vision inside productivity features**. The conventional playbook (hire sales, hire PM, iterate don't rewrite, pursue vision directly) was systematically inverted. The pattern reveals that product-led growth at its purest form requires constraints so severe they seem irresponsible—and that great products sometimes require hiding what you actually want to build inside what people actually want to use.

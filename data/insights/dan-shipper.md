## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. AI May Reshore American Jobs

**INSIGHT:** AI makes expensive services (lawyers, call centers, in-house counsel) affordable for small companies and individuals, while allowing fewer American workers to serve more people at lower cost than offshore alternatives.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Frontier

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 3
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** The dominant narrative ("AI takes jobs") is so loud that the inverse ("AI creates jobs here") can't be heard.
- **System Antimeme:** Media incentives favor fear-based headlines. "AI may bring jobs back to America" doesn't generate clicks.
- **Social Antimeme:** Expressing optimism about AI and jobs marks you as naive or corporate-captured.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "Jobs lost to AI" is the established frame
- The mechanism (cheap intelligence → affordable services → new demand) requires multi-step reasoning
- The offshore labor narrative has decades of momentum
- People who benefit from this (American workers) don't have platforms to amplify it

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The comfortable pessimism that requires no action
- The moral clarity of "AI is bad for workers"
- The assumption that technology always moves jobs away from expensive labor

**Verification:**
> "There are a lot of expensive services that rich people and big companies are paid for right now, so in-house counsel or call center or whatever. And what cheap intelligence does is it makes those kinds of things affordable for small companies and individuals. So it stimulates demand... it becomes much more cost-effective for American companies to hire people in the US."

### 2. The Winnicott Leash: AGI as Profitable Indefinite Autonomy

**INSIGHT:** AGI can be defined as when it becomes economically profitable to run agents indefinitely - never turning them off, never waiting for you to give them the next task. Like a teenager who doesn't need constant supervision.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** AGI debates focus on intelligence benchmarks, not economic utility. The profitable-to-run-forever frame is pragmatic rather than philosophical.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** This definition is testable and actionable, which makes it uncomfortable for people who prefer abstract debates.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- AGI debates are dominated by philosophers and AI safety researchers, not business operators
- "Profitability" feels like the wrong frame for something as momentous as AGI
- The child development analogy seems too casual for such a serious topic
- People prefer definitions that can't be tested (so they can argue indefinitely)

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The mystique around AGI
- The ability to argue about it forever without resolution
- The separation between "AI capabilities" and "AI economics"

**Verification:**
> "I think a good definition of AGI is when does it become economically profitable for people to run agents indefinitely? So it just never turns off. It's a Claude Code that's always running, it's always doing something, you just never turn it off, and you don't need to because you know that it's worthwhile to keep it on."

### 3. Compounding Engineering: Each Unit Makes the Next Easier

**INSIGHT:** For every unit of work, you should make the next unit of work easier to do. In an AI coding world, this means building prompts and tools that reduce the work needed for similar tasks in the future.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 4
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Implementation Antimeme:** The immediate pressure to ship conflicts with investing in tools that pay off later.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Each individual instance of "just do it the same way" seems reasonable. The compounding cost is invisible.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Shipping features feels more productive than building meta-tools
- The payoff is delayed and distributed across future work
- It requires discipline to stop and build infrastructure when you could be building product
- Traditional engineering culture values "getting it done" not "making it easier next time"

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The satisfaction of shipping immediately
- The simplicity of just doing the work
- The excuse that "we don't have time for tooling"

**Verification:**
> "For every unit of work, you should make the next unit of work easier to do. So an example is, in a Claude Code world, where you're not coding a lot, you end up spending a lot of time essentially typing PRDs... Or you could spend a little bit of time being like... There's a sort of platonic ideal of a PRD. And what I'm going to do is write a prompt that can take my rambling thoughts and then turn that into a PRD."

### 4. The 10/80/10 Adoption Curve

**INSIGHT:** In any organization, roughly 10% are early adopters who will figure out AI themselves, 10% will never touch it, and 80% will adopt it only if you show them exactly how to use it for their specific job.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 4
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Most AI training is generic ("here's what ChatGPT can do") not job-specific ("here's the exact prompt for your workflow").
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** The early adopters are visible and vocal, making it seem like "everyone gets it" when really it's 10%.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- AI enthusiasts (the 10%) dominate the conversation
- Generic training is easier to scale than customized training
- Acknowledging that 80% need hand-holding feels patronizing
- The 10% who never will are written off, not understood

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that "good tools sell themselves"
- The efficiency of one-size-fits-all training
- The belief that resistance is irrational (maybe it's just unserved)

**Verification:**
> "One of the interesting things about AI is it's such a general purpose technology, and I think people who work inside companies, 10% of them are like, I'm super curious about this. 10% are like, I will never touch this. And 80% are like, if you tell me how to do it for my job, I'll do it."

### 5. Entry-Level People Accelerate Faster With AI

**INSIGHT:** Contrary to the "AI replaces entry-level jobs" narrative, young people with AI tools are making years of progress in months because they can encode feedback into prompts and never make the same mistake twice.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Psychological

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** The "AI takes entry-level jobs" narrative serves multiple interests: journalists get clicks, senior people feel secure, policy makers have something to regulate.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We anchor on the tasks being automated, not the meta-skill of learning faster.
- **Psychological Antimeme:** Senior people may unconsciously prefer a narrative that doesn't threaten their advantage.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Bad news about jobs is more compelling than good news
- The acceleration is happening to people without platforms (young workers)
- "AI helps juniors catch up to seniors faster" threatens established hierarchies
- The mechanism (feedback → prompt → permanent improvement) requires understanding AI workflows

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- Comfortable pessimism about the next generation
- The assumption that experience compounds linearly
- The narrative that AI primarily threatens the powerless

**Verification:**
> "I hate the headlines that are like, 'Entry-level jobs are taken away by AI.' Whenever I see a kid with ChatGPT, I'm like, 'Holy shit, they're going to go so much faster than any other person that I've worked with.' We have this guy, he made a year's worth of progress in two months because every time I sat down with him... he recorded all of it, put it into a prompt, and he never made the same mistake twice."

### 6. Context Engineering Is At Least Half the Performance

**INSIGHT:** Getting the right context to the model at the right time is at least as important as model capability. This is solvable engineering, not waiting for better models.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Model capabilities are exciting and measurable. Context engineering is boring and fuzzy.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Context engineering requires domain-specific work. Model improvements are free.
- **System Antimeme:** AI labs market model capabilities, not "here's how to give it better context."

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Model benchmarks dominate AI discourse
- "Wait for the next model" is easier than engineering context
- Context engineering is unsexy infrastructure work
- The phrase itself is relatively new (Tobi from Shopify coined it)

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The comfortable waiting for better models
- The simplicity of "just use ChatGPT"
- The assumption that AI progress = model progress

**Verification:**
> "There's this real push now. Tobi from Spotify coined this term called 'context engineering,' which is getting the context to the model, the right context at the right time, is at least half the performance. And I think that's 100% true."

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Psychological

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Psychological Antimeme:** Admitting you don't love what you're doing threatens your identity and your investment.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** The tax is distributed over every day. No single day is bad enough to trigger change.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Sunk cost fallacy makes change feel wasteful

**Verification:**

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Compounding engineering** - Invest in tools that make future work easier
2. **The 10/80/10 curve** - Customize AI training to specific jobs, not generic capabilities
3. **Context engineering** - Focus on getting right context to models, not just waiting for better models

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **AI may reshore American jobs** - Inverts the dominant jobs narrative
2. **Entry-level acceleration** - Young people with AI tools are learning faster, not being replaced
3. **Software is becoming content** - New product forms emerging that don't require engineers

### Pattern Across Antimemes

These antimemes share a structure: **the dominant narrative serves someone's interest while the contrarian truth is actionable**. "AI takes jobs" serves media and policy makers but isn't actionable. "AI accelerates juniors" is actionable for hiring managers. "Wait for better models" serves AI labs' marketing but "context engineer now" is actionable for builders. The antimemetic quality comes from the contrarian truth requiring more work or challenging established interests.

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Most Companies Do Product Management Theater

**INSIGHT:** "Most companies perform 'product management theater'—they have the artifacts, processes, and titles of product management without the substance. They go through discovery motions without actually discovering anything."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 3
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Companies believe they're doing real product work.
- **System Antimeme:** The theater is indistinguishable from the real thing.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Having processes feels like doing the work.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- No one thinks they're doing theater
- The artifacts look identical
- Admitting theater means admitting failure
- The distinction requires deep pattern recognition

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that processes equal outcomes
- The belief that having PMs means having product management
- The comfort of following established practices

**Verification:**
> "Most companies do product management theater—process without substance."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Speed up discovery to hours** - Slow discovery is fake discovery
2. **Invest in coaching over strategy** - Build capability, not plans
3. **Assess competence before empowering** - Both required

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Most companies do product theater** - Process without substance
2. **Feature teams can't transform incrementally** - Wholesale change required
3. **Empowerment without competence is dangerous** - Not just empowerment

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Marty's antimemes share a structure: **the visible artifacts of good product work (processes, empowerment, thorough discovery, strategic leadership) can exist without the substance, and fixing the gap requires discontinuous change rather than improvement**. Product theater is indistinguishable from real product work by artifacts alone. Feature teams can't become empowered teams gradually. The pattern reveals that product excellence requires honest assessment of whether substance exists beneath the artifacts—and wholesale change when it doesn't.

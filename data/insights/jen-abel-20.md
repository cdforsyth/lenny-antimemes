## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. The Mid-Market Doesn't Actually Exist

**INSIGHT:** "It's either 'low-end enterprise' or 'upper-end SMB.' Treating them as hybrid creates fatal confusion. The sales dynamics are completely different—you must choose one game."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Mid-market is a valid segment.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Size defines segments.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Build for mid-market.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Mid-market is taught
- Size-based segmentation is standard
- Hybrid approach is common
- The confusion is hidden

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that mid-market exists
- The belief that size defines segments
- The comfort of hybrid positioning

**Verification:**
> Jen dismisses the concept entirely, arguing it's either "low-end enterprise" or "upper-end SMB." Treating them as hybrid creates fatal confusion. The sales dynamics are completely different—you must choose one game.

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Choose enterprise or SMB** - Mid-market doesn't exist
2. **Target Fortune 500 first** - They're desperate early adopters
3. **Start at $75-150K minimums** - Pricing mistakes are irreversible

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Mid-market doesn't exist** - Fatal confusion from hybrid
2. **Tier-one logos first** - Pull you to right product
3. **Salespeople don't need sales experience** - Vision-casting magic

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Jen's antimemes share a structure: **enterprise sales success requires accepting counterintuitive truths about segments, targets, pricing, and hiring—the mid-market doesn't exist because treating it as hybrid creates fatal confusion, target tier-one logos first because they're desperate early adopters, pricing mistakes are irreversible because $10K caps you at $100K, and salespeople don't need sales experience because vision-casting magic beats trained skills**. Choose your game. Go big first. Price high. Hire non-salespeople. The pattern reveals that enterprise sales expertise taught that success comes from inversions that feel risky—where picking a lane beats hybrid, big beats small, high beats low, and inexperience beats experience.

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Role prompts improve accuracy.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Expert personas help performance.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Always assign expert roles.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Role prompts are popular
- Expert personas feel helpful
- Assignment is standard

**What's lost by accepting it:**

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Skip role prompts for accuracy tasks** - Only use for writing
2. **Ignore threat/reward prompting** - No measurable benefit
3. **Accept prompt injection is unsolvable** - Design around it

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Role prompting doesn't work for accuracy** - Research disproves common belief
2. **Prompt injection is fundamentally unsolvable** - Can't patch a brain
3. **AI security cannot be solved by products** - Only labs can fix

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Sander's antimemes share a structure: **effective AI usage requires accepting counterintuitive truths about prompting and security—role prompting doesn't work for accuracy tasks because research shows it only helps writing, threat/reward prompting is ineffective despite viral popularity, prompt injection is fundamentally unsolvable because you can't patch a brain, and AI security cannot be solved by products because only model architecture innovations help**. Skip roles for accuracy. Ignore threats/rewards. Accept injection limits. Distrust security products. The pattern reveals that researching prompt engineering taught that AI effectiveness comes from inversions that feel wrong—where popular techniques fail, urgency doesn't help, security is unsolvable, and products provide false comfort.

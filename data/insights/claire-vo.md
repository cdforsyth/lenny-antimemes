## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Hired to Remind Them They Can Operate Like a Startup

**INSIGHT:** Late-stage companies hire experienced leaders expecting to learn "how to operate like a big company," but the actual value is reminding them they can still operate with startup speed and scrappiness.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 4
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Companies assume growth requires adding process. The counterintuitive truth (that you can simplify) doesn't match the pattern.
- **Social Antimeme:** Consultants and advisors often sell "professionalization" because it's what companies expect to buy.
- **System Antimeme:** The entire professional services industry is built on adding complexity, not removing it.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Growing companies assume they need to "grow up"
- Adding process feels like progress
- Simplification doesn't look impressive on a slide deck
- The people brought in to "professionalize" are incentivized to add, not subtract

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The comforting narrative that growth requires bureaucracy
- The excuse that "we're too big to move fast now"
- The assumption that startup speed is a phase, not a choice

**Verification:**
> "People often think that I get hired into later stage companies because I'm supposed to teach them how to operate like a big company, and in fact, I say I'm hired to remind them they can operate like a startup."

### 2. Don't Let Pace Lock to Meeting Cadence

**INSIGHT:** Organizations naturally snap their decision-making pace to their meeting calendar. "We'll discuss it in the next meeting" is an artificial timeline imposed by Google Calendar, not by the actual needs of the business.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 5
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Meetings are the default coordination mechanism. Challenging them requires active effort every time.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** The pattern (wait for the meeting) is so ingrained it feels like the natural way to work.
- **Social Antimeme:** Pushing for faster decisions can feel aggressive or disruptive to meeting culture.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Meetings are the default unit of organizational time
- "Let's discuss it in the next sync" is socially comfortable
- Breaking the pattern requires constant vigilance
- No one explicitly defends meeting-locked pace, but everyone defaults to it

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The comfort of deferring decisions
- The social ease of "we'll revisit this"
- The illusion that meetings are productive coordination

**Verification:**
> "I often find that pace of organization locks to pace of the calendar, and so I am really thoughtful that recurring meetings do not drive next steps. It's a very tactical thing, but when somebody says, 'Oh, we'll discuss this or we'll decide this in the next meeting,' it's, 'No, we should discuss this now. We should decide this tomorrow.'"

### 3. Draw the Org Chart With Your Name on Top

**INSIGHT:** When you see a gap in the organization, don't wait for someone to offer you the role. Draw out the solution with yourself in it and present it to your boss as a solved problem.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 4
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Psychological

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Psychological Antimeme:** Proposing yourself for a role feels presumptuous. We're trained to wait to be offered things.
- **Social Antimeme:** Self-promotion is uncomfortable. "Drawing the org chart with your name on top" sounds arrogant.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We think of careers as things that happen to us, not things we design.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Cultural norms about modesty and waiting your turn
- Fear of appearing arrogant or overconfident
- Uncertainty about whether you're "ready"
- The passive default of hoping to be noticed

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The comfort of not putting yourself forward
- The safety of not being rejected
- The narrative that good work will be recognized automatically

**Verification:**
> "I sat for about a half a day and I thought, I think I can help here, drew out an org chart, put my name on the top, walked into my boss's office and said, 'This is one potential solve of your marketing organization question...' And I got that job."

### 4. The Universe Is Bendable to Your Will

**INSIGHT:** Organizations are not fixed structures. They are fluid entities that reorganize around talented, motivated individuals. The org chart is a suggestion, not a constraint.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Psychological / Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We see org charts as fixed reality. The idea that they're fluid requires a paradigm shift.
- **Psychological Antimeme:** Believing you can reshape organizations requires a level of agency most people don't feel entitled to.
- **System Antimeme:** HR processes and career frameworks reinforce the idea of fixed ladders and lanes.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Org charts look permanent and official
- Most people experience organizations as constraints, not clay
- The people who can bend organizations don't need to tell others
- Learned helplessness about career paths is widespread

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The excuse that "that's just how things are"
- The safety of operating within defined boundaries
- The comfort of external constraints limiting your responsibility

**Verification:**
> "The universe is bendable to your will... In most, at least in the stage I operate in in startups and growth stage companies and late stage startups, organizations are very fluid and I like to organize around talented motivated individuals."

### 5. Sales-Led Product Is Underrated

**INSIGHT:** The product community's dismissal of sales-led companies is misguided. SAP is a powerhouse. Great products can be built with commercial orientation. Sales-led ≠ bad product.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Product Twitter / tech discourse celebrates PLG and dismisses sales-led. Defending SAP is contrarian.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Product purists have a religious belief that sales-led = compromised product.
- **System Antimeme:** The thought leaders, conferences, and content all reinforce the PLG superiority narrative.

### 6. Lowercase c vs Uppercase C Communication

**INSIGHT:** AI will replace lowercase c communication (the functional trading of information) but not uppercase C Communication (influence, persuasion, getting humans to follow you down a path).

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 4
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** "Communication" is one word covering two very different things. The distinction isn't obvious until articulated.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Knowing the distinction doesn't automatically help you invest in the right skills.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- We don't naturally distinguish between types of communication
- The AI discourse focuses on "will it replace communication?" not "which kind?"
- Uppercase C communication is harder to teach and talk about
- PMs often identify with lowercase c communication (docs, updates, specs)

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that communication skills are uniformly at risk
- The comfort of improving at lowercase c (easy to measure)
- The excuse to not develop uppercase C (harder to improve)

**Verification:**
> "Communication, lowercase c, I feel like is one of the places that's going to be replaced... Now, but capital C communication of are you influential? Are you convincing? Are you bold? Can you get this system of humans to follow you down a path? That's, I think, going to be much harder to replace."

### 7. Move One Click Faster

**INSIGHT:** Your natural pace will always be slower than optimal. Systematically compress every timeline by one iteration: year → half, half → quarter, quarter → month, week → today.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 5
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Implementation Antimeme:** The rule is simple but requires constant application. Each moment feels like the exception.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Our natural timeline estimates feel accurate. Systematic compression feels aggressive.
- **Psychological Antimeme:** Faster deadlines create immediate discomfort. The payoff is distributed and delayed.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Every timeline feels reasonable when you set it
- Aggressive compression feels stressful in the moment
- There's no natural forcing function to apply this rule
- People optimize for comfort, not speed

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The comfort of buffer time
- The illusion that your original estimate was correct
- The excuse that "this is just how long things take"

**Verification:**
> "I communicate to my leaders that my expectation is they bring in the clock speed one click faster. If you think something needs to be done this year, it needs to be done this half. If you think it needs to be done this half, it needs to be done this quarter. This quarter, this month. This week, today."

### 8. Content Creation as Documentation, Not Creative Generation

**INSIGHT:** The most sustainable content creation isn't trying to be creative - it's documenting what you already think about at work. Interesting meetings and interactions become content naturally.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 4
- Implication: 3
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Psychological / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Psychological Antimeme:** "Content creation" sounds like a separate, creative endeavor. Reframing it as documentation feels too simple.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Even knowing this, people don't build the capture habit.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "Creator" identity implies creating something new
- Documentation sounds less impressive than generation
- The habit of capturing daily work doesn't form automatically
- People separate "work" from "content" in their minds

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The mystique around content creation
- The excuse that "I'm not creative enough"
- The belief that you need special insights to share

**Verification:**
> "I think thinking about content creation as documentation, not creative generation is really helpful. I just like to talk about what I think about at work and I like to, an interesting meeting or interesting interaction, document why I thought that was interesting or what could be done better."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Move one click faster** - Systematically compress every timeline by one iteration
2. **Draw the org chart with your name on top** - Propose solutions, don't wait for offers
3. **Don't let pace lock to meeting cadence** - Challenge "we'll discuss in the next meeting"

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **The universe is bendable to your will** - Organizations are fluid, not fixed
2. **Hired to remind them they can operate like a startup** - Growth doesn't require bureaucracy
3. **Lowercase c vs uppercase C** - AI replaces information trading, not influence

### Pattern Across Antimemes

These antimemes share a structure: **the default feels natural but is actually chosen**. Meeting cadence is a choice. Timeline estimates are a choice. Org structures are a choice. Being passive about your career is a choice. The antimemetic quality comes from the defaults being invisible - we don't see them as choices until someone like Claire points them out. Once seen, they can be changed.

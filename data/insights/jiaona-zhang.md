## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. "Really Editing" Is the PM Job

**INSIGHT:** "Understand here are the opportunities, and then you're kind of pulling together all the different possibilities and you're really editing." The PM's core function is editing, not generating.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Identity / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Identity Antimeme:** PMs create vision.
- **System Antimeme:** Product leaders generate ideas.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Great PMs have great ideas.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Vision is the celebrated PM skill
- Generating ideas feels creative
- Editing seems subtractive
- The identity is generation, not curation

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that PMs generate vision
- The belief that great PMs have great ideas
- The comfort of creative identity

**Verification:**
> "You're really editing."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Reframe PM job as editing** - Not generating
2. **Assess competitive context** - Quality is relative
3. **Redirect solution attachment to problems** - Problem discovery first

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **"Really editing" is the PM job** - Not vision creation
2. **Quality tolerance is contextual** - Not absolute
3. **Team welfare over career** - During turbulence

### Pattern Across Antimemes

JZ's antimemes share a structure: **product leadership excellence requires accepting the less glamorous realities of the role—the PM job is really editing not generating despite vision being celebrated, quality standards depend on competitive context not absolute polish, new PMs commonly attach to solutions prematurely when they should focus on problems, and team welfare should come before career advancement during turbulence**. Edit, don't generate. Assess context. Focus on problems. Prioritize people. The pattern reveals that CPO-level product leadership is built on the humble, contextual, people-focused work that contradicts the visionary, standards-driven, career-building PM identity that's typically promoted.

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Product-Led Growth Fails With Bundles

**INSIGHT:** Product-led growth companies struggle with bundled offerings - customers hesitate evaluating multiple products simultaneously. Single-product velocity typically outperforms multi-product bundles for self-service adoption. The bundle strategy that works for enterprise sales actively hurts PLG.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Bundles are seen as value-add.
- **System Antimeme:** Revenue strategy often pushes bundling.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** More products = more value seems logical.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Enterprise playbooks emphasize bundles
- Cross-sell is a common growth strategy
- "Suite" products feel comprehensive
- The evaluation friction is invisible in revenue reports

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that bundles increase conversion
- The belief that more products means more value
- The comfort of enterprise sales patterns for PLG

**Verification:**
> "Product-led growth companies struggle with bundled offerings—customers hesitate evaluating multiple products simultaneously."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Assess time-to-value** - Determines PLG vs. sales-led decision
2. **Volunteer in downturns** - Expansion when others contract
3. **Consistency over volume** - Sustained messaging beats campaigns

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **PLG fails with bundles** - Counter to enterprise bundling wisdom
2. **Downturns are opportunity** - Invert the protection instinct
3. **Consistency beats spending** - Sustained presence over impression volume

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Carilu's antimemes share a structure: **the intuitive response to business situations (bundle for value, spend for awareness, protect in downturns) often produces the opposite of desired outcomes**. Bundles slow PLG. Spending volume doesn't compound. Protection mode misses opportunity. The pattern reveals that hypergrowth marketing requires inverting common instincts - the obvious moves are often wrong because they're optimized for different contexts (enterprise sales, campaign-based marketing, scarcity mentality).

## Stage 1: Popular vs. Rare Concepts

### Popular Takeaways (80-90% of listeners would mention)

1. **Product-market fit matters** - Find what resonates

### Rare/Antimemetic Insights (1-5% would notice)

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Look for friction not enthusiasm** - Specific questions beat vague positives
2. **Do it yourself before hiring** - Execute functions before delegating
3. **Experiment freely in marketing** - Zero maintenance cost

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Embrace imposter syndrome** - Don't fight it, proceed anyway
2. **Vague positive feedback signals failure** - Enthusiasm masks misalignment
3. **Marketing requires opposite thinking from product** - Different cost structures

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Raaz's antimemes share a structure: **startup marketing and product success requires accepting counterintuitive truths about confidence, feedback, and expertise—embracing imposter syndrome rather than fighting it enables action without false confidence, vague positive feedback signals failure because friction indicates genuine interest, marketing requires opposite thinking from product because failed experiments vanish without debt, and founders must execute functions before hiring specialists because you can't hire what you don't understand**. Embrace imposter feelings. Seek friction not enthusiasm. Experiment freely. Do it yourself first. The pattern reveals that building Wiz's marketing taught that startup success comes from inversions that feel wrong—where imposter is embraced, positivity is questioned, experimentation beats caution, and founders do before delegating.

- **System Antimeme:** Retention is the north star.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Important metrics should be goals.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Teams should own outcomes.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Retention is universally important

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Don't goal on retention** - Use leading indicators
2. **Track edge case failures** - "Never Delivered" signals churn
3. **Recruit from adjacent functions** - Diverse backgrounds help

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Retention is terrible to goal on** - Important ≠ good goal
2. **Composite metrics become meaningless** - Simpler is better
3. **Edge cases signal churn** - Not statistical noise

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Jessica's antimemes share a structure: **data-driven excellence requires abandoning the sophisticated metrics that seem more rigorous—retention is terrible to goal on despite being the north star, composite metrics with six factors become meaningless compared to three simple ones, edge case failures like "Never Delivered" signal churn risk despite being rare, and recruiting from adjacent functions works better than career specialists**. Don't goal on retention. Simplify composite metrics. Track edge failures. Hire adjacent. The pattern reveals that DoorDash's analytics excellence comes from counterintuitive simplification—where leading indicators, separate simple metrics, rare failure tracking, and cross-functional hiring outperform the sophisticated retention goals, weighted composites, average optimization, and specialist hiring that data teams instinctively build.

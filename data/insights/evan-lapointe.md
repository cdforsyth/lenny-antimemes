## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Ask "What experience am I?"** - More important than ability
2. **Design habitat, not training** - Environment over individuals
3. **Establish shared beliefs** - Not mission statements

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Experience quality matters most** - Over ability and trust
2. **Mission-vision-values are performative** - Don't create culture
3. **People think fundamentally differently** - Wiring, not just preference

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Evan's antimemes share a structure: **organizational effectiveness requires accepting uncomfortable truths about humans—the experience quality you provide matters more than your competence despite competence being what we develop, mission-vision-values statements are performative rather than culture-building despite every company having them, people think fundamentally differently due to wiring rather than trainable preferences, and habitat design shapes behavior rather than individual development despite coaching industries**. Ask what experience you are. Skip the values statement. Accept different wiring. Design the environment. The pattern reveals that professional and organizational improvement requires working with human nature rather than against it—where experience quality, shared beliefs, neurological differences, and environmental design matter more than the competence, values statements, alignment, and individual development that organizations focus on.

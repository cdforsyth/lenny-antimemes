## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Model Accuracy Isn't Product Quality

**INSIGHT:** "High model accuracy doesn't equal product quality. A 95% accurate model can create a terrible user experience if the 5% failures happen at critical moments. Product quality requires understanding failure modes, not just improving metrics."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Accuracy is the standard ML metric.
- **System Antimeme:** ML teams optimize for benchmarks.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Higher accuracy sounds better.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- ML training optimizes accuracy
- Benchmarks are how models are compared
- Product quality is harder to measure
- The failure mode analysis is additional work

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that better metrics mean better products
- The belief that ML optimization equals product optimization
- The comfort of single-metric success

**Verification:**
> "Model accuracy isn't product quality—different metrics matter."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Say no more than other PMs** - Manage AI expectations downward
2. **Evaluate failure modes, not just accuracy** - Product quality ≠ model metrics
3. **Pursue boring applications** - Clear value beats exciting demos

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Model accuracy ≠ product quality** - Different optimization targets
2. **Explanations confuse users** - Transparency can backfire
3. **Boring AI wins** - Unsexy applications succeed

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Marily's antimemes share a structure: **the AI product paradigm inverts expectations—accuracy isn't quality, transparency confuses, and boring beats exciting**. The hype around AI creates expectations that don't match how successful AI products actually work. Model metrics don't translate to user experience. Explanations don't build trust. Exciting demos don't become products. The pattern reveals that AI PM excellence requires actively resisting the AI narrative rather than embracing it.

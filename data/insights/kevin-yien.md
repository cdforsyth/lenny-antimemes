## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. The Unsell Email - Losing 30% Intentionally

**INSIGHT:** "Kevin's 'unsell email' strategy involves candidly presenting job challenges at offer stage. The method intentionally loses 30% of candidates but secures better culture fits and reduces post-hire regret."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Closing candidates is the goal.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Losing 30% seems like failure.
- **System Antimeme:** Recruiting metrics focus on acceptance.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Recruiting optimizes for closing
- 30% loss looks like bad conversion
- "Unselling" contradicts hiring instincts
- The quality benefit is delayed

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that closing is always good
- The belief that selling is the right approach
- The comfort of high acceptance rates

**Verification:**
> "The method intentionally loses 30% of candidates but secures better culture fits and reduces post-hire regret."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Unsell email at offer stage** - Intentionally lose 30%
2. **Automate research via Gong triggers** - Remove friction
3. **Track decisions to calibrate judgment** - Build product sense

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Lose candidates intentionally** - Quality over closing
2. **Week on single animation** - Deep execution involvement
3. **Write for customers, not stakeholders** - Audience shift

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Kevin's antimemes share a structure: **the conventional PM behaviors (closing candidates, manual research, strategic altitude, internal writing) produce worse outcomes than counterintuitive alternatives (unselling, automation, execution depth, customer writing)**. The unsell email is the clearest: intentionally losing 30% of candidates improves outcomes. The pattern reveals that PM effectiveness often comes from inverting the obvious approach - losing to win, automating the personal, going deep instead of staying high.

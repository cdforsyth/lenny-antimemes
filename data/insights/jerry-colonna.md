## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Ask "How have I been complicit?"** - Agency, not blame
2. **Examine childhood patterns in leadership** - Not just skills
3. **Notice busyness as avoidance** - Worth doubts drive productivity

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Busyness quiets worth doubts** - Achievement as avoidance
2. **Team dysfunction is childhood patterns** - Not talent or strategy
3. **Growth mindset can become rigid** - Meta-framework trap

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Jerry's antimemes share a structure: **the sources of leadership dysfunction (childhood patterns, worth doubts, self-delusion) are hidden beneath the professional surface, and the common interventions (skills training, strategy work, growth mindset) don't address the actual causes—which are psychological and require self-examination to surface**. Busyness hides worth doubts. Teams fail from childhood patterns. Complicity reveals agency. The pattern reveals that leadership improvement requires going beneath the professional layer to psychological foundations—and that the business/personal divide that seems healthy actually prevents the real work from happening.

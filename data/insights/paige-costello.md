**INSIGHT:** "Self-selection preventing career advancement" is a common PM challenge—people opt out of opportunities before being rejected, limiting their growth without realizing it.

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / Identity

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** I know my own readiness.
- **Identity Antimeme:** Not applying is being realistic.
- **Social Antimeme:** Don't overreach.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Self-assessment feels accurate
- Opting out feels responsible
- The counterfactual isn't visible
- "Not ready" seems humble

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that self-assessment is accurate
- The belief that not applying is humility
- The comfort of avoiding rejection

**Verification:**
> "Self-selection preventing career advancement."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Ask "How might the opposite be true?"** - Counter advocacy with inquiry
2. **Notice self-selection** - You're opting out before being rejected
3. **Distinguish performative from genuine collaboration** - Process isn't engagement

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Advocacy limits inquiry** - PM strengths become weaknesses
2. **Self-selection is invisible** - You don't see opportunities you don't pursue
3. **Skill-building over role-chasing** - Different career framework

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Paige's antimemes share a structure: **the behaviors that make PMs effective (conviction, advocacy, self-assessment, process adherence) contain their own failure modes that are invisible from the inside—overconfidence limits learning, self-selection limits opportunity, performative collaboration mimics the real thing**. Ask how you might be wrong. Notice when you're opting out. Check if collaboration is genuine. The pattern reveals that PM excellence requires constantly countering the instincts that got you there—the same skills that work become the behaviors that limit.

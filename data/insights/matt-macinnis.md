## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Deliberate Understaffing as Strategy

**INSIGHT:** "Maintain a principle of 'deliberately understaffing every project.' Overstaffing creates politics and work on lower-priority items before higher ones are validated—what he calls 'poison' that 'slows you down.'"

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Organizational

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** More resources accelerate projects.
- **Organizational Antimeme:** Request headcount when stalled.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Staff up to move faster.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- More resources seems helpful
- Headcount requests are standard
- Staffing up is natural
- The poison effect is hidden

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that resources accelerate
- The belief that staffing helps
- The comfort of requesting headcount

**Verification:**
> MacInnis rejects conventional resource allocation. Rather than requesting more people when projects stall, he maintains a principle of "deliberately understaffing every project." Overstaffing creates politics and work on lower-priority items before higher ones are validated—what he calls "poison" that "slows you down."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Deliberately understaff projects** - Overstaffing is poison
2. **Apply process strategically** - It kills alpha when uniform
3. **Reframe silence as selfish** - Withholding feedback hurts others

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Deliberate understaffing** - More resources slow you down
2. **Learning from success not failure** - Failure romanticism serves VCs
3. **Withholding feedback is selfish** - Silence is moral failure

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Matt's antimemes share a structure: **high-performance leadership requires accepting uncomfortable inversions about resources, learning, and feedback—deliberate understaffing is strategy because overstaffing is poison, learning from success beats learning from failure because failure romanticization serves VCs not founders, the alpha-beta framework shows process kills creativity when applied uniformly, and withholding feedback is selfish because silence optimizes your comfort at others' expense**. Understaff deliberately. Study success. Apply process strategically. Give feedback directly. The pattern reveals that leading product at Rippling taught that high performance comes from inversions that feel harsh—where less staff beats more, success teaches better than failure, process must be strategic, and silence is selfishness.

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. The Manager Death Spiral

**INSIGHT:** "New managers often keep the most interesting projects for themselves, delegating less valuable work to team members. This blocks organizational learning and prevents scaling." The death spiral: you keep the good work, team doesn't develop, you can't scale, you keep more work.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Keeping good work feels like good management.
- **Social Antimeme:** Visible individual contribution proves value.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Letting go of interesting work is hard.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "I should do the hard stuff" feels responsible
- Delegation seems like passing off work
- The spiral develops slowly
- Self-assessment of this pattern is difficult

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that managers should do the hardest work
- The belief that delegation is for boring tasks
- The comfort of staying hands-on

**Verification:**
> "New managers often keep the most interesting projects for themselves, delegating less valuable work to team members."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Avoid the manager death spiral** - Delegate interesting work, not boring work
2. **Practice generalization explicitly** - Extract principles after execution
3. **Demonstrate impact, not execution** - Frame business outcomes

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Manager death spiral** - Keeping good work kills scaling
2. **Portfolio careers with anchor** - Alternative to single-company ladder
3. **Scaling work as distinct category** - Problems created by success

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Fareed's antimemes share a structure: **the natural instincts of high performers (keeping interesting work, moving to next project, heads-down execution, climbing one ladder) prevent scaling to leadership**. The death spiral catches managers who can't let go. Missing generalization traps experience in specific contexts. Execution without impact communication doesn't compound. The pattern reveals that PM leadership requires inverting IC instincts - what made you successful as an individual prevents success as a leader.

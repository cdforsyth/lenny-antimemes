## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Ex-Growth Companies Are Dangerous

**INSIGHT:** "Companies that raised substantial capital but lost product-market fit present serious risks. He warns employees their equity may be worthless despite significant compensation." High valuation without PMF means equity is likely worthless—the compensation is illusory.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** High valuation signals success.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Compensation packages seem valuable.
- **System Antimeme:** Funding rounds are celebrated.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Valuation is the visible metric
- Equity in offer letters looks substantial
- Companies don't advertise lost PMF
- The danger signs aren't obvious

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that funded companies are safe
- The belief that equity packages are valuable
- The comfort of joining "successful" startups

**Verification:**
> "Companies that raised substantial capital but lost product-market fit present serious risks."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Examine discarded feedback** - Dismissed input reveals shadows
2. **Assess company PMF, not valuation** - Ex-growth companies are traps
3. **Share decisions as sidecar** - Not solo driver

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Catching the rabbit stops you running** - Achievement creates emptiness
2. **Shadows of superpowers** - Strengths create blindspots
3. **Ex-growth = danger** - High valuation without PMF is worthless

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Nikhyl's antimemes share a structure: **the things that seem positive (high valuation, goal achievement, strong feedback, superpowers, managerial authority) have hidden downsides that require deliberate examination**. Ex-growth companies look successful but are traps. Catching goals creates emptiness. Superpowers create shadows. The pattern reveals that career excellence requires interrogating the things that seem obviously good—the danger is in what you're not looking at.

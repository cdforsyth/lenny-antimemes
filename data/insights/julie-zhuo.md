## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Eliminated Product Manager Positions

**INSIGHT:** "Julie eliminated product manager positions at her startup Sundial, forcing engineers to take ownership of product thinking. This constraint drives better outcomes than delegating to specialists."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 3
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** PMs are standard practice.
- **System Antimeme:** Org charts assume PM roles.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Specialization is assumed to improve outcomes.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- PM is an established profession
- "Eliminating roles" sounds like cost-cutting
- The constraint mechanism isn't intuitive
- Engineers as product thinkers challenges identity

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that PM specialization improves products
- The belief that roles should be separated
- The comfort of standard org structures

**Verification:**
> "Julie eliminated product manager positions at her startup Sundial, forcing engineers to take ownership of product thinking."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Ask: "Help me get better"** - Establish psychological safety upfront
2. **Force product ownership on engineers** - Remove PM roles as constraint
3. **Check for conviction before assigning** - Belief required, not optional

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Eliminate PMs** - Specialization may hurt, not help
2. **Hypergrowth without data infrastructure** - Standard advice doesn't match reality
3. **Conviction required, not optional** - Compliance alone fails

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Julie's antimemes share a structure: **the standard practices that seem professional (separate PM roles, formal reviews, data infrastructure, professional execution without conviction) often produce worse outcomes than counterintuitive alternatives (constraint through role elimination, vulnerability-first feedback, conviction matching)**. The PM elimination is the clearest: removing the role forced better outcomes. The pattern reveals that management effectiveness often comes from removing barriers to ownership and belief, not adding structure and process.

## Stage 1: Popular vs. Rare Concepts

### Popular Takeaways (80-90% of listeners would mention)

1. **Three growth engines** - Paid acquisition, virality/referrals, SEO
2. **Focus over diversification** - Early-stage should concentrate on one channel
3. **Conversion benchmarks** - 20-35% landing to signup, 5-7%+ free to paid
4. **Onboarding drives 2-4X activation** - Early-stage opportunity
5. **Market contraction creates opportunity** - Disciplined companies win
6. **Attribution infrastructure matters** - Foundation for scaling

### Rare/Antimemetic Insights (1-5% would notice)

1. **"Only thing worse than not working is not giving it a shot"** - Premature channel abandonment
2. **Later-stage onboarding lifts only 20-30%** - Diminishing returns
3. **SEO requires unique angle, programmatic opportunity, or proprietary data** - Specific viability test
4. **Virality works only with inherent network effects** - Not all products qualify
5. **High LTV enables paid acquisition** - "Hundreds of dollars" threshold
6. **Channel fit isn't universal** - Match engine to product characteristics

**INSIGHT:** "The only thing worse than a tactic not working is not giving it the appropriate shot." Teams abandon channels before really testing them, declaring failure prematurely.

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** If it's not working, try something else.
- **System Antimeme:** Iterate quickly through options.
- **Social Antimeme:** Pivoting shows adaptability.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Quick iteration is celebrated
- Failure feels like signal
- Moving on feels like progress
- "Appropriate shot" is undefined

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that early results are predictive
- The belief that iteration speed is good
- The comfort of pivoting

**Verification:**
> "The only thing worse than a tactic not working is not giving it the appropriate shot."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Give channels a real shot** - Premature abandonment wastes opportunity
2. **Check SEO viability conditions** - Unique angle, programmatic, or proprietary data
3. **Stage-appropriate expectations** - 2-4X early vs 20-30% later

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Channel-product fit constrains options** - Not all channels work for all products
2. **Onboarding leverage diminishes with scale** - Early ≠ late
3. **Premature abandonment is common** - "Didn't work" often means "didn't try"

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Yuriy's antimemes share a structure: **growth tactics that seem universally applicable (SEO, onboarding, multi-channel) have specific preconditions that determine whether they can work—and teams waste resources either on channels that don't fit their product or abandoning channels before really testing them**. Check channel-product fit first. Stage matters for expected lift. Give tactics a real shot. The pattern reveals that growth strategy is mostly about constraint identification—knowing what won't work for your specific product and stage—rather than execution of universal best practices.

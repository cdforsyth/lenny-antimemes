## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. If You Meet Spec, You're Done - Perfectionism is Hiding

**INSIGHT:** Quality means meeting spec. If you meet spec, you're done. If the spec isn't good enough, make a better spec. But refusing to ship something that meets spec because you're a perfectionist - that's hiding.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 4
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Psychological

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Psychological Antimeme:** Perfectionism feels like high standards. Reframing it as hiding threatens identity.
- **Social Antimeme:** "I'm a perfectionist" is socially acceptable, even admirable. "I'm hiding" is not.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** The mechanism (refusing to ship = avoiding judgment) isn't visible to the perfectionist.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Perfectionism is culturally rewarded
- The ship/don't-ship decision feels like quality control, not fear
- "Make a better spec" is the move nobody makes
- The word "hiding" is confrontational

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The identity of being a perfectionist
- The comfort of delayed judgment
- The excuse that it's not ready yet

**Verification:**
> "What it doesn't mean is that you take something that meets spec and refuse to ship it because you're a perfectionist. That's hiding."

### 2. You've Sacrificed Your Agency Over the Four Most Important Choices

**INSIGHT:** The four choices (customers, competition, validation, distribution) are choices, not givens. Most product people gloss over them, assuming they're fixed. By treating them as given, you've sacrificed your agency over the most important decisions.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** These choices feel like constraints, not choices. The framing is invisible.
- **Organizational Antimeme:** In most companies, these decisions are made upstream. PMs inherit them.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Changing these choices requires political capital most people don't have.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "Our customers are X" feels like a fact, not a choice
- Distribution channels feel determined by the market
- Validation sources (boss, metrics, customers) feel fixed
- The language of "sacrifice your agency" is confrontational

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The comfort of inherited constraints
- The excuse that you're working within fixed boundaries
- The safety of not having to justify these choices

**Verification:**
> "For a product person, these are the critical choices and you probably have glossed over them. You have probably assumed it's a given and you've sacrificed your agency over the four most important things you should be choosing."

### 3. One Difficult Conversation with Your Boss

**INSIGHT:** If you're trying to please your boss's taste, have one difficult conversation: "Can we agree that I'm not trying to match your taste, I'm trying to match the taste of these 400 people we've agreed are our customers?" Every other meeting will go better.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 4
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Having a "difficult conversation" with your boss feels risky. Avoiding it feels prudent.
- **Organizational Antimeme:** Most organizations reward matching the boss's taste, not challenging it.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** The conversation itself is hard to have. The words feel confrontational.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- The short-term pain of the conversation outweighs the abstract future benefit
- "Every other meeting will go better" is hard to believe until you've done it
- The power dynamic makes this feel dangerous
- Most people don't have 400 people defined as their smallest viable audience

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The safety of implicit agreement
- The ability to blame the boss for taste mismatch
- The avoidance of one difficult moment

**Verification:**
> "Can we agree that I'm not trying to match your taste, I'm trying to match the taste of these 400 people that we've agreed are our customers? Every other meeting is going to go way better from now on."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Perfectionism is hiding** - If it meets spec, ship it. If spec isn't good enough, change the spec.
2. **One difficult conversation** - Establish whose taste you're matching once, make every meeting better.
3. **Buzz does not sell hamburgers** - Measure customer traction, not attention.

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **You've sacrificed your agency** - The four choices are choices, not givens
2. **Systems invent culture** - Your fears are system-imposed, not internal
3. **Kindness as brand** - Personality traits differentiate AI more than benchmarks

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Seth's antimemes share a structure: **the visible, celebrated thing (perfectionism, buzz, bold rebrands, divisive products) masks the invisible, valuable thing (shipping, customer traction, brand equity, empathy)**. People optimize for the visible because it's measurable and gets praise, but the invisible is what actually creates value. The antimemetic quality comes from industry incentives - agencies sell rebrands, social managers sell buzz, designers sell controversy, and perfectionism protects egos. Seth's consistent move is to name what's actually being traded away (agency, cookies sold, awareness assets, market share) to make the invisible visible.

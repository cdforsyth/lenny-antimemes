## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Giving Away Legos Feels Like Grief

**INSIGHT:** "The feeling of giving away your legos—the responsibilities you've built—isn't about competence or ego. It's actual grief for something you're losing." The emotional response to delegation is loss, not weakness.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / Identity

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Delegation resistance is ego or control issues.
- **Identity Antimeme:** Good leaders delegate easily.
- **Social Antimeme:** Struggling with delegation is weakness.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Grief framing seems dramatic for work
- Leaders are supposed to delegate happily
- The loss isn't acknowledged
- Control issues is the standard explanation

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that delegation resistance is a character flaw
- The belief that good leaders don't struggle with letting go
- The comfort of simple explanations for complex emotions

**Verification:**
> "Giving away your legos feels like grief—you built something and now you're giving it away."

### 3. J-Curve vs Stairs Career Paths

**INSIGHT:** "Some careers follow stairs—predictable promotions, steady climb. Others follow a J-curve—go down or sideways before shooting up. J-curve paths often reach higher peaks but require tolerance for ambiguity and apparent backward movement."

- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Identity

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Career progress should be visible and linear.
- **Identity Antimeme:** Taking a step back means failing.
- **System Antimeme:** Promotions are the measure of success.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Sideways moves feel like failure

**Verification:**

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Use "Bob the Monster"** - Personify change emotions to process them
2. **Apply Waterline Model** - Only escalate ship-sinking decisions
3. **Name the grief in delegation** - Acknowledge the loss, not just the opportunity

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **J-Curve careers outperform Stairs** - Sideways and down can lead to higher peaks
2. **Delegation resistance is grief** - Not ego, actual loss
3. **Chaos tolerance is a disposition** - Not trainable, must hire for it

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Molly's antimemes share a structure: **the emotional and dispositional realities of scaling (grief in delegation, chaos tolerance as fixed trait, J-curve career paths) are systematically underacknowledged because they conflict with professional norms of rationality and universal development**. Delegation feels like loss because it is loss. Chaos tolerance can't be trained because it's a disposition. Career progress isn't always linear because the best paths often aren't. The pattern reveals that scaling wisdom requires accepting uncomfortable truths about human nature that professional culture prefers to deny.

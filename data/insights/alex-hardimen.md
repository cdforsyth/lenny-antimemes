## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Editorial Judgment as Algorithm Training Data

**INSIGHT:** "Train algorithms on 'editorial importance scores' created by journalists themselves—inverting the typical tech approach where editorial expertise is an afterthought."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Algorithms optimize for engagement.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Editorial judgment is subjective.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Let data decide importance.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Engagement metrics are standard
- Editorial judgment seems unmeasurable
- Data-driven decisions are valued
- The inversion is counterintuitive

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that algorithms know best
- The belief that engagement equals quality
- The comfort of pure data optimization

**Verification:**
> They train algorithms on "editorial importance scores" created by journalists themselves. This inverts the typical tech approach—editorial expertise becomes the optimization target, not an afterthought.

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Alex's antimemes share a structure: **media product leadership requires inverting tech industry assumptions—editorial judgment should train algorithms rather than engagement metrics, wartime product management should be a core competency rather than avoided, transparency about limitations builds trust rather than eroding it, and full-stack ownership eliminates blindspots that platforms can't see**. Use editorial judgment as data. Seek crisis. Explain limitations publicly. Own the whole stack. The pattern reveals that building product at the New York Times taught that media success comes from inversions that feel counterintuitive to tech—where editorial beats engagement, chaos beats stability, transparency beats silence, and ownership beats specialization.

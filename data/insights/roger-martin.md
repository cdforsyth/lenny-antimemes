# Antimeme Analysis: Roger Martin on Lenny's Podcast

**Source:** Lenny's Podcast - Roger Martin Interview (Strategy Advisor, author of Playing to Win)
**Analyzed:** 2026-01-19

---

## Stage 1: Popular vs. Rare Concepts

### Popular Takeaways (80-90% of listeners would mention)

1. **Strategy Choice Cascade** - Five questions companies must answer
2. **Two paths to victory** - Differentiation or low-cost
3. **Playing to Win framework** - Where to play, how to win
4. **Capabilities must be hard to replicate** - Sustainable advantage
5. **Strategy = integrated set of choices** - Not a list
6. **Most painful gap approach** - Start there, improve incrementally

### Rare/Antimemetic Insights (1-5% would notice)

1. **"90 out of 100" strategies lack merit** - Even at major companies
2. **Companies attempting neither path fail** - Competitors "jerk you around"
3. **Resource-based view dominates academia despite limited application** - Theory-practice gap
4. **Great strategists practice relentlessly over decades** - Not born
5. **Business school education fails strategy** - Wrong frameworks taught
6. **Multifaceted capabilities create reluctance to enter** - Not just difficulty

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Companies Attempting Neither Path Eventually Fail

**INSIGHT:** "There are only two legitimate ways to win: be differentiated or be the low-cost provider. Companies attempting neither ultimately fail when competitors 'can jerk you around as much as they want.'"

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Good execution wins.
- **Social Antimeme:** We're doing both somewhat.
- **System Antimeme:** There are many paths to success.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Binary feels reductive
- "Both" seems safer
- The "jerk you around" consequence is distant
- Middle ground seems smart

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that middle ground is safe
- The belief that execution compensates for position
- The comfort of not choosing

**Verification:**
> "Companies attempting neither ultimately fail when competitors 'can jerk you around as much as they want.'"

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Choose differentiation or low-cost** - No middle ground
2. **Build capabilities that create reluctance** - Psychological, not just practical
3. **Practice strategy relentlessly** - Skill, not talent

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Neither path = failure** - Middle ground is dangerous
2. **Academic strategy doesn't work** - Theory-practice gap
3. **Reluctance, not difficulty** - Psychological barrier

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Roger's antimemes share a structure: **strategic success requires making uncomfortable choices and building capabilities over decades that most companies avoid—the middle ground that feels safe is actually dangerous, the academic training that seems rigorous is actually impractical, and the barriers that seem physical are actually psychological**. Choose one path or lose. Practice for decades. Build reluctance, not just difficulty. The pattern reveals that strategy is fundamentally about commitment and skill development, not the analytical frameworks that dominate business education—and that the "safe" middle ground is where companies go to die slowly.

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Every Company Learns Something New About Customers

**INSIGHT:** "Every company we've worked with has learned something new about their customers" that drives measurable improvements. This is universal—not occasional.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / Identity

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We know our customers.
- **Identity Antimeme:** Customer understanding is our strength.
- **System Antimeme:** We've done customer research.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Companies believe they already understand customers
- "Something new" challenges existing knowledge
- Universal applicability seems like sales pitch
- Customer knowledge feels like competitive advantage

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that customer understanding is complete
- The belief that research confirms what we know
- The comfort of existing customer models

**Verification:**
> "Every company we've worked with has learned something new about their customers."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Map ideal experience before optimizing** - Define the gap
2. **Use jobs-to-be-done over personas** - Tasks over demographics
3. **Expect to learn something new** - Every company does

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **You don't know your customers** - Universal discovery happens
2. **Funnels center business, not customers** - The frame is wrong
3. **Gap identification over optimization** - Ideal vs. actual

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Gia's antimemes share a structure: **customer-led growth requires abandoning standard marketing frameworks—every company learns something new about customers despite believing they already understand them, traditional funnels center business needs rather than customer value, jobs-to-be-done replaces demographic personas, and identifying the gap between ideal and actual experience matters more than optimizing current states**. Expect discovery. Abandon funnels. Define jobs, not demographics. Find the gap. The pattern reveals that marketing effectiveness requires first admitting that standard frameworks (funnels, personas, optimization) are business-centric constructs that hide rather than reveal what customers actually need.

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Whatever You Avoid, You Invite

**INSIGHT:** "Whatever emotion that you're trying to avoid, you are inviting into your life in exactly the way that you're trying to avoid it." Conflict-avoidant leaders create tense organizations through their avoidance.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**

- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Psychological / Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Psychological Antimeme:** Avoidance protects from pain.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Not engaging prevents the outcome.
- **System Antimeme:** Managing emotions means controlling them.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Avoidance feels protective
- The inversion is counterintuitive
- We don't see our own avoidance patterns
- The mechanism isn't obvious

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that avoidance prevents outcomes
- The belief that not engaging protects
- The comfort of emotional avoidance strategies

**Verification:**
> "Whatever emotion that you're trying to avoid, you are inviting into your life in exactly the way that you're trying to avoid it."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Respond differently to your critical voice** - Experiment, don't silence
2. **Ask "How can I enjoy this 10% more?"** - Efficiency through enjoyment
3. **Understand problems fully before solving** - Solution becomes obvious

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Avoidance creates what you avoid** - The inversion
2. **Shoulds create stagnation** - Wants drive evolution
3. **Understanding IS solving** - No separate solution effort

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Joe's antimemes share a structure: **personal and organizational effectiveness require inversions that feel wrong—what you avoid you create, discipline-based "shoulds" slow progress while authentic wants accelerate it, deep problem understanding eliminates the need for separate solution-finding, and the critical voice needs relationship rather than silencing**. Face what you avoid. Follow wants, not shoulds. Understand until the solution is obvious. Befriend your critic. The pattern reveals that productivity and growth operate opposite to how we're taught—where the direct, disciplined, solution-focused approaches actually create the problems they're trying to solve.

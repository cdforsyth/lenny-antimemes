## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Target elite developers for AI tools** - They benefit most
2. **Force production focus in research** - "How do we ship?" from day one
3. **Remove friction, don't add features** - Invisibility is the goal

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Elite engineers love Copilot most** - Not juniors
2. **Developer happiness beats raises for retention** - Freed-up time
3. **Research labs need production constraints** - Ship focus works

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Inbal's antimemes share a structure: **building GitHub Copilot taught counterintuitive lessons about AI product development—elite engineers love Copilot most despite assumptions it helps struggling developers, research succeeds when forced to answer "how do we ship?" from day one despite freedom being the standard, developer happiness drives retention more than raises despite compensation focus, and the magic is removing friction not adding features despite feature-focused development**. Target elites. Constrain research. Enable happiness. Remove friction. The pattern reveals that GitHub's CPO learned AI product success inverts conventional positioning—where elites are the adopters, constraints enable research, happiness beats comp, and less features is more.

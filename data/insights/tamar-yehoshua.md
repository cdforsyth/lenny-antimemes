## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Well-Run Companies Don't Need to Win

**INSIGHT:** "Operational excellence doesn't correlate with business success. You can think of companies with high executive turnover, people fired, reorgs all the time—the numbers are amazing. Meanwhile well-run companies sometimes flat lined."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Narrative

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Well-run companies succeed.
- **Narrative Antimeme:** Operational excellence drives results.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Fix processes for success.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Well-run is equated with success
- Excellence is valued
- Processes are fixed
- The decoupling is uncomfortable

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that well-run means successful
- The belief that excellence drives results
- The comfort of process improvement

**Verification:**
> "You can think of companies...with high executive turnover...people fired...reorgs all the time...the numbers are amazing" versus well-run companies that "flat lined."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Follow exceptional people instead of planning** - Skills over roadmaps
2. **Design for tomorrow's users** - Not today's vocal minority
3. **Focus on strategic thinking** - Execution gets automated

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Well-run companies don't need to win** - Excellence doesn't correlate with success
2. **You shouldn't plan your career** - Creates anxiety without results
3. **AI won't replace PMs—it will eliminate bad ones** - Filters, not replaces

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Tamar's antimemes share a structure: **product leadership and career success requires accepting counterintuitive truths about operations, planning, and optimization—well-run companies don't need to win because operational excellence doesn't correlate with business success, you shouldn't plan your career because following exceptional people beats five-year plans, don't optimize for unhappy users because tomorrow's user base is larger than today's power users, and AI won't replace PMs because it will eliminate bad ones by automating execution**. Accept chaos can win. Follow people not plans. Design for tomorrow. Embrace AI filtering. The pattern reveals that leading product at Google, Amazon, and Slack taught that professional success comes from inversions that feel irresponsible—where chaos can win, planning hurts, power users mislead, and AI strengthens the best.

1. **OKRs are vitamins, not medicine** - Amplify strength but don't fix problems

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. OKRs Reveal Everything That's Wrong

**INSIGHT:** "OKRs are more of a vitamin, they're not a medicine... they just reveal everything that's wrong." OKRs don't fix organizational problems - they expose them. Lack of psychological safety, weak strategy, poor hiring, and middle management bottlenecks all become visible through OKR implementation failures. The framework is diagnostic, not therapeutic.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** OKRs are sold as solutions.
- **System Antimeme:** Companies buy frameworks expecting fixes.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** "Implementation failed" is easier than "this revealed our dysfunction."

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Framework sellers emphasize benefits
- "It didn't work" is easier than "it showed us our problems"
- Diagnosis without treatment feels incomplete
- Companies want cures, not mirrors

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that OKRs solve problems
- The belief that framework failure means wrong framework
- The comfort of implementing solutions

**Verification:**
> "OKRs are more of a vitamin, they're not a medicine... they just reveal everything that's wrong."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Business model before product sense** - Study fundamentals; intuition comes from experience
2. **Work in engineering/design first** - Build before managing builders
3. **Slow down to go faster** - Invest in foundations and understanding

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **OKRs reveal, don't fix** - Diagnostic, not therapeutic
2. **Product sense is output, not input** - Develops from study and experience
3. **Speed without understanding kills adoption** - Hasty implementation fails

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Christina's antimemes share a structure: **the apparently slower, less glamorous path produces better outcomes than the apparently faster, more prestigious one**. Business model study beats chasing product sense. Functional experience before PM beats direct-to-PM. Slowing down beats speeding up. Deep OKR understanding beats fast rollout. The pattern reveals that product and operational success often requires inverting status hierarchies - valuing the foundational over the flashy, the studied over the intuitive, the slow over the fast. The antimemetic quality comes from these inversions contradicting what visible success and career prestige suggest.

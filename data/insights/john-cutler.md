## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Dysfunctional Companies Are All the Same; Happy Ones Are Different

**INSIGHT:** "Dysfunctional companies are all the same and then the happy companies...can be very, very different." This inverts Tolstoy (and conventional wisdom): there's no single formula for success, but there IS a common pattern of failure.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 3
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Best practices assume one right way.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We want replicable success formulas.
- **Social Antimeme:** "What do top companies do?" is the question.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Frameworks sell better than "it depends"
- Case studies suggest replicable patterns
- The failure patterns are less interesting
- Diversity of success is harder to teach

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that there's one right way
- The belief that success is formulaic
- The comfort of best practices

**Verification:**
> "Dysfunctional companies are all the same and then the happy companies...can be very, very different."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Complete loops, don't accumulate** - Focus on full cycles
2. **Adapt for context** - Startup advice doesn't transfer wholesale
3. **Match skills to situation** - Context matters more than general ability

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Dysfunctional same, happy different** - Success isn't formulaic
2. **Complexity over prescription** - Illumination more valuable than simplification
3. **Silicon Valley blindness** - Enterprise contexts need different approaches

### Pattern Across Antimemes

John's antimemes share a structure: **the product advice ecosystem (prescriptive frameworks, universal best practices, transferable skills, knowledge accumulation) fails to serve practitioners because it oversimplifies complexity and assumes one context**. The Tolstoy inversion captures it: dysfunction is patterned, success is diverse. The pattern reveals that becoming better at product work means embracing context-sensitivity and loop completion over framework collection - the opposite of what the advice market supplies.

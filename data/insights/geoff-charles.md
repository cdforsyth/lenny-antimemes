## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Shipping Speed as Proxy for Impact

**INSIGHT:** "The company prioritizes shipping speed as a proxy for impact, enabling teams to iterate quickly and de-risk decisions." Speed isn't about output volume—it's about learning velocity.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Speed sacrifices quality.
- **System Antimeme:** Measure outcomes, not velocity.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Move fast and break things is irresponsible.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Speed vs. quality tradeoff is assumed
- "Move fast" has become cliche
- The de-risking function isn't obvious
- Learning velocity isn't measured

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that speed and quality trade off
- The belief that outcomes are the only measure
- The comfort of careful, slow decisions

**Verification:**
> "Shipping speed as a proxy for impact, enabling teams to iterate quickly and de-risk decisions."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Build protective layers for focus** - Shield teams from distractions
2. **Align on strategy, not solutions** - Stop reviewing implementations
3. **Hire for desire for impact** - Over domain experience

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Speed is a proxy for learning** - Not output volume
2. **Protective roles enable, not burden** - Counter to lean orthodoxy
3. **Strategy alignment only** - Leaders don't approve solutions

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Geoff's antimemes share a structure: **velocity culture requires organizational design that seems inefficient—shipping speed is a learning proxy rather than output metric, protective layers shield focus rather than add overhead, leadership aligns on strategy rather than reviewing solutions, and hiring prioritizes desire for impact over domain experience**. Ship to learn. Protect focus with roles. Lead on strategy only. Hire for drive. The pattern reveals that Ramp's velocity comes from organizational structures that look like overhead but actually enable speed—where protective roles, limited leadership involvement, and non-domain hires create the conditions for fast iteration.

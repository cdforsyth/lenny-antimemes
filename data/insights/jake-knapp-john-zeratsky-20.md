## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Delay visualization** - Until strategy is clear
2. **Don't use AI for ideation** - Outputs are generic
3. **Align explicitly before building** - Not during iteration

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Differentiation is the common success factor** - Not execution or timing
2. **AI-generated ideas are generic** - Human thinking required first
3. **Early visualization locks in bad ideas** - "Primal mark" bias

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Jake and John's Foundation Sprint antimemes share a structure: **startup success requires the slow, human, non-visual work that building culture resists—differentiation is the common success element not execution or team, AI-generated ideas turn out generic making human thinking critical, early visualization creates "primal mark" bias that locks in bad ideas, and explicit alignment before building prevents iteration theater**. Focus on differentiation. Think before AI assists. Delay visualization. Align before building. The pattern reveals that the Foundation Sprint's value comes from slowing down the activities that startups rush—where strategic clarity, human thinking, delayed visualization, and explicit alignment compress months of misdirected building into weeks of validated direction.

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Most Deals Lost to No Decision, Not Competitors

**INSIGHT:** "40 to 60% of B2B purchase processes end in no decision" because buyers fear making wrong choices—the enemy isn't a competitor, it's customer paralysis.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Sales is about beating competitors.
- **System Antimeme:** Win rate measures competitive success.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Differentiation defeats alternatives.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Competitive framing dominates sales culture
- "Lost to competitor" is a trackable category
- "No decision" feels like failure, not insight
- The buyer's fear isn't visible

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that competitors are the primary enemy
- The belief that differentiation drives most wins
- The comfort of competitive battlecards

**Verification:**
> "40 to 60% of B2B purchase processes end in no decision."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Address silent objections** - What they're not saying matters most
2. **Arm the champion** - Equip them to sell internally
3. **Teach the market landscape** - Help buyers understand their decision

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **No decision is the real competitor** - 40-60% of deals lost to paralysis
2. **Teaching beats pressure** - Education over FOMO
3. **Champion sells without you** - The real pitch happens in rooms you're not in

### Pattern Across Antimemes

April's antimemes share a structure: **B2B sales effectiveness requires abandoning adversarial framing—40-60% of deals lost to "no decision" means paralysis not competitors is the enemy, teaching buyers beats pressure tactics despite FOMO training being standard, silent objections matter more than stated ones because prospects don't voice real fears, and champions must defeat internal stakeholders because real selling happens without you**. Fight paralysis, not competitors. Teach, don't pressure. Address the unspoken. Arm your champion. The pattern reveals that winning B2B sales requires helping buyers navigate internal complexity rather than persuading them with differentiation—where reducing buyer fear, educating decision-makers, and enabling champions matters more than competitive positioning.

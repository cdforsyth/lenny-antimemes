## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Hospitality Workers Had 70% of Customer Service Skills

**INSIGHT:** "During COVID, hospitality workers possessed ~70% of customer service skills, revealing marketplace inefficiencies that title-based hiring couldn't solve."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Job titles indicate capabilities.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Similar titles mean similar skills.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Filter by role, not skills.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Titles are the universal shorthand
- 70% overlap is invisible without data
- Hiring systems filter by title first
- The mismatch is structural

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that titles represent capabilities
- The belief that career paths are linear
- The comfort of title-based filtering

**Verification:**
> "Hospitality workers possessed ~70% of customer service skills."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Leverage your edge strength** - Not compensate weaknesses
2. **Use one guiding principle** - For all product decisions
3. **Enforce escalation timelines** - Five days, not "when needed"

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **70% skills overlap across titles** - Titles hide transferability
2. **Edge strengths over well-rounded** - Against development culture
3. **Single principle guides complexity** - Mission statements can work

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Hari's antimemes share a structure: **organizational effectiveness requires embracing simplification that feels reductive—hospitality workers had 70% of customer service skills despite title mismatch, exceptional PMs leverage edge strengths rather than becoming well-rounded, a single guiding principle enables product decisions across LinkedIn's ecosystem, and five-day escalation rules must be actively enforced**. Look past titles to skills. Play to your edge. Use one principle. Force escalation timelines. The pattern reveals that LinkedIn's complexity is managed through radical simplification—where skills-based matching, edge-focused development, single guiding principles, and enforced rules outperform the sophisticated, nuanced, flexible approaches that seem more appropriate for complex systems.

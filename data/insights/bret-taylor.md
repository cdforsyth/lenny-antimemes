## Stage 1: Popular vs. Rare Concepts

### Popular Takeaways (80-90% of listeners would mention)

1. **Google Maps origin story** - V1 failed, V2 became iconic by inverting the hierarchy
2. **Agents are the new apps** - The whole market is going towards agents
3. **Outcomes-based pricing** - The obviously correct way to sell AI software
4. **Sheryl Sandberg feedback** - "What is the most impactful thing I can do today?"
5. **Like button invention** - Started as a heart, changed because "like" was more neutral
6. **Learn computer science for systems thinking** - Not just for coding
7. **Three AI market segments** - Foundation models, tooling, applied AI
8. **FriendFeed lost to Twitter** - Despite better product, because of distribution

### Rare/Antimemetic Insights (1-5% would notice)

1. **Single-issue voter bias** - Founders systematically choose solutions matching their skillset
2. **The 30% rule** - If your strength is the solution, it's 30% likely you're wrong
3. **Incorrect storytelling kills companies** - Self-deception about why you lost
4. **Root cause every bad line of code** - Don't wait for models to magically improve
5. **The act of coding is becoming free** - This inverts which abstractions matter
6. **Confidence doesn't correlate with quality** - Often inversely correlated
7. **The drafting elimination pattern** - Not productivity gains, job elimination
8. **Product-led growth only works when buyer = user**

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. The Single-Issue Voter Bias

**INSIGHT:** Founders are systematically biased to see their core skillset as the solution to every problem. If you're an engineer, every problem looks like an engineering problem. If you're a designer, the redesign will fix everything. If you're BD, the partnership will change everything.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 4
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Psychological

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Your identity is tied to your skillset. Questioning whether engineering is the solution means questioning whether your value as an engineer is the value the company needs.
- **Psychological Antimeme:** The comfort of familiar solutions is seductive. Doing what you know feels productive even when it's not the right lever.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Nobody wants to admit their expertise might be the wrong tool
- The founder's authority often comes from their domain expertise
- Admitting the problem is outside your skillset feels like admitting inadequacy
- Confirmation bias makes your solutions seem to work more than they do

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The comfort of doing what you're good at
- The identity of being "the technical cofounder" or "the design founder"
- The illusion that your core skill is always the limiting factor

**Verification:**
> "So if you're a great engineer, the answer to almost every problem in your business is engineering. If you're a product designer, the answer almost to the proverbial redesign... it's like the dead cap balance of a consumer product like this next redesign will fix all of our problems. I don't know if it's ever, ever worked."

### 2. The 30% Rule: Self-Deception About Solutions

**INSIGHT:** If you believe the solution to your company's problem happens to be the thing you've been doing your whole career, there's at least a 30% chance you're choosing it for comfort and familiarity, not truth.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Psychological

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** This rule is specifically designed to create doubt about your own judgment - the thing you need most as a founder.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Even knowing this, the pull toward familiar solutions remains strong because uncertainty is uncomfortable.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Founders need confidence to lead
- Creating doubt about your instincts seems counterproductive
- Nobody teaches "distrust your expertise" in business school
- The alternative (admitting you need help elsewhere) feels vulnerable

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that your gut is reliable
- The efficiency of fast, instinctive decision-making
- The narrative that you're the right founder for this company

**Verification:**
> "And I think it's really important when you're a founder to be self-aware that you will naturally, subconsciously pick the thing that is your strength, your superpower as a solution to more problems. And in fact, if you think that's a solution to your problem, it may be right, but you probably by default should question it. If you think the thing that you've been doing your whole career is the way to fix your problem, it's at least 30% likely that you've chosen that because of comfort and familiarity not truth."

### 3. Incorrect Storytelling Kills Companies

**INSIGHT:** The stories you tell yourself about why customers don't buy become facts that drive strategy. But customers lie (pleasantly), salespeople rationalize, and founders confirm their biases. The narrative can be completely divorced from reality.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 4
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Customers don't give honest feedback because they want to be pleasant. "It's too expensive" is easier than "I don't see value in your product."
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** The story is comforting. If price is the problem, that's fixable. If value perception is the problem, that's existential.
- **System Antimeme:** The entire feedback loop (customer → salesperson → PM → founder) has incentives to sanitize and simplify.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Every link in the chain prefers the easier story
- Challenging the narrative creates conflict
- The real problem is often harder and scarier than the stated one
- "We lost on price" is actionable; "They didn't see the value" requires soul-searching

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The comfort of a clear, fixable problem
- Trust in customer feedback
- Confidence in your sales team's diagnosis

**Verification:**
> "So why did you lose a deal? You could talk to the salesperson who is on the account... It's very important to have intellectual honesty in those moments because you could say something like, 'Oh, they didn't buy it because the platform cost too much.' And that's something a salesperson might say. Maybe the real reason is they didn't actually see much value in your platform. So it was communicated to the salesperson as it was too expensive. But in fact, the problem was product differentiation."

### 4. Root Cause Every Bad Line of AI-Generated Code

**INSIGHT:** Don't wait for AI to magically get better. Treat every incorrect output as a debugging opportunity - what context was missing that would have produced the right answer? Build systems that continuously improve context.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Fixing errors one-by-one feels inefficient compared to waiting for the next model. But the compounding effect of context engineering is invisible until it's dramatic.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** "The models will get better" is a comfortable default that requires no action now.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Model improvements are constantly in the news, making waiting seem rational
- Root cause analysis is tedious compared to just fixing the code
- The payoff is delayed and distributed
- Most teams don't have dedicated "AI context engineering" roles

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The comfortable assumption that AI will solve itself
- The simplicity of treating AI as a black box
- The efficiency of just fixing errors individually

**Verification:**
> "We have an engineer at Sierra who exclusively focuses on the model context protocol server serving our cursor instance. And our whole philosophy is, if cursor generated something incorrect, rather than just fixing it, try to root cause it. Try to get it so the next time Cursor will produce the correct code... What context did Cursor not have that would've been necessary to produce the right outcome?"

### 5. The Act of Coding Becoming Free Inverts Which Abstractions Matter

**INSIGHT:** Programming languages were designed for human ergonomics. Python is popular because it's pleasant to write, not because it's efficient. When coding is free, we should optimize for verifiability and robustness, not ergonomics.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Frontier

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** The entire programming ecosystem is built on human ergonomics assumptions. Questioning this challenges trillions of dollars of infrastructure and millions of careers.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** "Why use this language?" has been answered by productivity for decades. The new answer (verifiability) requires completely different thinking.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Programmers have deep investments in current languages
- The alternative (formal verification, etc.) has historically been too expensive
- The insight requires accepting that current best practices will become irrelevant
- Nobody wants to hear their favorite language is "comically bad" for AI

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- Attachment to language preferences
- The assumption that programmer productivity matters most
- Decades of accumulated wisdom about "good" code

**Verification:**
> "I always laugh that I assume Python is probably the most common generated code just because of how much it's in the training data... It's such a comically bad thing for AI to generate just because it's one of the most inefficient programming languages of all time... Python was designed to be very ergonomic, almost looked like pseudo code for humans, for me to write code in a delightful way."

### 6. Confidence Doesn't Correlate with Quality of Advice

- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Psychological / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We're wired to trust confident delivery. Overriding this instinct requires conscious effort.
- **Social Antimeme:** Pointing this out undermines high-confidence advisors who have social capital.

### 7. The Drafting Elimination Pattern

**INSIGHT:** AI won't make workers "more productive" - it will eliminate entire job categories that exist only because of computational limits. Just as CAD eliminated drafting entirely (not "made drafters 10x faster"), AI agents will eliminate support staff entirely.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Psychological

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** "Productivity gains" sounds positive. "Job elimination" sounds dystopian. The same phenomenon, different framing.
- **Psychological Antimeme:** Admitting your job might be a "drafting" job is existentially threatening.
- **System Antimeme:** The productivity narrative is preferred by companies, politicians, and workers - everyone avoids the elimination frame.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "Productivity" is politically acceptable; "elimination" is not
- Workers don't want to hear they're drafters
- Companies don't want to create fear
- The pattern is obvious in retrospect but hard to see prospectively

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The comfortable assumption that AI augments rather than replaces
- The narrative that upskilling will save everyone
- Plausible deniability about which jobs are at risk

**Verification:**
> "When he first started his career in the late '70s and he went into a mechanical engineering firm, the majority of the firm were drafts people... Now, there are zero drafts people at his company. You just make the design in first AutoCAD and now Revit... The drafting has actually been eliminated. It's just not a thing one needs to do anymore."

### 8. PLG Only Works When Buyer = User

**INSIGHT:** Product-led growth is not universally applicable. It only works when the person using the software is the person with the credit card. When buyer and user are different (which is most enterprise software), PLG is the wrong motion.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 4
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** PLG is fashionable and feels modern. Direct sales feels old-school. The fashion obscures the fit.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Building PLG is more fun for product teams than building sales enablement.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- PLG success stories get more press
- Engineers prefer building product to enabling sales
- "Direct sales" sounds like the old Oracle playbook
- PLG feels more aligned with product quality

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that great products sell themselves
- The belief that PLG is universally superior
- The comfort of avoiding sales

**Verification:**
> "Product-led growth... means users can sign up from the website, often get put on a trial... those work where your user and your buyer are the same person... It doesn't work well when your buyer and the user of the software are different. So I'll use the example of something like expense reporting software. The user of that software is an individual employee, but the buyer is often a finance department."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **The 30% Rule** - If your expertise is the solution, default to questioning it
2. **Root cause every bad AI output** - Context engineering beats waiting for better models
3. **Ask "why?" until you find the framework** - Reduce advice to first principles

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Coding becoming free inverts abstractions** - Current languages optimized for wrong things
2. **Drafting elimination pattern** - AI replaces job categories, not just tasks
3. **Incorrect storytelling** - The feedback loop is systematically corrupted

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Many of these antimemes share a structure: **what feels right is systematically wrong**. Confidence ≠ quality. Familiar solutions ≠ correct solutions. PLG fashion ≠ PLG fit. Customer explanations ≠ real reasons. The antimemetic quality comes from our brains preferring the comfortable pattern when the uncomfortable one is true.

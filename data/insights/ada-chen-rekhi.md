## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. "I'm Here to Learn to Be a Better Founder"

**INSIGHT:** Ada explicitly told her LinkedIn manager that her goal wasn't promotion or title - it was to learn the skills she'd need as a future founder. She optimized her role choices for learning (growth marketing, subscriptions), not career advancement.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Telling your manager you're using the job as a learning opportunity for leaving sounds like career suicide.
- **System Antimeme:** Corporate evaluation systems reward tenure ambition, not explicit skill-harvesting.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We assume managers want employees who want to stay and grow within the company.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Saying "I'm here temporarily to learn" feels like it would get you deprioritized
- Managers are evaluated on retention, not on developing people who leave
- The honest version sounds ungrateful or transactional
- Most people never think to frame employment this way

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that loyalty is the correct presentation
- The belief that managers want you to stay forever
- The safety of pretending your goals align with theirs

**Verification:**
> "I even told my manager this when I first came in, 'I'm here to learn to be a better founder.' So there were a lot of things when I started connected, which I didn't know how to do very well... So if I can make my LinkedIn experiences match to that, that would be a huge win for me independent of promotion, or compensation or a whole bunch of those things because the plan afterward is actually to go off and start another company."

### 2. Coaches Are Terrible Mentors (The Anti-Sell)

**INSIGHT:** Ada's "hot take" as a coach: most people probably don't need one. Coaches are one person's opinion. A curiosity loop, a Reforge course, or building a tribe of supporters are often better alternatives.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Coaches have financial incentive not to say this. Ada undermines her own business by being honest.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** "Get a coach" is simple advice. "Figure out if you actually need one" requires more work.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We assume experts in a field will advocate for their field.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Coaches don't advertise "you probably don't need me"
- The coaching industry markets coaching as universally beneficial
- Anti-selling is counterintuitive business advice
- Most people asking about coaches want validation, not challenge

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The simple solution of "just get a coach"
- The assumption that experts advocate for their expertise
- The comfort of outsourcing self-development to a professional

**Verification:**
> "My hot take is that for the vast majority of people, they probably do not need a coach... If you're looking for a mentor, a coach is actually a terrible mentor in some ways because it's this one person's opinion. It's actually way better to run a curiosity loop."

### 3. No One Tells You the Rules of the Game

**INSIGHT:** Getting to executive or funded founder is an Olympic-level sport, but no one tells you the rules. What are the right intros? The right follow-ups? How appearance affects perceived competence? The rules exist, but no one makes them explicit because there's no safe way to share them.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Giving explicit feedback on appearance or social performance is high-risk, low-reward for the giver.
- **System Antimeme:** HR policies make certain feedback legally dangerous. The safe move is silence.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We assume if something matters, someone would tell us.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- There's no winning in giving this feedback - "don't cover this topic"
- The people who know the rules succeeded partly by luck or privilege
- Writing down unwritten rules makes them attackable
- The feedback that would help most is the feedback no one will give

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The belief that meritocracy surfaces what matters
- The assumption that if you're good enough, the rest will follow
- The comfort of thinking feedback systems are complete

**Verification:**
> "The difficulty of this game is that no one tells you the rules of the game. You actually don't really know what are the rules on how to get intros or give intros. What's the right way to follow up? What are the impressions that people have about you? It turns out that in a career context, it's so unsafe for your manager to turn to a young woman on the team and say, 'Here, let me give you feedback on your physical appearance'... That the vast majority of them will just never do it because there's no winning."

### 4. The Trap You Build Is the Trap You're Caught In

**INSIGHT:** The terrible outcome is waking up late-career, feeling trapped by a lifestyle and expectations you built - going to a job that makes you unhappy because you've constructed a life that requires it. The trap isn't imposed; you build it around yourself.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We see traps as external constraints. Recognizing we built them requires uncomfortable ownership.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Each upgrade (house, lifestyle, expectations) feels like progress, not trap-building.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** By the time you see the trap, the exit costs are enormous.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Current success makes future trappedness invisible
- Each lifestyle upgrade is individually justified
- The trap reveals itself only when escape becomes hard
- Young people can't imagine becoming trapped

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that career success leads to freedom
- The belief that more money means more options
- The comfort of "I can always change later"

**Verification:**
> "It's a terrible outcome to wake up one day and be late career and feel trapped because you have a certain lifestyle or a certain expectations of the people around you that you have to go work this job, but then you look at yourself in the mirror and you're not happy going in there. I think that's a terrible trap that we should all try to avoid."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Tell your manager your real goal** - Frame employment honestly as skill-harvesting, not ladder-climbing
2. **Seek the feedback no one will give** - Create safe contexts (coaching, trusted friends) to surface what's unsaid
3. **Audit the trap you're building** - Check if each lifestyle upgrade increases or decreases future optionality

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **The rules exist but no one tells you** - Meritocracy is incomplete; the unwritten rules are written nowhere
2. **The trap is self-constructed** - Late-career feeling trapped isn't bad luck; it's the accumulated result of your own choices
3. **Coaches are terrible mentors** - The expert in self-development openly anti-sells her own expertise

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Ada's antimemes share a structure: **the information that would help you most is systematically withheld because sharing it has no upside for the sharer**. Your manager won't tell you how to leave. Other executives won't share the appearance rules. Even coaches won't tell you that you don't need them. The antimemetic quality comes from incentive misalignment - the people who could help you have nothing to gain and much to risk from telling the truth. The cure is finding or creating contexts where truth-telling is safe: structured curiosity loops, coaching relationships, explicit feedback norms.

- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Expertise prevents mistakes.

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Say "I don't know" when you don't know** - Signals earnestness
2. **Watch for defensiveness** - Always a bad sign
3. **Use ignorance as permission to innovate** - Conventions constrain

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Ignorance enables discovery** - Inexperience as advantage
2. **Uncertainty signals strength** - "I don't know" is positive
3. **Attribution doesn't follow contribution** - Erasure happens

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Jessica's antimemes share a structure: **founder evaluation and startup success often depend on signals that invert conventional performance—admitting uncertainty demonstrates earnestness rather than weakness, ignorance enables innovation rather than causing mistakes, and the behaviors we associate with strength (defending ideas, projecting confidence) actually indicate problems**. Say you don't know. Don't defend. Use your ignorance. The pattern reveals that founder evaluation requires seeing through performed confidence to genuine qualities—where the things that look strong (certainty, defense, experience) often mask the weaknesses, and the things that look weak (uncertainty, openness, inexperience) often indicate strength.

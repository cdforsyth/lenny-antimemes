# Antimeme Analysis: Adam Fishman on Lenny's Podcast

**Source:** Lenny's Podcast - Adam Fishman Interview
**Analyzed:** 2026-01-19

---

## Stage 1: Popular vs. Rare Concepts

### Popular Takeaways (80-90% of listeners would mention)

1. **Growth Competency Model** - Four competencies: growth execution, customer knowledge, growth strategy, communication/influence
2. **Onboarding is underrated** - 100% of users touch onboarding, it's the first chance to deliver on your brand promise
3. **PMF for candidates** - People, Mission, Financials when choosing where to work
4. **Hire for trajectory** - Hire junior people who can grow into roles, not just experienced seniors
5. **Patreon creator success** - Human intervention improved first month revenue by 25%
6. **Opinionated defaults** - Make it hard to do the wrong thing, easy to do the right thing

### Rare/Antimemetic Insights (1-5% would notice)

1. **Hire internally first for growth roles** - Internal transfers often outperform external hires for growth
2. **Evaluation failure caused the bad hire** - When Wyzant failed, Adam's diagnosis was the founders didn't evaluate properly, not that he was wrong
3. **Observe the C-suite before joining** - Ask to attend an executive meeting before accepting
4. **Growth execution first, strategy later** - Junior growth people should nail execution before strategy
5. **Time is more scarce than money** - You're investing time, not just taking a job
6. **Back-channel the other way** - Candidates should back-channel companies, not just vice versa

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Hire Internally First for Growth Roles

**INSIGHT:** Adam "biases toward internal hire" for growth roles because you know what you're getting, faster time to results, and lower likelihood of making the wrong hire. External growth experts often fail because they lack company context.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** "Hire the best" usually means hire external experts with impressive resumes.
- **System Antimeme:** Recruiting teams are optimized for external hires. Internal mobility is often an afterthought.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We overweight visible expertise (the person's LinkedIn) and underweight invisible context (knowing the company).

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Recruiting is a function; internal mobility isn't
- "We hired the Head of Growth from Uber" sounds impressive; "We promoted Sarah from marketing" doesn't
- External hires command higher compensation, creating perverse incentives
- Founders want to de-risk by hiring "proven" talent

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that growth expertise transfers cleanly across companies
- The belief that external hires bring more value than internal promotions
- The recruiting-industrial complex that profits from external hires

**Verification:**
> "I tend to skew more towards internal hire to be perfectly honest, because I believe in creating opportunity inside a company... you know what you're getting better and much less likelihood of making the wrong hire there."

### 2. Evaluation Failure Caused the Bad Hire

**INSIGHT:** When Adam's stint at Wyzant ended badly, his diagnosis was that the founders didn't properly evaluate what they needed - not that he was the wrong person. The company wanted a "silver bullet" when they needed a system.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** When a hire fails, we blame the person. The hiring process is invisible.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Admitting you evaluated wrong means admitting you don't know what you need.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- It's easier to blame the person who failed than the process that selected them
- "We hired wrong" is a better story than "we don't know what we want"
- The evaluation process happens once; the failure is visible for months
- Founders protect their hiring decisions because they made them

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that failed hires are the hire's fault
- The belief that senior titles indicate capability match
- The comfort of "we know what we need"

**Verification:**
> "What they really needed... was to create a strategy to add on new growth loops and a system for how to execute against that strategy. If we had talked about that as part of evaluating each other... it would've given me a lot of confidence that it was the right hire to make."

### 3. Observe the C-Suite Before Joining

**INSIGHT:** Before accepting a senior role, ask to attend an executive meeting or observe leadership dynamics. Candidates have more power than they realize to request this, and companies that refuse are showing you something.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Candidates feel like supplicants. Asking to observe feels presumptuous.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** The logistics of arranging this seem complicated. It's easier to just take the offer.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Power dynamics in hiring favor the company
- Candidates fear seeming demanding or difficult
- Companies prefer to control the narrative through curated interviews
- "Just trust us" is easier than opening up the machine

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that interview panels tell you what you need to know
- The belief that you can assess culture from conversations
- The comfort of avoiding awkward requests

**Verification:**
> "I would have tried to observe the inner workings of the C-Suite a little bit more closely before I accepted an offer... if you're entering the C-suite, you're going to ask for more stuff... You can observe a team meeting, you can observe a product review."

### 4. Growth Execution First, Strategy Later

**INSIGHT:** The two most important competencies for junior growth hires are customer knowledge and growth execution - not strategy. If they can't execute and don't know the customer, you can't teach them. Strategy comes later.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** "Strategic thinking" sounds impressive. "Can execute" sounds pedestrian.
- **System Antimeme:** Interview processes weight strategic thinking heavily because it's easier to assess in 45 minutes.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Strategy is high-status; execution is low-status
- Senior leaders want to hire people who think like them
- Teaching strategy feels like mentorship; teaching execution feels like management
- "Ability to execute" is vague; "strategic frameworks" are concrete

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The belief that strategic thinking transfers across contexts
- The assumption that smart people figure out execution
- The preference for hiring people who sound impressive in interviews

**Verification:**
> "Teaching somebody how to execute is not that fun, right? That's a hard skill. The ability to execute is something like executive function skills... If you haven't developed those by the time you are an adult, I don't know that I'm going to be able to teach you."

### 5. Time is More Scarce Than Money

**INSIGHT:** When interviewing at a company, you're not just evaluating a paycheck - you're investing your most scarce resource: time. You can always get more money, but you can't get more time. Evaluate with the rigor of an investor.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We think about jobs in terms of compensation, not time investment.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Accepting this reframes every career decision as existential.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "How much does it pay?" is the default question
- Time feels renewable until you're older
- Opportunity cost is invisible; salary is concrete
- We optimize for the wrong scarcity

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that a job is primarily about money
- The belief that you can always change later
- The comfort of thinking your time is unlimited

**Verification:**
> "You're an investor... you're investing an even more scarce resource. You're investing your time. There is no way to get more time. We're all on a finite clock. You can always get more money."

### 6. Back-Channel the Other Way

**INSIGHT:** Companies back-channel candidates, but candidates rarely back-channel companies. Ask for references from people the company didn't choose. A PM Tal Raviv asked for a list of people Adam had managed - the first time that had ever happened.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Candidates feel they should be grateful for the opportunity, not investigating the company.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Finding back-channel references takes work and feels awkward.

**Why it resists spreading:**

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Hire internally first** - Look for growth talent inside before recruiting outside
2. **Observe the C-suite** - Request to attend an executive meeting before accepting a senior role
3. **Back-channel the company** - Ask for references from people the company didn't choose

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Time is more scarce than money** - You're investing your finite life, not just accepting a paycheck
2. **Evaluation failure caused the bad hire** - When hires fail, the process failed, not just the person
3. **Execution first, strategy later** - The foundational skill is execution, not strategic thinking

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Adam's antimemes share a structure: **the candidate has more power and responsibility than they realize**. You can ask to observe the C-suite. You can back-channel the company. You can demand proper evaluation criteria. The antimemetic quality comes from power asymmetry - candidates feel like supplicants, but the best ones should act like investors evaluating where to deploy their most scarce asset. The interview process is designed to evaluate candidates, but candidates should be evaluating the process itself.

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. The MVP Definition Reversal

**INSIGHT:** "20% of what you build drives 80% of the willingness to pay, but that 20% is often the easiest thing to build. Redefine MVP as 'Most Valuable Product' rather than minimum viable."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** MVP means minimum viable.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Build the least possible.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Minimum reduces risk.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- MVP meaning is fixed
- Minimum is taught
- Less seems safer
- The value inversion is missed

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that MVP means minimum
- The belief that less is safer
- The comfort of building less

**Verification:**
> "20% of what you build drives 80% of the willingness to pay. But the irony is that 20% is the easiest thing to build often." Founders inadvertently undermonetize by launching their most valuable features too early.

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Redefine MVP as Most Valuable** - 20% drives 80% of WTP
2. **Fix churn through acquisition discipline** - Not retention tactics
3. **Use POCs for business case co-creation** - Not technical validation

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **MVP definition reversal** - Most valuable, not minimum viable
2. **Churn prevention paradox** - Acquisition beats retention
3. **AI pricing breaks SaaS rules** - 25-50% value capture is ethical

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Madhavan's antimemes share a structure: **pricing and monetization requires accepting inversions about product, retention, and value capture—MVP should mean Most Valuable Product because 20% of features drive 80% of willingness-to-pay, churn prevention works through acquisition discipline not retention tactics, POCs should co-create business cases not prove technology, and AI pricing breaks SaaS rules because 25-50% value capture is ethical when outcomes are measurable**. Redefine MVP. Fix acquisition. Reframe POCs. Price AI differently. The pattern reveals that pricing expertise taught that monetization success comes from inversions that feel wrong—where most valuable beats minimum, acquisition beats retention, business case beats technical proof, and high capture beats SaaS norms.

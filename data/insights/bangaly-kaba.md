## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Slow Down to Speed Up: The 40/60 Rule

**INSIGHT:** When joining a team that doesn't fully understand its problem space, spend 40% of time on "understand work" (research, analysis, instrumentation) and only 60% on execution. Over time, as understanding compounds, the ratio flips to 85% execution, 15% understand. Most teams do the opposite - they sprint on execution without understanding, ship flat experiments, and never build velocity.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 10

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** All organizational pressure is toward more execution, faster shipping.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** "Slow down to go faster" sounds like a cliche; this is the specific mechanism.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Progress feels like shipping; understanding feels like stalling.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Executives want to see shipping
- Understanding work is invisible to stakeholders
- Sprint velocity is the common metric, not win rate
- The compounding effect isn't visible for quarters

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that more execution means more progress
- The belief that shipping velocity is the core metric
- The comfort of showing activity instead of understanding

**Verification:**
> "When I started at YouTube we were doing 60% execution, 40% understand work. And over time, as we understand more and more, the mix shifts. Now my teams are probably doing 80% execution, 20 to or 85% and 20 to 15% understand work."

### 2. Dog-Food in Adjacent User State

**INSIGHT:** A power user's experience of a product is completely different from a new user's. When you've used YouTube for years, the recommendation graph is trained on you. But if you create a new account and use it as an adjacent user would - no history, no training - you see an entirely different and often much worse experience. This reveals obvious opportunities that power users miss.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We assume our experience of the product is the product.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Creating new accounts and using products as new users is tedious.
- **Social Antimeme:** "I don't use our product like a real user" sounds embarrassing.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Power users don't see the new user experience
- Creating new accounts is friction
- The product team's job is the product, not the experience of specific user types
- PMs assume they know their product

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that your product experience is representative
- The belief that you understand the new user journey
- The comfort of not having to simulate beginner state

**Verification:**
> "If you were to go and create a new Gmail account and look at YouTube today, I guarantee you it's a completely different and significantly worse experience. And there's a lot of obvious opportunities missed... if you don't actually go and use things in a new state."

### 3. The Missing Component Creates Specific Dysfunction

**INSIGHT:** The "Managing Complex Change" framework has 5 components: vision, skills, incentives, resources, action plan. If you're missing any one, you get a specific dysfunction - no vision = confusion, no skills = anxiety, no incentives = resistance, no resources = frustration, no action plan = false starts. Diagnosing dysfunction means finding the missing component.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We treat dysfunction as general "things aren't working" rather than diagnosable.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Mapping dysfunction to specific causes requires the framework.
- **System Antimeme:** Organizations often treat all dysfunction the same way.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- The framework isn't widely known
- Most people don't diagnose dysfunction systematically
- Adding action plans is easier than fixing vision or skills
- The mapping isn't intuitive until learned

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that dysfunction is generalized
- The belief that all problems have the same solution
- The comfort of not diagnosing root causes

**Verification:**
> "If you're missing the vision component, you end up in a state of confusion or if you're missing the incentives, you end up in a state of resistance because people aren't incentivized to do the right work. Or if you're missing an action plan, you end up in a state of false starts."

### 4. PM as Coach, Not CEO

**INSIGHT:** The "PM as CEO" analogy is wrong. PM is more like a coach - you need to understand that not everyone is a star, you need role players, you need to develop people into their specific roles. Your success is measured by your "coaching tree" - where do the people you developed go? Their success is your success.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** "PM as CEO" is empowering; "PM as coach" sounds like less status.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We think about team performance, not about developing role players.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Building a coaching tree takes years to see results.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- CEO analogy is flattering and widely shared
- Coach sounds less important than CEO
- Role players don't get celebrated
- Coaching tree takes years to build

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that everyone should be a star
- The belief that PM authority comes from CEO-like status
- The comfort of the CEO analogy

**Verification:**
> "People talk a lot about product as you're the CEO, and I don't actually fully believe that analogy. If you think about it as a team sport... You need role players. You need really strong role players. You need people who feel valued in their role."

### 5. Bloom's Taxonomy for Coaching

**INSIGHT:** Use Bloom's Taxonomy (knowledge → comprehension → application → analysis → synthesis → evaluation) to diagnose where people are struggling. Someone might understand a concept but can't apply it. Someone might apply it but can't analyze across contexts. Managers need to live at the top of the pyramid across all their areas. This is how you "meet people where they are."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We ask "do they get it?" not "at what level do they get it?"
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Bloom's Taxonomy is educational theory, not tech culture.
- **Social Antimeme:** Treating adults like students feels condescending.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Educational frameworks seem out of place in tech
- "Meet people where they are" is vague without the pyramid
- Most managers don't have pedagogical training
- Diagnosing level of understanding takes effort

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that understanding is binary
- The belief that people should "figure it out"
- The comfort of not needing to diagnose learning levels

**Verification:**
> "Do they have the core knowledge that they need? Do they understand it but they can't apply it or they apply it, but they're not able to analyze it across different business segments? Right... How do you meet people where they are as opposed to just saying, hey, you need to figure this out?"

### 6. Instagram's Registration Had No Logging

**INSIGHT:** When Bangaly joined Instagram in January 2016, the sign-up flow had literally no logging between start and end - just "how many people started" and "how many people ended." Eight steps with no visibility into where drops happened. Even the best growth teams have shocking gaps in fundamental instrumentation.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 4
- Implication: 3
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We assume top companies have all the basics covered.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** "We need to add logging" sounds boring and non-urgent.
- **Social Antimeme:** Admitting your funnel has no logging is embarrassing.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Instrumentation isn't glamorous work
- We assume successful companies have figured this out
- Logging is "hygiene" that's easy to skip
- The gap isn't visible until you look

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that successful products have great instrumentation
- The belief that the basics are always covered
- The comfort of assuming your predecessors did their job

**Verification:**
> "Believe it or not, the onboarding, the sign-up flow at Instagram had literally no logging to it. It had logging of how many people started and how many people ended... And there was eight steps in the funnel and we don't know what is going on."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Dog-food in adjacent user state** - Create a new account and use your product as a new user would
2. **Use Bloom's Taxonomy for coaching** - Diagnose which level of understanding is missing
3. **Instrument the basics first** - Even Instagram had no logging in their sign-up flow

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Slow down to speed up** - 40% understand work early compounds into 85% execution later
2. **PM as coach, not CEO** - Build role players and a coaching tree, not a hierarchy
3. **Dysfunction is diagnosable** - Missing vision = confusion, missing skills = anxiety, etc.

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Bangaly's antimemes share a structure: **what looks like progress is often performance, and what looks like slowing down is often building the foundation for acceleration**. Shipping without understanding looks like progress but produces flat experiments. The CEO analogy sounds empowering but creates wrong incentives. Using your product as yourself looks like dog-fooding but misses the adjacent user's experience. The antimemetic quality comes from hidden foundations - understand work, instrumentation, coaching, diagnosis - that are invisible but compound dramatically over time. Teams that skip these foundations look faster initially but plateau; teams that build them look slower initially but accelerate.

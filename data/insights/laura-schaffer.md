## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Lower Confidence Intervals Accelerate Learning

**INSIGHT:** "Using 80% instead of 95% confidence allows running 2-3x more experiments annually, yielding net positive results despite higher false-positive rates."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Statistical rigor is essential.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** 95% confidence is the standard.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Avoid false positives.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Rigor is valued
- 95% is taught
- False positives seem bad
- The volume trade-off is hidden

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that rigor requires 95%
- The belief that false positives are the enemy
- The comfort of statistical standards

**Verification:**
> "80 plus percent of things fail... you can take advantage of that... run way more experience."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Use 80% confidence intervals** - Run 2-3x more experiments
2. **Ship embarrassing first versions** - Catches problems faster
3. **Share insights without approval** - Builds influence faster

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Lower statistical thresholds accelerate learning** - Volume beats precision
2. **Embarrassing iterations beat polished redesigns** - 80% failure rate
3. **12-month horizons for growth teams** - Prevents vanity metrics

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Laura's antimemes share a structure: **effective growth work requires accepting looser standards that feel unprofessional—lower confidence intervals accelerate learning despite rigor standards, embarrassing iterations outperform polished redesigns despite quality expectations, growth teams need 12-month horizons despite weekly metric norms, and ungating knowledge without approval accelerates careers**. Lower your thresholds. Ship embarrassing versions. Plan longer. Share without permission. The pattern reveals that growth leadership at Twilio and Amplitude taught that growth effectiveness comes from the looseness that feels sloppy—where 80% beats 95%, incomplete beats polished, annual beats weekly, and informal beats approved.

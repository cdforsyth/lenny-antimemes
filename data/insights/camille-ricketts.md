## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Cap Monthly Community Growth at ~20

**INSIGHT:** Notion capped monthly ambassador growth at approximately 20 new members to maintain intimate community feel. This deliberately limited scale to preserve what made the community valuable. The decision to constrain growth was counterintuitive for a growth-focused company.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Growth metrics reward scale.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** "More is better" for communities.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Caps feel like artificial limits on success.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Growth teams are measured on numbers
- Community size is the default success metric
- Limiting feels like leaving opportunity on the table
- The intimacy value is hard to quantify

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that community growth should be maximized
- The belief that scale is the primary goal
- The comfort of accelerating growth

**Verification:**
> "Capped monthly growth at ~20 new members to maintain intimate community feel."

**INSIGHT:** "Let community needs shape programming rather than imposing top-down structure." The community team's job is listening and responding, not directing. The energy and value come from members; the team's role is facilitation, not leadership.

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Cap community growth deliberately** - Preserve intimacy over scale
2. **Enable member businesses** - Align incentives through economic opportunity
3. **Listen more than prescribe** - Let community needs shape programming

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Slower growth preserves quality** - Speed destroys what makes community valuable
2. **Scale kills intimacy** - 5,000 ambassadors isn't better than 200
3. **Community as economic platform** - Member businesses, not just engagement

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Camille's antimemes share a structure: **the metrics that look like community success (scale, speed, control) often destroy the actual value (intimacy, emotional quality, member agency)**. Capping growth looks like underperformance but preserves value. Slower scaling looks like inefficiency but maintains quality. Listening looks like passivity but creates better programs. The pattern reveals that community building requires inverting the standard growth playbook - the things that work for product don't work for community, because community value is relational, not transactional.

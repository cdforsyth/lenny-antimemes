# Antimeme Analysis: Ryan Singer on Lenny's Podcast

**Source:** Lenny's Podcast - Ryan Singer Interview (Former Head of Strategy at 37signals, Creator of Shape Up)
**Analyzed:** 2026-01-20

---

## Stage 1: Popular vs. Rare Concepts

### Popular Takeaways (80-90% of listeners would mention)

1. **Shape Up is a methodology** - Six-week cycles work
2. **Basecamp built differently** - Small team, profitable
3. **Scope needs definition** - Know what you're building
4. **Process matters** - How you work affects output
5. **Teams need autonomy** - Give them space
6. **Shipping is important** - Get things out the door

### Rare/Antimemetic Insights (1-5% would notice)

1. **Feature factories aren't the real problem** - Lack of shipping velocity is
2. **Basecamp is actually NOT a good model to copy** - Structural advantages others lack
3. **Shaping requires engineers early** - Before detailed designs
4. **PMs should move upstream, not manage processes** - Strategic definition becomes bottleneck
5. **Beautiful Figma files blow up upon reality** - Without engineer input
6. **AI makes building faster, making definition the bottleneck** - Upstream becomes critical

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

**INSIGHT:** "Most critique focuses on building unused features, but the widespread struggle is actually that stuff isn't moving, it's dragging. Teams rarely have predictable shipping; they need better input to the factory, not fewer features."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**

- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**

- Implication: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Narrative / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Narrative Antimeme:** Feature factories are the problem.
- **System Antimeme:** Build fewer features.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Stop shipping so much.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Feature factory critique is popular
- Fewer features is taught
- Stopping is valued
- The velocity problem is missed

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that too many features is the problem
- The belief that fewer features helps
- The comfort of feature factory critique

**Verification:**
> "The widespread struggle is actually that stuff isn't moving, it's dragging."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Fix velocity not feature count** - Better input to the factory
2. **Include engineers in shaping** - Before detailed designs
3. **Move PMs upstream** - Problem definition beats process management

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Feature factories aren't the real problem** - Shipping velocity is
2. **Basecamp is NOT a good model to copy** - Structural advantages others lack
3. **PMs should move upstream** - Definition becomes bottleneck

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Ryan's antimemes share a structure: **effective product development requires accepting counterintuitive truths about feature factories, role models, and PM scope—feature factories aren't the real problem because lack of shipping velocity is the actual struggle, Basecamp is actually not a good model to copy because most companies lack its structural advantages, shaping requires engineers early because beautiful Figma files blow up upon reality, and PMs should move upstream because strategic definition becomes the bottleneck when building gets faster**. Fix velocity. Adapt not copy. Shape with engineers. Move upstream. The pattern reveals that creating Shape Up taught that product development success comes from inversions that feel uncomfortable—where velocity beats reduction, adaptation beats copying, early involvement beats handoff, and definition beats coordination.

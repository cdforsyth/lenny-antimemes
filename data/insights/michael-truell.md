## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Custom Model Development Was Unexpected

**INSIGHT:** "Every magic moment in Cursor involves a custom model in some way. The team didn't plan this—it emerged as they discovered foundation models couldn't handle specialized tasks like ultra-fast autocomplete."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Technical

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Foundation models are sufficient.
- **Technical Antimeme:** Don't build custom models.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Use existing APIs.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Foundation models seem complete
- Custom models seem hard
- APIs are the standard approach
- The necessity emerged gradually

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that foundation models suffice
- The belief that custom is unnecessary
- The comfort of API-only approach

**Verification:**
> Most founders assume they'll use existing models exclusively. Truell reveals: "every magic moment in Cursor involves a custom model in some way." The team didn't plan this—it emerged gradually as they discovered foundation models couldn't handle specialized tasks.

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Expect to build custom models** - Foundation models have limits
2. **Hire slower than you think** - Cultural fit matters more
3. **Senior engineers need AI training** - They underestimate capabilities

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Custom models are necessary** - Every magic moment involves one
2. **Hired too slowly not too fast** - 60 people for $300M ARR
3. **Growth feels gradual even when exponential** - No inflection points

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Michael's antimemes share a structure: **building transformative AI products requires accepting counterintuitive truths about models, hiring, growth, and expertise—custom model development was unexpected but every magic moment requires it, hiring too slowly not too fast enabled $300M ARR with 60 people, growth was exponentially consistent not explosive with no inflection points, and senior engineers underestimate AI capabilities more than juniors**. Build custom models. Hire slowly. Expect gradual. Train seniors. The pattern reveals that building Cursor taught that AI company success comes from inversions that feel wrong—where custom beats standard, slow beats fast, gradual beats explosive, and juniors beat seniors on adoption.

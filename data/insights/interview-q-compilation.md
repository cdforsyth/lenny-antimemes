- **Psychological Antimeme:** Attributing luck shows character.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Reward modest answers.

**Why it resists spreading:**

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Watch for excessive humility in interviews** - May signal low self-awareness
2. **Use low-stakes fictional scenarios** - Reveal better thinking than real cases
3. **Don't over-reward preparation** - May mask inauthenticity

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Low stakes reveal better thinking** - Fictional beats practical
2. **Interview preparation backfires** - Skilled candidates sound inauthentic
3. **Humility can be a red flag** - Excessive luck attribution lacks self-awareness

### Pattern Across Antimemes

This interview compilation's antimemes share a structure: **hiring success requires accepting counterintuitive truths about humility, stakes, and preparation—humility is a red flag when overdone because excessive luck attribution shows lack of self-awareness, low stakes reveal better thinking because fictional scenarios expose sharper reasoning than memorized narratives, interview preparation backfires because skilled candidates sound inauthentic when predicting what you want, and children outthink professionals because adults overthink while kids rapidly simplify**. Watch for excessive humility. Use fictional scenarios. Discount over-preparation. Value simplification. The pattern reveals that experienced interviewers taught that hiring insight comes from inversions that feel wrong—where humility warns, fiction reveals, preparation masks, and simplicity wins.

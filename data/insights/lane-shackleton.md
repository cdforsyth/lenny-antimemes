## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Career Ladders Actively Harm Culture

**INSIGHT:** "Most organizations implement leveling systems that inadvertently shift incentives from company success to individual advancement; Coda deliberately delayed introducing levels."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y

- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Organizational / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Organizational Antimeme:** Levels provide clarity.
- **System Antimeme:** Career ladders motivate.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Structure enables growth.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Levels seem necessary
- Ladders are universal
- Structure is expected
- The incentive flip is hidden

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that levels provide clarity
- The belief that ladders motivate
- The comfort of standard structures

**Verification:**
> "As soon as you introduce this, the incentives flip from being company focused to individual focused."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Delay career ladders** - They flip incentives
2. **Plan only 10% of time** - Execution reveals more
3. **Talk to diverse customers continuously** - Not deep relationships

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Career ladders harm culture** - Individual over company focus
2. **Minimal planning beats thorough** - False certainty trap
3. **Separate strategy from OKRs** - Conflation kills strategy

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Lane's antimemes share a structure: **effective product leadership requires rejecting organizational defaults—career ladders actively harm culture by flipping incentives from company to individual, planning should be minimal not thorough because execution reveals what planning can't, diverse continuous customer contact beats deep relationships for intuition, and strategy must be separated from OKRs or metrics discussions dominate**. Delay levels. Plan minimally. Diversify customer contact. Separate strategy. The pattern reveals that 8 years as CPO at Coda taught that organizational best practices often backfire—where levels harm, planning wastes, depth misleads, and combined processes kill strategy.

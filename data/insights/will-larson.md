## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Use imperfect actionable measures** - Don't wait for perfect data
2. **Test values for reversibility** - Would anyone choose the opposite?
3. **Trust reality over models** - When they conflict, reality wins

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Stop coddling engineers** - Real responsibility, not protection
2. **Models aren't reality** - Observation beats analysis
3. **Generic values are useless** - Reversibility test exposes them

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Will's antimemes share a structure: **engineering leadership norms (protecting engineers, trusting models, waiting for good data, declaring universal values) often produce the opposite of their intended effect—real responsibility beats protection, imperfect action beats perfect data, and reversible values beat universal ones**. Hold engineers accountable. Act on imperfect data. Make values that distinguish. The pattern reveals that engineering leadership requires challenging the protective, perfectionist, universal norms that feel like good management—and that effectiveness often means choosing the uncomfortable option.

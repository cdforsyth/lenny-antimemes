## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Presentation Quality Paradox

**INSIGHT:** "Her most-viewed LinkedIn video wasn't her polished, professionally produced one—it was an informal, uncolor-corrected recording. High production value doesn't equal higher engagement."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Identity / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Identity Antimeme:** Professionals produce polished work.
- **System Antimeme:** Quality correlates with production value.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Invest in production.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Polish is valued
- Production quality is standard
- Investment seems required
- The engagement paradox is hidden

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that polish drives engagement
- The belief that production value helps
- The comfort of professional standards

**Verification:**
> Nancy discovered that her most-viewed LinkedIn video wasn't her polished, professionally produced one—it was an informal, uncolor-corrected recording. This challenges the assumption that high production value equals higher engagement.

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Skip polish for engagement** - Informal beats professional
2. **Prepare more for internal** - Skeptical expert audiences
3. **Use frameworks everywhere** - Even convincing spouse

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Presentation quality paradox** - Unpolished performs better
2. **Internal is harder than external** - Expert teams are skeptical
3. **Process gaps trump execution** - Map processes to resolve conflict

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Nancy's antimemes share a structure: **effective communication requires accepting counterintuitive truths about quality, audiences, and conflict—the presentation quality paradox shows unpolished videos outperform professional ones, internal presentations are harder than stage talks because expert teams are more skeptical, the micro-story application works in casual moments not just formal ones, and process gaps trump execution because mapping processes resolves disputes faster than debate**. Skip polish. Prepare for internal. Use frameworks everywhere. Map processes. The pattern reveals that decades of presentation expertise taught that communication effectiveness comes from inversions that feel unprofessional—where informal beats polished, internal beats external, casual beats formal, and mapping beats debating.

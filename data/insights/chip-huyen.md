## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Why Do You Need to Keep Up With AI News?

**INSIGHT:** Chip questions the common assumption that keeping up with the latest AI news is essential. She suggests users' actual needs matter more than technological trends. Most of what improves AI applications has nothing to do with news - it's talking to users, preparing data, writing better prompts.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Keeping current is a badge of professionalism.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** FOMO makes news feel essential.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** News consumption is habitual.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "Staying current" is a professional identity marker
- Fear of missing important developments
- AI news is exciting and engaging
- The alternative (talk to users, prep data) is less interesting

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that AI news improves AI applications
- The belief that staying current is professionally essential
- The comfort of consuming instead of building

**Verification:**
> "'Why do you need to keep up to date with the latest AI news?' - Chip questions this common assumption."

### 2. Highest Performers Gain Most From AI Tools

**INSIGHT:** Chip's research with a 30-40 person engineering team revealed counterintuitive findings: highest-performing engineers gained most from coding assistants like Cursor, yet some senior engineers resist these tools entirely. The tools amplify existing capability rather than compensating for gaps.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** AI tools are assumed to help weaker performers most.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** "Leveling the playing field" is the expected narrative.
- **Social Antimeme:** Top performers "don't need help."

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "AI helps everyone equally" is the common assumption
- The Matthew effect (rich get richer) is uncomfortable
- Training is often targeted at lower performers
- The amplification effect isn't intuitive

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that AI levels the playing field
- The belief that lower performers benefit most
- The comfort of democratization narratives

**Verification:**
> "Highest-performing engineers gained most from coding assistants like Cursor, yet some senior engineers resist these tools entirely."

### 3. Eval Is a System Problem, Not Separate

**INSIGHT:** "Eval is not a separate problem. It's a system problem." This breaks down organizational silos between engineering, product, and marketing teams. Evaluation of AI can't be isolated to a quality team - it requires cross-functional integration because the system properties determine what "good" means.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Organizations separate QA from development.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Cross-functional work is hard to organize.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Evaluation seems like a testing phase.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Org charts separate functions
- "Evals" sounds like testing, which is downstream
- Cross-functional collaboration requires coordination
- The system framing is more complex

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that eval is a testing function
- The belief that quality can be isolated
- The comfort of functional separation

**Verification:**

### 4. Coding Is Means to an End; CS Is System Thinking

**INSIGHT:** Chip references Professor Mehran Sahami's framework: "Coding is just a means to an end. CS is about system thinking." This reframes education priorities from syntax and tools to understanding how systems work. The skill isn't coding - it's thinking about systems.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Coding skills are the job requirement.
- **Social Antimeme:** Technical interviews focus on coding.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** System thinking is abstract; coding is concrete.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Job postings list languages and frameworks
- Coding bootcamps emphasize syntax
- System thinking is harder to assess
- The immediate reward is writing code that works

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that coding skill is the goal
- The belief that languages matter most
- The comfort of concrete skill building

**Verification:**
> "'Coding is just a means to an end. CS is about system thinking' - Professor Mehran Sahami."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Question AI news consumption** - User needs matter more than trends
2. **Focus on system thinking** - Not just coding syntax
3. **Treat eval as system problem** - Cross-functional, not isolated

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Top performers benefit most from AI** - Amplification, not leveling
2. **AI news may be irrelevant** - What improves apps isn't in the news
3. **Eval is system, not testing** - Organizational structure implication

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Chip's antimemes share a structure: **the activities that feel like progress in AI (following news, learning frameworks, running evals) are often disconnected from what actually improves AI applications (user conversations, data prep, system thinking)**. The viral chart she shared captures this: what people think matters versus what actually matters are almost opposite lists. The antimemetic quality comes from the gap between visible professional activity and actual application improvement - the things that feel productive aren't.

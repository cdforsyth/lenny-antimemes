## Stage 1: Popular vs. Rare Concepts

### Popular Takeaways (80-90% of listeners would mention)

1. **"The more it matters, the more design matters"** - Maturing markets
2. **No A/B testing** - Intuition over data

4. **Paid work trials over interviews** - Actual work experience

2. **"Go slow to go fast"** - Thoughtfulness over rushing

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. One PM for Entire Company

**INSIGHT:** "Linear maintains one product manager (Nan Yu) with engineers and designers leading projects. This structure encourages broader ownership and accountability beyond specialized roles."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 3
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** PM ratios are standard practice (1 PM per X engineers).
- **Social Antimeme:** PMs are expected at well-functioning companies.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** More PMs = better product thinking.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- PM hiring is default as companies grow
- "One PM" sounds under-resourced
- The ownership transfer mechanism isn't obvious
- It challenges PM profession identity

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that PM scaling matches engineering
- The belief that more PMs improve products
- The comfort of standard org structures

**Verification:**
> "Linear maintains one product manager (Nan Yu) with engineers and designers leading projects."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Cohort-based onboarding** - Small, regular groups for feedback
2. **Launch to select users first** - Curated feedback before public
3. **Hire for taste across all roles** - Not just design

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **One PM for whole company** - Engineers and designers lead
2. **Go slow to go fast** - Thoughtfulness prevents rework
3. **Taste as universal criterion** - Product sensibility for everyone

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Karri's antimemes share a structure: **Linear's constraints (one PM, slow onboarding, curated launches, taste requirements) produce better outcomes by forcing ownership and quality that abundance obscures**. One PM forces engineer ownership. Slow onboarding forces feedback systematization. Taste requirements force quality across functions. The pattern reveals that product excellence often comes from constraints that look like under-resourcing - the limitation is the feature.

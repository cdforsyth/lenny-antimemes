## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Evaluate experiences over features** - What you learn, not what you're called
2. **Question "years of experience" requirements** - They're arbitrary
3. **Seek 50/50 energy balance** - Not maximum engagement

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Titles depreciate, experiences compound** - Inverts career evaluation
2. **Redesign jobs around people** - Not people into roles
3. **50/50 beats 80/20** - Counter to optimization logic

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Bob's career antimemes share a structure: **career satisfaction requires abandoning the optimization logic that seems obviously correct—job titles and salaries depreciate while experiences compound, organizations should redesign jobs around people rather than fitting people into boxes, "5 years experience" requirements are arbitrary despite seeming objective, and 50/50 energy balance feels lighter than 80/20 despite the math seeming wrong**. Value experiences over features. Reshape roles around people. Question arbitrary requirements. Seek balance over maximization. The pattern reveals that Jobs-to-be-Done applied to careers inverts conventional wisdom—where the things we're taught to optimize (titles, salary, engagement ratio, experience accumulation) matter less than the experiential dimensions we're taught to treat as secondary.

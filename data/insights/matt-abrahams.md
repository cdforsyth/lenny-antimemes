## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Prepare to be spontaneous** - Practice enables improvisation
2. **Stop self-evaluating while speaking** - It consumes bandwidth
3. **Target the anxiety window** - First 2 minutes are peak

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Preparation paradox** - Must prepare to be spontaneous
2. **Embodied cognition over willpower** - Body directs mind
3. **Dare to be dull** - Lower standards improve performance

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Matt's antimemes share a structure: **effective communication requires accepting counterintuitive relationships between preparation, cognition, and performance—you must prepare to be spontaneous because improvisation requires practice, cognitive bandwidth competition means self-evaluation hurts performance, embodied cognition means body directs mind not vice versa, and anxiety concentrates in a narrow window rather than persisting throughout**. Prepare for spontaneity. Stop self-monitoring. Use your body. Target the window. The pattern reveals that communication expertise taught that effectiveness comes from the inversions that feel wrong—where preparation enables spontaneity, less monitoring improves performance, body leads mind, and anxiety is concentrated not persistent.

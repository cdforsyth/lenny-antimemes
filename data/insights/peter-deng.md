## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Product Experience vs Business Value Misalignment

**INSIGHT:** "The best user experience often conflicts with business value. Removing friction might delight users but destroy revenue. The PM job is navigating this tension, not pretending it doesn't exist."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Identity / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Identity Antimeme:** Great product means great experience.
- **System Antimeme:** User delight drives business success.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Optimize for user experience.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- UX focus is taught
- Delight seems aligned with business
- Experience optimization is standard
- The tension is hidden

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that great experience equals great business
- The belief that user delight drives success
- The comfort of pure UX optimization

**Verification:**
> The best user experience often conflicts with business value. Removing friction might delight users but destroy revenue. The PM job is navigating this tension, not pretending it doesn't exist.

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Match PM archetype to stage** - Builder vs scaler vs fixer
2. **Ask "what moved?" not "what shipped?"** - Launches are distractions
3. **Diagnose people problems first** - Product issues are symptoms

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Product experience vs business value misalign** - UX may hurt business
2. **Feature launches are distractions** - Most don't move metrics
3. **Biggest product problems are people** - Decisions are symptoms

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Peter's antimemes share a structure: **product leadership requires accepting counterintuitive truths about experience, shipping, and diagnosis—product experience vs business value often misalign because the best UX may destroy revenue, PM archetypes determine fit because builders and scalers have different skills, feature launches are distractions because most don't move metrics, and biggest product problems are usually people because decisions are symptoms not causes**. Navigate UX-business tension. Match archetype to stage. Measure impact not shipping. Diagnose people first. The pattern reveals that leading product at Facebook, Instagram, and Uber taught that product success comes from inversions that feel disloyal—where UX can hurt business, launches are ignored, and people problems trump product problems.

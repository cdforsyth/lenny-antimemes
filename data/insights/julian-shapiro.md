## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Bad Ideas Must Be Emptied Before Good Ideas Arrive

**INSIGHT:** "Bad ideas must be emptied before good ideas arrive." The creative process requires writing out bad ideas first—you can't skip to the good ones.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Good ideas should come directly.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Bad ideas feel like wasted time.
- **Social Antimeme:** Sharing bad ideas is embarrassing.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- We want to skip to good ideas
- Bad ideas feel like failure
- The emptying process seems inefficient
- Talent myth suggests good ideas come easily

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that good ideas come first
- The belief that creativity is direct
- The comfort of waiting for inspiration

**Verification:**
> "Bad ideas must be emptied before good ideas arrive."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Empty bad ideas before good ones** - Required sequence
2. **Build non-transferrable state** - Retention through accumulated value
3. **Draft one novelty, draft two resonance** - Staged writing process

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Bad ideas must come first** - Can't skip to good
2. **Iterate imitations into originality** - Process, not talent
3. **Bad ideas are diagnostic** - Learn what makes ideas weak

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Julian's antimemes share a structure: **the creative process that seems wasteful (bad ideas first, weak imitations, multiple drafts) is actually the required path to quality—and skipping steps produces worse outcomes than embracing them**. Empty bad ideas to reach good ones. Iterate imitations to originality. Separate novelty from resonance. The pattern reveals that creative excellence comes from counterintuitive process discipline, not from talent that skips directly to good output.

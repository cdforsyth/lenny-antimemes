## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Organizational Kayfabe

**INSIGHT:** Kayfabe is a thing everyone knows is fake but acts like is real. In organizations, status updates compound upward - each layer adds a small optimistic bias, and by the time it reaches leadership, you can be orders of magnitude off from ground truth. The dangerous part: if you try to speak the ground truth, you will be "flying tackled" and stabbed in the dark because acknowledging the kayfabe could shatter everything.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 3
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Speaking about kayfabe threatens your position. The people who understand it can't say it.
- **System Antimeme:** The organization's immune system attacks truth-tellers. Earnestly believing the kayfabe is safer.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Acknowledging kayfabe means acknowledging your own participation in it.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Saying it makes you a target
- The people who could articulate it are precisely those who would be punished for doing so
- Organizations become zombies - everyone privately agrees something is false, but collectively acts as if it's true
- Earnestly believing the kayfabe is the easiest cognitive strategy

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that organizations are rational information processors
- The belief that speaking truth is valued
- The comfort of thinking senior leaders have accurate information

**Verification:**
> "If you go and say, 'I think it's actually not going to work for these reasons,' a senior person will say, 'Alex, I agree with you. It's not perfect, but if you hit the ground truth button... the whole thing will shatter and we can't do anything.' ...Before you hit the ground truth button, you'll be flying tackled to the ground by somebody and stabbed in the dark."

### 2. AI Makes Individuals Better Privately

**INSIGHT:** AI tools make individuals more productive in ways that are invisible to the organization. Unlike collaborative tools (Slack, Figma) which help teams, AI helps individuals. But individuals have incentives to hide this - if your manager knows you're 2x faster, they might pay you half as much or lay off your colleagues. The massive value being created is happening below organizational visibility.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Individuals have strong incentives to hide their productivity gains.
- **System Antimeme:** Organizations can't see or measure what's happening in the long tail of individual usage.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** "No breakout AI company besides OpenAI" leads to conclusion that value isn't being created.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Employees don't want to signal they can do more with less
- The value is diffuse across millions of individual workflows
- Organizations look for scaled, measurable impact - not long-tail individual gains
- "How are you using AI?" questions get vague or misleading answers

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that AI adoption will be visible and measurable
- The belief that organizational AI strategies will capture the value
- The comfort of top-down AI transformation

**Verification:**
> "This is a thing that makes individuals better in a way they might not want to tell their manager about. 'I can now do my job twice as fast.' And if the organization sees that, they might go, 'Wait, we should pay you half as much,' or 'What if we get rid of some extra people?'"

**INSIGHT:** When Alex stopped caring about being promoted inside large organizations, he was able to do the highest-impact work even when it wasn't legible to the organization. The freedom to pursue emergent, serendipitous, gardening-style work - which can't be measured or attributed to individual effort - was the key to doing great work.

- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 3
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Career advice says make your work visible. This says do invisible work.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Accepting this requires redefining what "success" means.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** "If it works, people will say you got lucky. You can't claim credit."

**Why it resists spreading:**

### 4. 70/30 Allocation

**INSIGHT:** Spend 70% of effort on things everybody acknowledges are valuable - boring, linear work that no one can question. This creates cover. With the remaining 30%, plant seeds: encourage junior PMs' "silly" ideas, try experiments that might not work. The 70% prevents anyone from saying "what does that team do anyway?" while the 30% is where great things emerge.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Conventional wisdom says focus all effort on the important thing. This says split deliberately.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Requires maintaining two modes of work simultaneously with different success criteria.
- **Social Antimeme:** The 30% work is hard to justify in reviews or roadmaps.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "Do boring work to buy permission for interesting work" isn't inspirational
- The 30% can't be planned or promised in advance
- Leadership wants 100% focused on the roadmap
- The balance requires judgment that can't be codified

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that all work should serve the main goal
- The belief that efficiency means eliminating slack
- The comfort of fully committed roadmaps

**Verification:**
> "70% of my effort and my team's effort should go on things that everybody acknowledges are important and useful... You're trying to minimize the chance that any person in the company will say 'What does that team do anyway?'... But now once you do this, you have 30% of your extra time that you can plant all these seeds."

### 5. Communities Die Before They Know They're Dead

**INSIGHT:** A community with no one speaking is dead. A community with one person speaking is already dead - they just don't realize it yet. The leading indicator of community death isn't silence, it's the moment when only the facilitator is speaking. By the time you notice silence, you're past the point of recovery.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Activity (one person speaking) looks like life. It masks death.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Most community builders don't track this specific metric.
- **Social Antimeme:** The one person speaking often won't notice they're the only one.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- The facilitator is too busy facilitating to notice they're alone
- "At least someone's talking" feels like success
- The death state has all the appearances of life
- By the time it's obvious, recovery is impossible

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that activity = health
- The belief that a talking community is alive
- The comfort of not monitoring for early warning signs

**Verification:**
> "A community with zero people speaking is dead and a community with one person speaking doesn't yet realize it's dead."

### 6. Always Rules Beat Sometimes Rules

**INSIGHT:** For self-control, absolute rules work better than flexible ones. "I'll skip lunch every day" will break on the day of a big review. "I won't have dessert unless it would be socially awkward not to" is sustainable forever. The strength comes from never having to decide whether this is the exception - the rule answers for you.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Flexibility seems more intelligent than rigid rules. Admitting you can't be flexible feels like weakness.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Designing good always-rules requires knowing your failure modes.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** "That rule is too rigid" is the surface-level response.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Flexibility is praised as wisdom
- Rigid rules sound unsophisticated
- The failure mode of "sometimes rules" only becomes visible over long time periods
- "Is today the day I break my streak?" becomes a daily battle with flexible rules

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that flexibility is always better
- The belief that willpower can handle exceptions
- The comfort of not committing to absolutes

**Verification:**
> "Always rules are better than sometimes rules for self-control. If you're going to diet, 'I'm going to skip lunch every day' - you will at some point have a day with a big executive review where you need to be well-fed... Now you've broken the streak and now it's over."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Use 70/30 allocation** - 70% unquestionable value work, 30% planting seeds
2. **Create always rules** - Absolute rules with clear exception criteria beat flexible rules
3. **Monitor for one-person-speaking** - This is the leading indicator of community death

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Organizational kayfabe** - Status updates compound into orders-of-magnitude lies, and truth-tellers are attacked
2. **AI makes individuals better privately** - The value is invisible to organizations because individuals hide it
3. **Stop caring about promotion to 10X impact** - The freedom to do illegible work is where great outcomes come from

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Alex's antimemes share a structure: **what organizations can see and measure is usually the wrong thing, and the right thing is invisible by design**. Kayfabe means the status that leadership sees is wrong. AI value is hidden by employees who don't want to signal productivity gains. Great work is illegible because it's emergent, not planned. Community health is invisible because activity masks death. The antimemetic quality comes from the mismatch between organizational measurement and actual value. Organizations optimize for the visible and measurable - and the visible and measurable is precisely what doesn't matter, or worse, what actively misleads. The insight throughout is: stop optimizing for what can be seen, and start doing what can't be claimed.

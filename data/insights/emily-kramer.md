## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Diagnose Fuel vs Engine Before Hiring by Title

**INSIGHT:** Rather than defaulting to title-based hiring ("we need a growth marketer"), diagnose whether your constraint is fuel (content, messaging, positioning) or engine (distribution, channels, operations). A startup with strong content but poor distribution needs different skills than one with unclear positioning.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Job titles define the search.
- **System Antimeme:** Recruiting processes use titles.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** "We need a marketer" feels like clear thinking.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Job posts require titles
- Titles are how candidates search
- Constraint diagnosis requires reflection
- Hiring managers want simple definitions

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that titles define what's needed
- The belief that marketing roles are interchangeable
- The comfort of standard hiring processes

**Verification:**
> "Rather than defaulting to title-based hiring, Kramer recommends diagnosing whether your constraint is fuel or engine."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Diagnose fuel vs engine first** - Before hiring by title
2. **Write DRI lists** - Explicit ownership documentation
3. **Avoid activity-based goals** - Measure outcomes, not activities

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Constraint diagnosis before titles** - Changes hiring process
2. **Explicit "big bet" articulation** - Forces hard categorization
3. **Genuine skill set differences** - Marketing and product are truly different

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Emily's antimemes share a structure: **the implicit, assumed aspects of marketing work (title-based hiring, obvious ownership, activity metrics, similar skill sets) create dysfunction when left unexamined**. Constraint diagnosis must precede title search. Ownership must be written down. Big bets must be named. Activities must be separated from outcomes. The pattern reveals that marketing effectiveness comes from making implicit assumptions explicit - the default assumptions about hiring, ownership, and measurement are usually wrong.

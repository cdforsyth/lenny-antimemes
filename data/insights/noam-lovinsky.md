## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Kill Your Own Projects to Advance

**INSIGHT:** "Within months at YouTube, I advocated to wind down my own project, recommending leadership reallocate 50 engineers elsewhere. Even though that was likely negotiating my way out of a job in month three, that was the right thing for the team and the business—and it elevated me to lead YouTube's creator focus area."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Identity / Career

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Identity Antimeme:** Protect your projects.
- **Career Antimeme:** Don't negotiate yourself out of a job.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Advocate for your work.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Project protection is instinctive
- Job security is prioritized
- Self-advocacy is taught
- The advancement mechanism is hidden

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that protecting projects is strategic
- The belief that job security comes from project success
- The comfort of advocating for your work

**Verification:**
> "Even though that was likely negotiating my way out of a job in month three...that was the right thing for the team and for the business."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Anchor in 1-2 proven strengths** - Selective discomfort
2. **Question growth metrics** - They mask problems
3. **Work directly, skip branding** - Excellence over visibility

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Kill your own projects to advance** - Month 3 shutdown recommendation
2. **Growth conceals fundamental problems** - Triple-digit growth hid broken mechanics
3. **Authenticity outperforms self-promotion** - No tweets, exceptional career

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Noam's antimemes share a structure: **product leadership requires accepting counterintuitive truths about projects, growth, and visibility—killing your own projects can advance your career because the right thing for the business gets noticed, growth conceals fundamental problems because you don't understand what's working when everything's growing, selective career discomfort works by anchoring in strengths while stretching elsewhere, and authenticity outperforms strategic self-promotion because direct work beats personal branding**. Kill projects. Question growth. Anchor strengths. Work directly. The pattern reveals that leading product at YouTube, Thumbtack, and Grammarly taught that career success comes from inversions that feel self-defeating—where killing projects elevates, growth conceals, selective discomfort beats constant stretch, and direct work beats visibility.

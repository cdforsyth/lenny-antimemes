## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Audit processes for accumulated complexity** - Most isn't necessary
2. **Constrain AI access to curated examples** - Less is more
3. **Expect deep customization for AI tools** - Never plug and play

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Complexity was accumulated not necessary** - Work isn't complex, processes are
2. **AI amplifies excellence not mediocrity** - Top performers benefit most
3. **Constraints outperform scale in AI** - Curated beats comprehensive

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Tomer's antimemes share a structure: **AI adoption success requires accepting counterintuitive truths about complexity, capability, and constraints—complexity wasn't necessary but accumulated because processes made work complex, top performers adopt AI first because excellence amplifies rather than democratizes, off-the-shelf tools never work because every legacy system requires deep customization, and giving AI access to everything produces worse results because constraints and curation outperform scale**. Audit complexity. Expect amplification. Plan customization. Constrain access. The pattern reveals that implementing AI at LinkedIn scale taught that success comes from inversions that feel limiting—where accumulated complexity is unnecessary, AI amplifies inequality, customization is always required, and less access beats more.

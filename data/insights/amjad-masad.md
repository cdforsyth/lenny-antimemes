## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. AI Computer Interface (ACI) Not HCI

**INSIGHT:** There's an emerging discipline of AI Computer Interfaces - designing computers specifically for AI to use, not humans. LLMs are "alien creatures" that need different interfaces. Replit gives AI tools for editing, running shells, and checking screenshots - all optimized for how AI processes information (text representations, not visual interfaces).

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 3
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** We design computers for humans; AI is assumed to use human interfaces.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** "Computers for AI" sounds like science fiction.
- **Technical Antimeme:** The discipline is so new it doesn't have established vocabulary.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- All existing computer design principles are human-centered
- The idea of designing for AI users is conceptually novel
- Anthropic's approach (teaching AI to use human interfaces) is more intuitive
- The field is nascent with no established best practices

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that AI should learn to use human interfaces
- The belief that human-computer interaction principles transfer to AI
- The comfort of applying existing UX frameworks

**Verification:**
> "There's an entire discipline called like HCI, right?... So now there are papers about AI computer interfaces and interactions. And so large language models are trained on large stacks of corpus from the internet, but they're still kind of alien creatures. So they're not like humans, so they have different behaviors."

### 2. Jevons Paradox for Software

**INSIGHT:** When the cost of something goes down, total consumption goes up (not down as you might expect). As electricity got cheaper, total electricity spending increased. The same will happen with software - as AI makes software cheaper to create, total software creation will explode, not contract. This is why democratizing coding creates more software, not unemployment for developers.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** We assume lower costs mean less total spending.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** The fear narrative (AI replaces jobs) is more intuitive.
- **Social Antimeme:** Optimism about AI employment is harder to voice than fear.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Zero-sum thinking is intuitive
- Fear narratives get more attention
- The paradox is counterintuitive by definition
- Benefits are diffuse while losses are concentrated

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that AI coding tools reduce developer employment
- The belief that cheaper software means smaller software markets
- The comfort of predicting job displacement

**Verification:**
> "I think it was it called the Jevons paradox or something like that, which is when the cost of things go down, the total consumption of it goes up... And so I think that's going to be the case of software. As the costs go down, people will just make a lot more software."

### 3. Agent Will Call a Human When Stuck

**INSIGHT:** Rather than humans supervising AI that occasionally needs help, Amjad envisions reversing the model: AI agents work autonomously and grab a human when they run into a problem. The human becomes the fallback for the AI, not the overseer.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 3
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Current mental models have humans as principals, AI as agents.
- **Social Antimeme:** "AI will call you when it needs help" inverts status hierarchies.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Human oversight is assumed necessary for AI safety.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Current AI framing emphasizes human control
- The inversion of hierarchy is psychologically uncomfortable
- Safety concerns prioritize human oversight
- It's hard to imagine being on-call for an AI

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that humans must supervise AI work
- The belief that human-in-the-loop is always the right model
- The comfort of being the principal, not the resource

**Verification:**
> "You know what we want to do? We want to get to a point where the agent can go grab a human when it runs into a problem. I think that would be sick."

**INSIGHT:** When AI capabilities drop every six months, rigid roadmaps become liabilities. When Anthropic released computer use capabilities, Replit "slaughtered our roadmap" and immediately pivoted. The ability to abandon plans instantly when new capabilities emerge is a competitive advantage.

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Companies are organized around roadmaps and quarterly planning.
- **Social Antimeme:** "We don't have a roadmap" sounds irresponsible.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Investors, boards, and employees expect roadmaps.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Roadmaps provide apparent structure and predictability
- Stakeholders expect and demand them
- "We'll pivot when capabilities drop" is hard to communicate

**What's lost by accepting it:**

- The belief that roadmaps equal discipline

**Verification:**

### 5. Bottleneck Shifts to Idea Generation

**INSIGHT:** When execution is easy (AI can build things quickly), the bottleneck shifts to idea generation. Most people have more ideas than they can execute. AI unlocks execution, making creativity the new constraint. You become "limited by how fast you can generate ideas."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Execution is traditionally the hard part; ideas are cheap.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** "Ideas are easy" is a common startup maxim.
- **Social Antimeme:** Admitting you're running out of ideas is vulnerable.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "Ideas are cheap, execution is everything" is conventional wisdom
- The inversion is recent and counterintuitive
- Most people haven't experienced execution becoming easy
- Being "idea-limited" sounds like a weakness

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that execution is the bottleneck
- The belief that ideas are the cheap part
- The comfort of the old mental model

**Verification:**

And so now you open up that bottleneck. So now actually making things is a lot easier. Actually, you become limited by how fast you can generate ideas."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Roadmaps are liabilities** - Be ready to slaughter your roadmap when capabilities drop
2. **Bottleneck shifts to ideas** - Train the muscle of generating ideas faster
3. **Learn debugging, not Git** - AI-native coding skips traditional tooling

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **ACI not HCI** - Build computers for AI to use, not just humans
2. **Agent calls the human** - AI is the principal, human is the fallback resource
3. **Jevons paradox for software** - Cheaper software creation means more software, not less

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Amjad's antimemes share a structure: **what we assume about human-AI relationships and organizational structure is inverting, and the constraints we thought were fixed (execution difficulty, role boundaries, human oversight) are dissolving, revealing new constraints (idea generation, interface design for AI, capability response speed)**. AI doesn't use human interfaces optimally - it needs its own. Humans don't oversee AI - AI calls humans when stuck. Roles aren't fixed - everyone's on a spectrum. Roadmaps aren't assets - they're liabilities in exponential change. The antimemetic quality comes from the inversion of familiar relationships - the things that were hard become easy, the things that were easy become hard, and the structures we built around the old constraints become obstacles rather than supports.

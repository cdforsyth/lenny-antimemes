## Stage 1: Popular vs. Rare Concepts

### Popular Takeaways (80-90% of listeners would mention)

1. **Product ops = systems and processes for PM teams** - Core definition

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Quality of Questions Indicates PM Distraction

**INSIGHT:** When revenue teams (sales, success) start asking lower-quality questions or seem confused about product direction, it signals that PMs are distracted from stakeholder communication. The quality degradation is a leading indicator that product ops could help - it's a diagnostic, not just a symptom.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Question quality isn't typically measured.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Bad questions seem like the asker's problem.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** PM distraction isn't visible in metrics.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Question quality is subjective
- Blame naturally flows to the asker
- PM workload isn't connected to stakeholder confusion
- The diagnostic insight requires looking upstream

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that bad questions = bad askers
- The belief that stakeholder confusion is their problem
- The comfort of not examining PM capacity

**Verification:**
> "Quality of questions from revenue teams indicates PM distraction."

**INSIGHT:** "If there's no how you will be measured or what successful outcomes look like, that's probably a red flag." When a company can't articulate product ops success criteria, the role is likely poorly defined, under-resourced, or set up for failure.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**

- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Job seekers want to seem flexible, not demanding.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Asking about metrics feels aggressive in interviews.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** The opportunity seems good despite vague metrics.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Candidates don't want to seem difficult
- Excitement about the role overrides concerns
- "We'll figure it out together" sounds collaborative

**What's lost by accepting it:**

### 3. Product Ops as Strategic Partner, Not Service Function

**INSIGHT:** Product ops should be positioned as "strategic partner" to PMs, not as a service function that does tasks for them. The distinction matters: partners advise and enable, service functions execute requests. Mispositioning leads to ops being treated as admin support.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** "Ops" sounds like execution, not strategy.
- **System Antimeme:** Org charts place ops as support functions.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** "Partner" seems like title inflation.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "Operations" has service connotations
- Strategic positioning requires ongoing defense
- PMs may prefer a service model
- The partner framing challenges hierarchy

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that ops executes PM requests
- The belief that ops is downstream of strategy
- The comfort of clear service relationships

**Verification:**
> "Product ops encompasses... the dedicated role of individuals who serve as strategic partners to product managers."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Question quality as PM distraction signal** - Diagnostic for when to add ops
2. **No success metrics = red flag** - Job evaluation criterion
3. **Content strategy under ops** - Expand scope to adoption

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Strategic partner, not service function** - Positioning determines treatment
2. **Question quality as upstream indicator** - Look to PM capacity, not asker
3. **Non-PM backgrounds qualify** - Consulting, CS develop relevant skills

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Christine's antimemes share a structure: **the obvious assumptions about product ops (service function, PM background required, narrow scope) limit the role's effectiveness and career accessibility**. The strategic partner framing isn't title inflation - it determines whether ops is treated as admin support or strategic enabler. The question quality signal isn't blaming PMs - it's diagnosing where ops can help. The non-PM paths aren't exceptions - they develop the actual required skills. The pattern reveals that product ops success requires challenging the "ops = operations = service" assumption at every turn.

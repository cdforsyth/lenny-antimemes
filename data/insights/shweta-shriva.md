## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Redefine MVP for safety contexts** - High bar, not minimal
2. **Design for naturalistic behavior** - Micro-behaviors build trust
3. **Focus on impact not promotion signals** - Not wanting paradox

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **MVP means something entirely different** - Safety redefines minimum
2. **Level 4 beats Level 5 for transformation** - Pragmatic over perfect
3. **Promotion requires not wanting it** - Focus on impact instead

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Shweta's antimemes share a structure: **building safety-critical products requires accepting counterintuitive truths about speed, goals, and trust—MVP means something entirely different in safety contexts with an extremely high bar, you don't need Level 5 autonomy because Level 4 can transform transportation, micro-behaviors drive trust more than transparency because naturalness beats visibility, and promotion requires deliberately not wanting it because focus on impact beats visibility optimization**. Redefine MVP. Accept Level 4. Design for naturalistic. Focus on impact. The pattern reveals that building Waymo taught that autonomous vehicle success comes from inversions that feel slow—where high bar beats minimal, pragmatic beats perfect, naturalistic beats transparent, and not-wanting beats wanting.

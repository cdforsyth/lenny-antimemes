## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Only 8% of Notes Show

**INSIGHT:** "The system deliberately rejects 92% of submissions to maintain quality—fewer notes shown actually increases trust."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** More content is better.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** User contributions should be shown.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Reward participation.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Participation should be rewarded
- More seems better
- Rejection seems harsh
- The trust mechanism is counterintuitive

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that more content helps
- The belief that contributions should be shown
- The comfort of rewarding participation

**Verification:**
> "We show about 8% of notes...the bar is there. There's clearly some good ones in that remaining 90% that we don't show."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Reject 92% to build trust** - High bars increase quality
2. **Remove moderation buttons** - Force system excellence
3. **Look for cross-partisan facts** - Agreement exists on verifiables

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **8% acceptance builds trust** - Fewer notes, more credibility
2. **No moderation button** - System design over gatekeeping
3. **Layoffs can accelerate innovation** - Focus beats headcount

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Keith and Jay's antimemes share a structure: **building Community Notes required accepting counterintuitive constraints that platforms avoid—only 8% of notes show because rejection builds trust, no moderation button exists because forcing system excellence beats reactive gatekeeping, 80% staff reduction accelerated innovation through forced focus, and facts transcend polarization more than believed when verifiable claims are isolated**. Reject most contributions. Remove override buttons. Focus beats headcount. Facts can unite. The pattern reveals that Community Notes succeeded by embracing constraints that seem to contradict platform design wisdom—where rejection builds trust, removal of control increases quality, smaller teams ship faster, and agreement is possible despite polarization narratives.

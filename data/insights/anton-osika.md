## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Scaling Laws for Unsticking AI

**INSIGHT:** Lovable discovered a scaling law: the more work you put in, the reliably better the system gets. But the work is specific - painstakingly identifying where the AI gets stuck, addressing those stuck points, tuning quantitatively, and maintaining a fast feedback loop. They prioritized the most important stuck points (login, data persistence, Stripe integration) and systematically unstuck them.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We think AI improvement is about model size or data quantity; this is about systematic stuck-point fixing.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Finding where AI gets stuck requires painstaking observation, not just training.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Accepting this means the work is unglamorous enumeration, not breakthrough insights.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "We fixed the stuck points" is less exciting than "we had a breakthrough"
- The work is grunt work - identifying, cataloging, addressing
- No single insight to celebrate
- The scaling law only works with sustained unglamorous effort

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that AI improvement is about algorithmic breakthroughs
- The belief that better models fix everything
- The comfort of waiting for the next model release

**Verification:**
> "What we did was to painstakingly identify places where it got stuck... address the places where it gets stuck, tune the entire system quantitatively and having a very fast feedback loop to improve it in the areas where it got stuck."

This creates an arbitrage: if you can inspire people to be more ambitious than the ambient culture suggests, you can hire exceptional people who wouldn't have self-selected into startup culture elsewhere.

### 6. The Iteration Cost Changes What You Test

**INSIGHT:** When an iteration with a human engineer costs weeks and thousands of dollars, you test only what you're confident about. When an iteration with AI costs 30 seconds and pennies, you test things you'd never have tried. The cost structure doesn't just speed things up - it fundamentally changes what you attempt. Failed experiments become essentially free.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We think of AI as making existing processes faster, not changing what we attempt.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Accepting this means the set of testable ideas is expanding radically.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Most people use AI to do the same things faster, not different things.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "Faster" is easier to understand than "different"
- Existing workflows have momentum
- We don't notice the ideas we don't try
- Cheap failures require a mindset shift

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that you should only build what you're confident about
- The belief that quality requires expensive iteration
- The comfort of a constrained idea space

**Verification:**
> "I love that when you have an engineer you're working with that does a very expensive mistake to miscommunicate something... and here it's... You do that and then 30 seconds later you're like, 'Oh okay, sorry, that was wrong.' And then you could just try again."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Lunch together** - Schedule shared meals as a productivity investment
2. **Hire for caring first** - Ask in interviews what they've cared obsessively about
3. **Use AI to test what you wouldn't have tried** - Use cheap iteration to expand the idea space

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Scaling laws for unsticking AI** - Systematic stuck-point fixing, not algorithmic breakthroughs
2. **Generalists are now more valuable** - The era of narrow specialization is ending
3. **Iteration cost changes what you test** - AI doesn't just speed up - it changes what's attempted

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Anton's antimemes share a structure: **what looks like a faster version of the old thing is actually a different thing entirely**. AI coding tools look like faster engineering but actually change what you test. Generalist hiring looks like less rigorous hiring but actually produces better teams. Lunch looks like a break from work but is actually a productivity mechanism. The antimemetic quality comes from the category-shift being invisible - we see "faster" when the real change is "different." This is especially true with AI tools: the obvious use case (speed up existing work) masks the transformative use case (attempt previously impossible things).

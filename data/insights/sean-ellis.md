## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. 40% Is "Not That Firm"—An Alignment Tool

**INSIGHT:** The threshold emerged from pattern recognition, not rigorous statistical methodology. Ellis notes the number is "not that firm" but serves as a valuable alignment tool, helping teams agree on readiness before growth investments.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Metrics are scientifically derived.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Thresholds are definitive.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Hit the number, you're ready.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- The test is famous and cited
- Thresholds seem precise
- Pattern recognition is less impressive
- Alignment function is less exciting

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that 40% is scientifically derived
- The belief that the threshold is definitive
- The comfort of precise metrics

**Verification:**
> "The number itself is 'not that firm.'"

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Ask "why didn't you?"** - Reveals what analytics miss
2. **Fix activation before acquisition** - Priority order matters
3. **Use 40% as alignment tool** - Not definitive threshold

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **100x without product change** - Conversion leverage
2. **40% is pattern recognition** - Not scientific threshold
3. **Simple questions beat analytics** - Human insight power

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Sean's antimemes share a structure: **growth fundamentals require embracing the simple, imprecise, conversion-focused approaches that seem less sophisticated—the 40% threshold is pattern recognition not science yet works as an alignment tool, hundred-fold improvement came from conversion not product changes, simply asking "why didn't you?" reveals friction analytics miss, and activation must precede acquisition despite funnel thinking**. Use 40% loosely. Fix conversion first. Just ask users. Activate before acquiring. The pattern reveals that the "growth pioneer" built his methods on the humble, qualitative, conversion-focused approaches that seem basic—where alignment tools, conversion optimization, simple questions, and activation priority outperformed the sophisticated, quantitative, product-focused, acquisition-first methods that seem more advanced.

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Conflating "Doing It Yourself" with Competence

**INSIGHT:** People often conflate "doing it yourself" with competence, when the actual job is "getting it done well." Managers want employees to succeed and can remove obstacles. The widespread professional mistake is failing to ask managers for help because it feels like admitting inadequacy. But leveraging your manager IS the job.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Asking for help signals weakness.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Independence is a professional virtue.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Self-sufficiency feels like the goal.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "I've got this" is the professional default
- Asking for help feels like admitting failure
- Self-reliance is culturally celebrated
- Managers often don't explicitly invite help requests

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that self-sufficiency is the goal
- The belief that asking for help signals weakness
- The comfort of handling things alone

**Verification:**
> "People often conflate 'doing it yourself' with competence, when the actual job is 'getting it done well.'"

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Leverage your manager** - The job is getting it done well, not doing it alone
2. **Read parenting books for management** - Dr. Becky's techniques transfer directly
3. **Check for identity threat** - When disproportionately defensive, examine the real cause

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **You cannot not communicate** - Every moment signals something
2. **Identity threat causes worst behavior** - Defensiveness is about self, not issue
3. **Distinguish vision from implementation** - Protest and usage can coexist

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Boz's antimemes share a structure: **the signal we're responding to often isn't the signal we think it is**. Defensiveness feels like it's about the issue but it's about identity. User protest feels like rejection but coexists with doubled usage. Silence feels like nothing but communicates something. Self-sufficiency feels like competence but the job is outcomes. This pattern reveals that experienced reality (emotional, observable, intuitive) often points away from the actual mechanism. Leadership at scale requires the meta-skill of questioning which layer you're actually operating on.

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Pain vs. Context Framing

**INSIGHT:** The core misconception in product development is focusing on "pain and gain" instead of "context and outcome." Pain-based thinking leads to feature checklists. Context-based thinking reveals why people make decisions that seem irrational from the outside but are completely logical given their circumstances.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** "Pain points" is the dominant language of product development.
- **System Antimeme:** Entire user research methodologies are built around pain identification.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Pain is emotionally salient; context requires patience to uncover.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Pain-based frameworks are simpler to teach and apply
- "What's your pain point?" is the default research question
- Pain/gain maps directly to marketing language
- Context requires longer conversations and more interpretation

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that identifying pain is the hard part
- The belief that fixing pain automatically creates demand
- The comfort of problem/solution framing

**Verification:**
> "The core misconception involves focusing on 'pain and gain as opposed to context and outcome.'"

### 2. Snickers Competes with Protein Bars, Not Candy

**INSIGHT:** The same product occupies completely different competitive sets depending on context. Snickers consumed during busy workdays competes with protein bars, energy drinks, and quick meals. Milky Way consumed in the evening competes with wine, ice cream, and emotional comfort items. Understanding context reveals your true competitors - often from completely different categories.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Competitive analysis starts with category definition.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Products feel like they belong to fixed categories.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Cross-category competition is hard to track.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Industry reports organize by product category
- Investors ask "who are your competitors?" expecting same-category answers
- Marketing teams organize around category positioning
- It's uncomfortable to say "we compete with everything"

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that you know who you're competing against
- The belief that category leadership equals market dominance
- The comfort of well-defined competitive sets

**Verification:**
> "Snickers competes with meals and energy drinks during busy work, while Milky Way competes with emotional comfort items like wine or desserts"

### 4. Study Churned Customers to Find Your Real Competition

**INSIGHT:** Your real competitors are revealed by studying who customers leave you for - and it's often not who you'd expect. Churned customers show you the actual alternatives people consider, not the ones you've defined in your competitive analysis. This is systematically underutilized because it's uncomfortable to study losses.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Churn is embarrassing; we prefer to study wins.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Exit interviews are awkward and often avoided.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We assume we know why people leave.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Talking to churned customers feels like picking at wounds
- Companies fear negative feedback
- Win stories get shared more than loss stories
- Churn reasons are often surprising and uncomfortable

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that you understand why customers leave
- The belief that your defined competitors are the real ones
- The comfort of focusing on success stories

**Verification:**
> "Studying recently churned customers to understand competing solutions"

### 5. Adding Moving Services Increased Condo Sales 30%

**INSIGHT:** Sometimes the friction preventing purchase has nothing to do with your core product. Adding moving and storage services to condo sales increased conversions by 30% - not by improving the condos, but by reducing the anxiety of the transition. The Four Forces model explains this: reducing F3 (anxiety about new) is as effective as improving F2 (pull toward product).

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Product teams focus on the product, not the transition.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Sales problems should be solved by improving sales.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Auxiliary services feel like distractions from core value.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "Stay focused" advice pushes against adding services
- Org charts separate product from customer success/operations
- Moving services don't look like product improvements
- It's easier to optimize the known than discover the hidden

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that product quality is the primary purchase driver
- The belief that focus means ignoring auxiliary friction
- The comfort of working only on the core product

**Verification:**
> "Housing/Condo Sales: Adding moving and storage services increased sales 30% by reducing friction"

### 6. Don't Sell - Enable Buying

**INSIGHT:** "We don't need to sell them, we need to enable them to buy" and "help them make progress." This inverts the sales paradigm from persuasion to facilitation. The struggling moment already exists; your job is to reduce friction, not create desire. Selling assumes you need to convince; enabling assumes they already want to move.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Sales training focuses on persuasion techniques.
- **Social Antimeme:** Salespeople are measured on "closing" not "enabling."
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Selling feels active; enabling feels passive.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Sales quotas create pressure to push
- "Closing" language is embedded in sales culture
- Enabling requires understanding context first
- The psychology of effort makes pushing feel productive

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that more selling activity means more sales
- The belief that prospects need convincing
- The comfort of controlling the sales process

**Verification:**
> "We don't need to sell them, we need to enable them to buy" and "help them make progress"

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Study churned customers** - Immediately reveals true competitive alternatives
2. **Add transition services** - Look for friction outside your core product
3. **Context over pain** - Reframe user research around circumstances, not problems

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Pain vs. context framing** - Fundamental reframe of product development
2. **D-students outperform A-students** - Challenges expertise-worship in entrepreneurship
3. **Enable buying, don't sell** - Inverts the entire sales paradigm

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Bob's antimemes share a structure: **the obvious frame obscures the real mechanism**. Pain seems like the driver, but context is. Category competitors seem like the threat, but cross-category alternatives are. Selling seems like the action, but enabling is. Product quality seems like the lever, but transition friction is. This pattern appears because humans naturally focus on what's salient (pain, competitors, selling, products) while the actual mechanisms operate in less visible dimensions (context, alternatives, enabling, transitions). The JTBD methodology is essentially a systematic process for shifting attention from the salient to the real.

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Consumer Needs Unique Insight, Maybe Impossible

**INSIGHT:** "Consumer companies need 'a unique insight, maybe impossible' to succeed without one." The bar for consumer success is having an insight that seems almost unattainable.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 3
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Identity

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Consumer success is about execution.
- **Identity Antimeme:** My consumer idea is good enough.
- **Social Antimeme:** Consumer is a valid startup category.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- It discourages most consumer founders
- The "maybe impossible" bar is harsh
- B2B seems like the safer path
- Consumer success stories create false hope

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that good ideas are sufficient
- The belief that consumer execution can overcome ideas
- The comfort of working on consumer products

**Verification:**
> "Consumer companies need 'a unique insight, maybe impossible' to succeed."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Frame competition as attention** - You compete with Netflix, not startups
2. **Prioritize internal triggers** - External notifications aren't enough
3. **Create positive energy spirals** - Momentum is self-reinforcing

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Consumer needs impossible insight** - The bar is higher than expected
2. **Entertainment giants are your competition** - Different frame for competition
3. **Internal triggers are the hard problem** - Not growth or external prompts

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Ryan's antimemes share a structure: **consumer success requires insights and capabilities that seem almost impossible because the real competition is attention itself—not other startups, but entertainment giants fighting for the same finite resource, and the solution isn't external triggers but internal habit formation**. You need an impossible insight. You compete with Netflix. You must build internal triggers. The pattern reveals that consumer product building operates by different rules than B2B—where the scarcity is attention, the competition is entertainment, and the moat is habit formation that most products never achieve.

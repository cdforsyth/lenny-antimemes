## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. AI Distribution Has No Hardware Constraints

**INSIGHT:** "Absence of physical infrastructure creates explosive, non-linear growth trajectories distinct from internet/mobile adoption curves."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Tech adoption follows S-curves.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** AI adoption will mirror mobile/internet.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Use historical adoption models.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- S-curves are the mental model
- Historical analogies are comfortable
- The hardware constraint is invisible
- Exponential is hard to internalize

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that AI follows prior curves
- The belief that historical models apply
- The comfort of predictable adoption

**Verification:**
> "There's no weight on hardware distribution. It means that the space is just growing so exponentially."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Focus on domain specifics, not model capability** - Intelligence is already there
2. **Design agent interfaces, not manage people** - Different skill set
3. **Execute basics intensely** - Clichés become more true at scale

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **AI distribution is non-linear** - No hardware constraints
2. **Base model intelligence is sufficient** - Work is domain teaching
3. **Management becomes interface design** - Not emotional labor

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Scott's antimemes share a structure: **AI product building requires accepting that familiar mental models don't apply—AI distribution follows no historical adoption curve because there are no hardware constraints, base model intelligence is already sufficient so work is teaching domain complexity, management of agents resembles interface design more than people leadership, and startup clichés about speed and hiring become MORE true at scale not less**. Expect exponential adoption. Teach domain specifics. Design interfaces. Execute basics intensely. The pattern reveals that building Devin taught that AI product development breaks from both prior tech revolutions and startup conventional wisdom—where adoption is faster, capability is present, management is design, and basics matter more than sophistication.

## Stage 1: Popular vs. Rare Concepts

### Popular Takeaways (80-90% of listeners would mention)

1. **The Chasm** - Gap between early adopters and mainstream pragmatists
2. **Beachhead market** - Start extremely narrow to dominate
3. **Four adoption phases** - Early market, bowling alley, tornado, main street
4. **"Big enough to matter, small enough to lead"** - Market sizing principle
5. **Pragmatists need peer references** - Unlike visionary early adopters
6. **Problem-centric selling** - "Shut the laptop" and focus on pain

### Rare/Antimemetic Insights (1-5% would notice)

1. **Misaligned playbooks produce opposite results** - Wrong phase = wrong outcome
2. **Product-led growth cannot cross chasms** - Only works in land-and-expand
3. **Visionaries can't serve as pragmatist references** - The reference problem
4. **18-24 months to dominate a single use case** - Specific commitment
5. **Compelling sales reasons ≠ compelling buying reasons** - The confusion kills deals
6. **Consumer computing doesn't follow chasm dynamics** - Framework limits

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Good tactics are universally good.
- **System Antimeme:** Best practices transfer across contexts.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Success is about execution quality.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- We share tactics as universal advice

- The comfort of generic strategy advice

**Verification:**

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Match playbook to phase** - Misalignment produces opposite results
2. **Don't use visionaries as pragmatist references** - Different buyer psychology
3. **Separate sales reasons from buying reasons** - They're not the same

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **PLG cannot cross chasms** - It's phase-specific, not universal
2. **Same tactic, opposite result** - Phase determines outcome
3. **Early success doesn't predict mainstream** - Different buyers, different game

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Geoffrey's antimemes share a structure: **go-to-market tactics that work brilliantly in one phase fail or backfire in another—and the transitions between phases require completely different motions that feel counterintuitive**. PLG works until it doesn't. Visionaries validate but can't reference. Sales reasons aren't buying reasons. The pattern reveals that B2B go-to-market is fundamentally phase-dependent, and the universal advice that dominates startup culture (PLG, early wins, feature differentiation) often misses the phase-specific nature of what actually works.

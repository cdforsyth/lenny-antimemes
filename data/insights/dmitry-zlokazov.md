## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. 99% Done Is Closer to 0% Than 100%

**INSIGHT:** "If something is 99% done, it's closer to 0% rather than 100%." This reflects emphasis on completion—the last 1% contains go-to-market, stakeholder alignment, and cross-functional activation.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Progress is linear.
- **System Antimeme:** 99% means almost done.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Focus on the 99%, the 1% is details.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Linear progress is intuitive
- "Almost done" feels accurate at 99%
- The last 1% seems trivial
- The reframe is mathematically jarring

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that progress is linear
- The belief that 99% means nearly complete
- The comfort of milestone-based tracking

**Verification:**
> "If something is 99% done, it's closer to 0% rather than 100%."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Polish early-stage products** - Remove attribution ambiguity
2. **Focus deeply on 7-10% of projects** - Signal priorities
3. **Grant hiring/firing authority to POs** - Create real ownership

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **99% done is closer to 0%** - Last 1% is everything
2. **POs with hiring/firing authority** - Beyond PM influence
3. **Polish before validation** - Counter to MVP orthodoxy

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Dmitry's antimemes share a structure: **scaling product organizations requires extremes that seem inefficient—treating 99% done as closer to 0% because completion includes everything after build, polishing early-stage products substantially before validation to remove attribution ambiguity, granting product owners genuine hiring/firing authority, and having founders go deeply technical on only 7-10% of projects to signal priorities**. 99% is 0%. Polish before validating. Grant real authority. Go deep on few. The pattern reveals that Revolut's product velocity at scale comes from counterintuitive resource allocation—where the "wasteful" polish, the "extreme" authority, and the "narrow" founder focus actually enable the speed and quality the organization achieves.

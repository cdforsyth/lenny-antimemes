## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. High Conviction Doesn't Equal Closed-Mindedness

**INSIGHT:** "Separate conviction expression from conviction strength by explicitly stating confidence levels ('medium confidence' vs. 'I feel really strongly'). This creates psychological safety for others to disagree while maintaining forward momentum."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Communication

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Strong conviction means certainty.
- **Communication Antimeme:** Leaders should be definitive.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** State positions clearly.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Conviction implies certainty
- Leaders are expected to be definitive
- Clear positions are valued
- The confidence-level mechanism is missed

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that conviction means certainty
- The belief that leaders must be definitive
- The comfort of clear positions

**Verification:**
> Most PMs think strong conviction means stubbornness. Mihika separates conviction expression from conviction strength, explicitly stating confidence levels ("medium confidence" vs. "I feel really strongly"). This creates psychological safety for others to disagree while maintaining forward momentum.

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Mihika's antimemes share a structure: **effective product work at large companies requires accepting counterintuitive truths about conviction, polish, and scope—high conviction doesn't equal closed-mindedness when you state confidence levels, strategic scrappiness accelerates approval because underinvestment in polish signals momentum, culture beats roadmap for retention because emotional connection drives durability, and your scope is the world not your assigned project**. State confidence levels. Underinvest in polish. Build rituals. Think bigger than your project. The pattern reveals that product leadership at Figma taught that effectiveness comes from inversions that feel unprofessional—where uncertainty beats certainty, scrappy beats polished, rituals beat roadmaps, and vision beats assignment.

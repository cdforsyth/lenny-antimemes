## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Craft Through Silence

**INSIGHT:** "By creating space for customers to articulate their deepest challenges unprompted, product teams uncover authentic needs." Silence in customer conversations isn't awkward - it's a discovery tool. The space you create allows problems to surface that pitching obscures.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Silence feels awkward in meetings.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Filling silence is habitual.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** "I should explain" is the reflex.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Silence is uncomfortable
- Product people want to share their ideas
- Conversations are expected to be interactive
- The discovery mechanism isn't intuitive

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that explaining helps
- The belief that active conversation is better
- The comfort of filling silence

**Verification:**
> "By creating space for customers to articulate their deepest challenges unprompted, product teams uncover authentic needs."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Craft through silence** - Stop explaining, create space
2. **Hide identity in testing** - Theatrical constraint reveals friction
3. **Treat customer outreach as gift** - Effort signals importance

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Zero support tickets as north star** - Absence of problems as success
2. **Compounding over years** - Time horizon for infrastructure
3. **Silence as discovery tool** - Not explaining reveals needs

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Jeff's antimemes share a structure: **the counterintuitive practices at Stripe (silence over pitching, zero tickets over growth, hiding identity, years-long infrastructure) work because they create space for authentic signal to emerge**. Silence lets customers reveal real problems. Zero tickets measures actual satisfaction. Theatrical constraints expose friction. The pattern reveals that product craft often requires creating conditions for truth to surface, rather than optimizing for visible metrics.

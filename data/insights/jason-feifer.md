# Antimeme Analysis: Jason Feifer on Lenny's Podcast

**Source:** Lenny's Podcast - Jason Feifer Interview (Editor-in-Chief of Entrepreneur Magazine)
**Analyzed:** 2026-01-19

---

## Stage 1: Popular vs. Rare Concepts

### Popular Takeaways (80-90% of listeners would mention)

1. **Journalists serve their audience, not you** - Critical mindset shift
2. **Three-paragraph pitch maximum** - Keep it concise
3. **Pitch freelancers, not editors-in-chief** - More responsive due to financial incentives
4. **Match publications to your actual audience** - Not prestige targeting
5. **Be human in every interaction** - Conversational, authentic approach
6. **Press works as complementary strategy** - Not primary growth driver

### Rare/Antimemetic Insights (1-5% would notice)

1. **"Only chase press when you know what it's for"** - Goal clarity before outreach
2. **Coverage reaches only 5,000-10,000 directly** - Realistic expectations
3. **Create data that becomes newsworthy** - Like Barbara Corcoran's real estate reports
4. **Position yourself as expert source for breaking news** - Not feature subject
5. **Leverage > direct reach** - Article value in pitch decks and website
6. **Avoid PR agencies recommending press releases** - Red flag indicator

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Only Chase Press When You Know What It's For

**INSIGHT:** "You should only chase press when you know what it's for." Goal clarity must precede press strategy—awareness, investor positioning, or expert authority require different approaches.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Press is always good.
- **System Antimeme:** More coverage is better.
- **Social Antimeme:** Being featured validates the company.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Press feels inherently valuable
- Vanity metrics dominate
- Goal clarity is unsexy
- "More is better" thinking

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that press is always worth pursuing
- The belief that coverage volume matters
- The comfort of chasing any press opportunity

**Verification:**
> "You should only chase press when you know what it's for."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Create newsworthy data** - Generate news, don't just pitch
2. **Define press goals first** - What is this coverage for?
3. **Plan for leverage, not reach** - Article as asset, not traffic source

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **5,000-10,000 direct reach** - Much smaller than imagined
2. **Leverage over reach** - The article is an asset to deploy
3. **Goal clarity first** - Different goals need different strategies

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Jason's antimemes share a structure: **press effectiveness requires reframing what coverage actually delivers—direct reach is much smaller than assumed (5,000-10,000), the real value is leverage in other contexts (pitch decks, website badges), and strategy requires goal clarity rather than coverage volume**. Know what it's for. Create the news. Plan for leverage. The pattern reveals that press strategy often fails because founders optimize for the wrong outcomes—chasing coverage for its own sake rather than treating articles as assets to deploy toward specific goals.

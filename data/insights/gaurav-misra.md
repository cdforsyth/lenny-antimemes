## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Secret Roadmap Generates Breakthroughs

**INSIGHT:** "Dual roadmap approach: public (user-requested features) vs. secret (proprietary innovations). The secret roadmap generates breakthrough wins; public roadmap yields incremental competitive gains."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5

- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Customer requests drive roadmap.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Listening to customers is good product management.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Transparency builds trust.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Customer-driven development is celebrated
- Secret roadmaps feel non-collaborative
- Transparency is a stated value
- The distinction isn't commonly made

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that customer requests should drive product
- The belief that transparency is always beneficial
- The comfort of collaborative roadmapping

**Verification:**
> "The secret roadmap generates breakthrough wins; public roadmap yields incremental competitive gains."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Create a secret roadmap** - For breakthroughs, not just public requests
2. **Cut scope until useless** - Then stop cutting
3. **Give PMs growth channel ownership** - Not just features

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Strategic technical debt accelerates** - Intentional debt creation
2. **Secret roadmaps generate breakthroughs** - Public roadmaps are incremental
3. **Marketing is product distribution** - PM-owned, not marketing-owned

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Gaurav's antimemes share a structure: **AI-native product velocity requires intentionally doing what engineering culture says not to—creating strategic technical debt rather than avoiding it, maintaining secret roadmaps that ignore customer requests, cutting scope until the product would be useless, and integrating marketing into product ownership rather than separating functions**. Accrue debt strategically. Hide your best roadmap. Cut mercilessly. Own distribution. The pattern reveals that Captions' shipping velocity comes from explicitly rejecting engineering and product management orthodoxy—where debt is strategic, customer input is incremental, scope should approach uselessness, and PM boundaries should expand to include distribution.

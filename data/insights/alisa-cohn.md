# Antimeme Analysis: Alisa Cohn on Lenny's Podcast

**Source:** Lenny's Podcast - Alisa Cohn Interview (Executive Coach)
**Analyzed:** 2026-01-19

---

## Stage 1: Popular vs. Rare Concepts

### Popular Takeaways (80-90% of listeners would mention)

1. **Scripts for difficult conversations** - Specific language patterns for performance feedback, firing, promotions
2. **Observable facts, not judgments** - Ground feedback in what you saw, not what you think it means
3. **Three questions to end meetings** - What did we decide? Who does what by when? Who needs to know?
4. **Founder prenup** - Questions co-founders should discuss before starting (values, vision, conflict style)
5. **Pause when they get defensive** - Stop, acknowledge emotions, refocus on why this helps them
6. **"Yes, and..." from improv** - Build on ideas instead of blocking them

### Rare/Antimemetic Insights (1-5% would notice)

1. **Your job isn't to make employees happy** - Trying to keep everyone happy leads to company demise
2. **The feedback you need to give is the feedback you're avoiding** - The discomfort is the signal
3. **Avocado toast culture kills companies** - Confusing employee happiness with winning culture
4. **Co-founders should discuss how they monetarily value outcomes** - Vision of success can be radically different
5. **Burnout from the failure to confront** - Not having difficult conversations creates its own exhaustion

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Your Job Isn't to Make Employees Happy

**INSIGHT:** Leaders who focus on keeping employees happy end up destroying their companies. An employee not doing well, and you don't push them because you don't want to upset them? That avoidance leads to company failure. Happiness is a byproduct of winning, not a goal.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** "Happy employees = good leader" is deeply embedded. Challenging it sounds callous.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Accepting this means reframing your entire self-concept as a leader.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We conflate caring about people with making them happy. Separating these feels contradictory.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- It sounds heartless even when it's not
- HR departments, culture initiatives, and engagement surveys all reinforce the happiness goal
- Leaders get praised for high morale, not for hard conversations
- The counterintuitive truth (push harder = care more) violates intuition

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that being liked means leading well
- The belief that discomfort is harmful to employees
- The comfort of avoiding difficult conversations

**Verification:**
> "They're trying now to be the leader who everyone loves, but what really needs to happen very often is, we need to drive towards results. This employee continuing to not really do a great job at their job, you don't want to push them because you don't want to upset them... Ultimately, that leads to the demise of your company."

### 2. The Feedback You Need to Give Is the Feedback You're Avoiding

**INSIGHT:** The conversations you're dreading are precisely the ones you need to have. If you haven't been crystal clear with someone about their performance, you're part of the problem. The discomfort is a signal, not a reason to avoid.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** We tell ourselves we've been clear. Admitting we haven't is embarrassing.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Having the conversation is harder than believing we already did.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We remember our intentions as actions. "I meant to say it" becomes "I said it."

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Self-deception is comfortable
- "I've told them" feels true even when it isn't
- The hand-wavey "sort of, maybe" is self-protective
- Admitting you avoided the conversation means you're responsible for the problem

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that the employee knows what's wrong
- The belief that hints and signals count as feedback
- The comfort of thinking you've done your part

**Verification:**
> "Regularly with my clients, I'll say, 'Okay, have you been crystal clear about what you need from this person?' And what they always do is the hand motion like well, sort of, but well, maybe. Which means no, which means no."

### 3. Avocado Toast Culture Kills Companies

**INSIGHT:** A founder wanted to "do right by his workforce" with rituals like avocado toast at 10am. This evolved into long socials, cliquey gossip, and a culture committee focused on making people happy—while results cratered. The focus on engagement replaced focus on winning.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Perks and rituals are visible and praised. The rot underneath is invisible.
- **System Antimeme:** HR departments, culture consultants, and benefits packages all optimize for this.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Nice rituals feel like good culture. Distinguishing them is hard.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "We have amazing culture" is based on visible perks
- Cutting perks looks mean even when it's strategic
- The people who benefit from perks resist changing them
- Winning culture (expectations, accountability) is less photogenic than avocado toast

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that perks = culture
- The belief that happy employees automatically produce results
- The comfort of doing the easy parts of culture

**Verification:**
> "Rather than talk about expectations about the work and about results... They started at a culture committee. So they had a culture committee to talk about how we can make people happier around here... There's now layers and layers of things where we're trying to focus on engagement."

### 4. Co-Founders Should Discuss Monetary Vision of Success

**INSIGHT:** One co-founder said wistfully, "I don't see why we have to grow. I just wish we could have fewer employees." But they were already venture-backed and the other co-founder wanted a massive company. They never discussed this. By the time it surfaced, it was too late.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Discussing money and outcomes feels awkward at the honeymoon phase of founding.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Having this conversation requires admitting you might have different goals.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** In the excitement of starting, you assume alignment.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- The conversation is awkward before there's a problem
- Founders assume they share vision because they share excitement
- "We're both ambitious" obscures radically different definitions of success
- Raising the question feels like doubting the partnership

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that co-founders automatically share the same vision
- The belief that alignment on the idea means alignment on the outcome
- The comfort of not asking hard questions early

**Verification:**
> "Unfortunately, they were already venture backed and also, the other co-founder had a very lofty ambition for a very big company. And since they hadn't talked about that, it was way too late to even have that conversation and it was a very painful reckoning."

### 5. Burnout from the Failure to Confront

**INSIGHT:** Not having difficult conversations creates its own exhaustion. The mental load of avoiding, the growing problem, the anxiety of the unresolved—this drains more energy than the confrontation would. Avoiding the hard thing is harder than doing it.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We experience the dread of confrontation but not the cost of avoidance. The comparison is invisible.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Admitting avoidance causes burnout means admitting you're doing it to yourself.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- The cost of avoidance is diffuse; the cost of confrontation is concentrated
- "I'm burnt out from work" sounds better than "I'm burnt out from avoiding work"
- We don't track the energy drain of unresolved issues
- The short-term relief of avoidance is more salient than the long-term cost

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that confrontation is the hard thing
- The belief that avoiding difficulty saves energy
- The comfort of blaming external factors for exhaustion

**Verification:**
> (Implicit throughout the conversation and connecting to Lenny's opening: "I personally dread difficult conversations... I worry about the reaction.")

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Ask yourself: "Have I been crystal clear?"** - If the answer involves hand-waving, you haven't
2. **Discuss vision of success with co-founders** - "What does winning look like?" may have very different answers
3. **Audit your culture for avocado toast** - Are you building rituals or results?

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Your job isn't to make employees happy** - Happiness is a byproduct of winning, not a goal
2. **Avoidance costs more than confrontation** - The mental load of unresolved issues drains more than having the conversation
3. **Perks ≠ culture** - The visible things (avocado toast) can mask the invisible problems (no accountability)

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Alisa's antimemes share a structure: **avoidance masquerades as kindness but is actually selfishness**. Not giving feedback feels like protecting someone but protects yourself from discomfort. Not discussing founder expectations feels like harmony but sets up painful reckoning. Not pushing employees feels like caring but leads to their failure. The antimemetic quality comes from mistaking short-term comfort (for you) with long-term care (for them). The person who seems harsh—who gives hard feedback, who asks uncomfortable questions, who pushes for results—is actually the one who cares most.

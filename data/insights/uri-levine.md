## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Certainty Beats Time Savings as Core Value

**INSIGHT:** "Users don't primarily want to save time; they want predictability. Waze's real value emerged as knowing arrival time, not avoiding traffic."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Time savings is the value proposition.
- **System Antimeme:** Navigation apps save time.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Optimize for fastest routes.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "Save time" is intuitive framing
- Navigation = speed seems obvious
- Certainty is less tangible
- The reframe requires deep user insight

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that time savings drive value
- The belief that speed is the metric
- The comfort of obvious value props

**Verification:**
> "Creating certainty creates way more value than saving time."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Reframe value from time to certainty** - What users actually want
2. **Fire quickly to retain top performers** - Delay drives exits
3. **Remove unused features** - Fail fast means subtraction

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Certainty beats time savings** - Waze's real insight
2. **Top performers leave from indecision** - Not dysfunction
3. **You can't predict how different users behave** - Watching is mandatory

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Uri's antimemes share a structure: **building unicorns requires accepting uncomfortable truths about value, people, and products—certainty creates more value than time savings despite navigation seeming about speed, top performers leave when leaders avoid hard decisions not just when teams dysfunction, you cannot predict how early majority users behave from your own usage, and fail fast means removing features not pivoting quickly**. Sell certainty. Fire quickly. Watch users. Remove features. The pattern reveals that building Waze taught the opposite of startup conventional wisdom—where the value prop was wrong, fast firing retains talent, empathy isn't enough, and progress means subtraction.

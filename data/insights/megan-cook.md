## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. The Opposite of Play Is Fear, Not Work

**INSIGHT:** "Flow states require psychological safety. People aren't being 'lazy,' they're operating in fear mode, which kills innovation."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / Psychological

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** The opposite of play is work.
- **Psychological Antimeme:** Low performance is laziness.
- **System Antimeme:** Pressure drives results.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Work/play dichotomy is assumed
- Laziness is blamed
- Pressure is valued
- The fear mechanism is hidden

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that work opposes play
- The belief that low performance is laziness
- The comfort of pressure-based management

**Verification:**
> Cook references sports psychologist Ben Crowe's framework: flow states require psychological safety. This reframes team dysfunction—people aren't being "lazy," they're operating in fear mode, which kills innovation.

### Most Actionable Antimemes

2. **Meet 3x yearly for lasting productivity** - Seasonal not continuous
3. **Schedule conflict intentionally** - Fight Club prevents bigger problems

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **The opposite of play is fear** - Not work
2. **Size inflection at 15 people** - Earlier than expected
3. **Productivity gains are seasonal** - 3x yearly meetings last months

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Megan's antimemes share a structure: **product team effectiveness requires accepting counterintuitive truths about safety, scale, presence, and conflict—the opposite of play is fear not work because fear kills innovation, the size inflection point is 15 people which is earlier than expected, productivity gains are seasonal with 3x yearly meetings lasting months, and scheduled conflict prevents bigger problems rather than causing dysfunction**. Address fear. Watch the 15-person threshold. Meet seasonally. Schedule disagreement. The pattern reveals that leading product at Atlassian taught that team effectiveness comes from inversions that feel uncomfortable—where fear explains low performance, small is the limit, occasional beats constant, and scheduled conflict beats harmony-seeking.

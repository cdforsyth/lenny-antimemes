## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

**INSIGHT:** "Experimentation is expensive. Teams should experiment primarily for risk mitigation when making dramatic changes, not for metric precision alone." A/B testing has hidden costs that make it overused.

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Data-driven means testing everything.
- **System Antimeme:** Experimentation is best practice.
- **Social Antimeme:** "Let's test it" ends debates.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- A/B testing is the gold standard
- "More data" feels like progress
- The costs of testing aren't tracked
- Testing provides social cover

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that more testing is better
- The belief that experiments are free
- The comfort of deferring to data

**Verification:**
> "Experimentation is expensive... teams should experiment primarily for risk mitigation when making dramatic changes."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Use harder activation metrics** - 5-15% range predicts retention
2. **Separate pricing logic from onboarding** - Don't let tiers dictate UX
3. **Experiment for risk mitigation only** - Testing isn't free

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Experimentation is expensive** - A/B testing is overused
2. **Harder metrics are better metrics** - Counterintuitive correlation
3. **Company interests masquerade as user needs** - The invisible trap

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Lauryn's antimemes share a structure: **growth best practices (A/B testing, high activation rates, feature-based onboarding) often optimize for what's measurable or comfortable rather than what actually drives retention—and the counterintuitive moves (less testing, harder metrics, user-first design) require resisting institutional defaults**. Don't test everything. Make activation harder. Separate pricing from UX. Build for users, not metrics. The pattern reveals that growth teams systematically over-index on measurement and under-index on user value creation.

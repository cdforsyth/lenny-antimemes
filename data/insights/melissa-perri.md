## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Everyone Who Successfully Implemented SAFe Eventually Abandoned It

**INSIGHT:** "Everyone who successfully implemented SAFe eventually abandoned it for something customized." Success with the framework leads to leaving it—the endpoint of good SAFe implementation is no longer using SAFe.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** SAFe is sold as the destination.
- **Social Antimeme:** Abandoning frameworks looks like failure.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Success should mean continuing.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- SAFe vendors have financial interest in permanence
- "Abandoning" sounds negative
- Success narratives don't include leaving
- The customization endpoint isn't marketed

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that frameworks are destinations
- The belief that SAFe certification is the goal
- The comfort of permanent process solutions

**Verification:**
> "Everyone who successfully implemented SAFe eventually abandoned it for something customized."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Question certification value** - Two days doesn't create competence
2. **Plan for framework evolution** - SAFe success means leaving SAFe
3. **Invest in patience** - No transformation shortcuts exist

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **SAFe success = SAFe abandonment** - The endpoint is customization
2. **PO was designed for developers, not product** - Origin determines limitations
3. **Process compliance can bankrupt** - Frameworks aren't safe

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Melissa's antimemes share a structure: **the structures that promise product transformation (frameworks, certifications, role titles) often prevent it—and the only path through is patient, custom work that can't be bought or shortcut**. SAFe success leads to SAFe abandonment. Certifications create false confidence. The PO role was never designed for strategy. The pattern reveals that product transformation requires accepting there's no packaged solution, which is exactly what organizations don't want to hear.

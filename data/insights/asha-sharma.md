## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. GUIs Are Being Replaced by Code-Native Interfaces

**INSIGHT:** Just as databases moved from desktop to SQL, and cloud moved from consoles to Terraform, AI interfaces are moving from GUIs to code-native (terminal, composable) formats. A stream of text connects better with LLMs. Product builders spend too much time on UI when they should think about composability and how agents will read their product.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**

- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Product development culture is built around designing beautiful interfaces.
- **System Antimeme:** Every product playbook starts with UI/UX; this says start with composability.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Terminals look like a regression to non-tech users.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "Good design = good product" is deeply embedded
- Terminals look like the past, not the future
- Product teams are hired for UI/UX skills
- The composability benefit is invisible to end users

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that UI is the primary interface
- The belief that visual design is the core product skill
- The comfort of designing for human eyes first

**Verification:**
> "I think we're literally just seeing the same pattern that's played out in history... product makers really need to rewire their mindset around this because I think we spend an inordinate amount of time thinking about the UI of something rather than how something composes."

### 2. The Loop Not the Lane

**INSIGHT:** What matters isn't which function you're in (the lane), but how fast you can complete the feedback loop: ingest data, digest the rewards model, create an outcome. "The loop is the product, the loop is the IP." Functions start to blur because the competitive advantage is loop velocity, not functional expertise.

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Organizations are structured by function (PM, Eng, Design) - by lane.
- **Social Antimeme:** Careers are built in lanes; loop velocity has no clear career path.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** We've been trained to specialize, not to optimize loops.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Org charts are organized by function
- Job titles are functional
- Performance reviews measure functional skills
- "What's your loop velocity?" isn't a normal question

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that functional excellence is the goal
- The belief that career advancement happens within lanes
- The comfort of a defined professional role

**Verification:**
> "I think it's all about the loop, not the lane here. And so I think that whatever function you are, you have to be obsessed with trying to understand the efficiency or the cost of the product, the actual rewards or system design."

### 3. 500 Touch Points Is Insufficient for AI Speed

**INSIGHT:** An average product org has 10 steps to launch, 5+ functions, 6-7 layers. That's 500+ touch points to get something out. When there are 500 models released per week, 500 touch points per product is way too slow. This is why full-stack builders are resurgent - you need one person who can do the whole loop.

You don't think about layers and communication up the chain; you think about which human or agent is working on which task.

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Reduce touch points** - If you have 500 touch points, you're too slow for AI speed
2. **Obsess over the loop** - Feedback velocity matters more than functional excellence
3. **Invest in invisible infrastructure** - Reliability, privacy, performance beat features

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **GUIs → code-native interfaces** - Composability over canvas
2. **Org chart → work chart** - Tasks and agents replace hierarchy
3. **Marginal cost approaching zero** - Exponential demand follows near-zero production cost

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Asha's antimemes share a structure: **what was the constraint is becoming free, and what was free is becoming the constraint**. Production used to be the constraint (expensive outputs); now it's approaching zero cost. GUIs were the interface; now they're overhead. Org charts were how we coordinated; now they're too slow. The antimemetic quality comes from inversion - the things we spent decades optimizing (functional specialization, beautiful UIs, hierarchical coordination) are becoming the bottlenecks, while the things that were bottlenecks (production cost, development velocity) are dissolving. The winners will be those who recognize which inversions have already happened.

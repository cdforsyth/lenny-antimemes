## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Proactively Identify Problems Before Business Requests

**INSIGHT:** Relentless curiosity isn't just a personality trait - it "enables PMs to proactively identify problems before explicit business requests." The function of curiosity is predictive problem-finding, not just learning. Curious PMs surface issues before stakeholders even know to ask.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Implementation / Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Curiosity is framed as learning, not prediction.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Problems come from stakeholders in reactive model.
- **Social Antimeme:** "Proactive" sounds like overstepping.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Curiosity is valued as personal trait, not function
- PM role is often defined as reactive to requests
- Proactive problem-finding isn't in job descriptions
- The predictive function isn't obvious

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that problems come from stakeholders
- The belief that curiosity is just personality
- The comfort of reactive problem-solving

**Verification:**
> "This trait enables PMs to proactively identify problems before explicit business requests."

### 2. Humans as Backstop, Not Addition

**INSIGHT:** PLG means "your product job is to grow revenue and you use humans as a backstop." The framing matters: humans aren't added to product-led motion, they're a safety net for when product alone can't close. The product carries the load; humans catch exceptions.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** "PLG plus sales" sounds like addition.
- **System Antimeme:** Sales teams don't want to be "backstops."
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Hybrid feels like balance, not hierarchy.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "Backstop" implies secondary status
- Sales organizations resist subordinate framing
- "PLG + sales" avoids the hierarchy question
- The framing has organizational implications

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that hybrid means equal partnership
- The belief that sales adds to PLG
- The comfort of avoiding hierarchy discussions

**Verification:**
> "Taking a go-to-market approach where your product job is to grow revenue and you use humans as a backstop."

### 3. Understaffing and Premature Return Expectations

**INSIGHT:** Common PLG mistakes include "understaffing growth teams" and "expecting premature returns." These are sequence errors: companies invest too little too early, then expect results too fast. The right pattern is adequate staffing with patient timelines.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Budgets constrain staffing; boards expect returns.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Patience is hard to operationalize.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Understaffed teams can still "try PLG."

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Resource constraints are real
- Investor expectations set timelines
- "Lean" feels responsible, not understaffed
- Premature expectations aren't recognized as such

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that small teams can test PLG
- The belief that quick returns prove the model
- The comfort of constrained investment

**Verification:**
> "Common PLG mistakes include understaffing growth teams, expecting premature returns."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Start PLG before scale** - Qualitative methods work early
2. **Fix data hygiene first** - Infrastructure precedes tactics
3. **Staff adequately, expect returns later** - Avoid common sequence errors

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Curiosity predicts problems** - Not just learning trait, but forecasting function
2. **Humans as backstop, not addition** - PLG hierarchy, not partnership
3. **Scale isn't prerequisite** - PLG adapts to stage

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Miller's antimemes share a structure: **the visible parts of PLG (experiments, metrics, sales collaboration) distract from the invisible foundations (curiosity-driven problem prediction, data hygiene, adequate resourcing, stage-appropriate methods)**. The backstop framing reveals the actual hierarchy hidden in "hybrid." The scale prerequisite turns out to be false. The pattern reveals that PLG success depends on getting the foundations right - infrastructure, staffing, timing - rather than optimizing the visible tactics.

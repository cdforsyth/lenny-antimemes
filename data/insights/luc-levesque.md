**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Respect the no.
- **Temporal Antimeme:** Move on after rejection.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Don't push too hard.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Respecting no is polite
- Moving on seems mature
- Persistence seems pushy
- The multi-year timeline is unusual

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that no means no
- The belief that rejection is final
- The comfort of moving on

**Verification:**
> "No doesn't necessarily mean no...be relentless, involve the family, involve the spouse."

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Luc's antimemes share a structure: **growth leadership at scale requires breaking professional conventions—"no" isn't permanent in recruitment and multi-year persistence works, YOLO beats experimentation when intuition is strong and speed matters, SEO can show results in days not months for existing pages, and involving families in executive recruiting dramatically increases conversion**. Persist after no. Skip testing sometimes. Expect fast SEO wins. Include families. The pattern reveals that growth leadership at Meta and Shopify taught that effectiveness comes from breaking polite professional norms—where persistence beats respect, intuition beats data, speed beats patience, and family involvement beats professional boundaries.

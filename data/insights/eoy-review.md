## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Losing to "No Decision" Is Your Real Competition

**INSIGHT:** "April Dunford reveals that B2B companies lose ~40% of deals not to competitors, but to inaction. Most positioning strategies ignore the spreadsheet and status quo as competitors, yet these are the primary obstacles to conversion."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Market / Narrative

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Market Antimeme:** Competitors are your competition.
- **Narrative Antimeme:** Win against rivals.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Differentiate from alternatives.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Competitors are focused on
- Rivals are battled
- Alternatives are differentiated against
- The inaction problem is missed

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that competitors are competition
- The belief that winning against rivals matters most
- The comfort of competitive positioning

**Verification:**
> B2B companies lose ~40% of deals not to competitors, but to inaction.

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Position against inaction, not competitors** - 40% lost to no decision
2. **Consider smaller teams for higher output** - Geometric coordination reduction
3. **Solve retention before product-led acquisition** - Habit loops first

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **No decision is your real competition** - 40% of deals lost to inaction
2. **Fewer people produce more** - Exponential communication friction
3. **Status quo explains most failures** - Yesterday's behavior dominates

### Pattern Across Antimemes

This year-end compilation's antimemes share a structure: **product and growth success requires accepting counterintuitive truths about competition, scale, and behavior—losing to no decision is your real competition because 40% of deals are lost to inaction not competitors, team size reduces coordination overhead geometrically because additional humans create exponential friction, status quo effect explains most product failures because humans follow yesterday's behavior, and product-led acquisition requires solved retention first because users who don't habitually engage can't refer**. Position against inaction. Consider smaller teams. Address status quo. Solve retention first. The pattern reveals that Lenny's best guests taught that success comes from inversions that feel backward—where inaction is the enemy, fewer is more, yesterday beats today, and retention precedes acquisition.

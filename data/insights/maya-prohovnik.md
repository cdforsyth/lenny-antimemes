## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Heavy Dogfooding Reveals What Metrics Miss

**INSIGHT:** "Intensive product usage (dogfooding) surfaces problems that metrics don't capture. The qualitative experience of being a heavy user generates insights that dashboards can't—friction, confusion, and delight aren't fully measured."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**

- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 3
- Implication: 4
- Specificity: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** System / Epistemic

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **System Antimeme:** Data dashboards are the source of truth.
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Metrics are more objective than experience.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** One person's experience isn't representative.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Data is scalable, dogfooding isn't
- Personal experience seems anecdotal
- Metrics feel more rigorous
- Time for heavy usage is scarce

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that metrics capture product quality
- The belief that dashboards show the full picture
- The comfort of data-driven decisions

**Verification:**
> "Heavy dogfooding reveals what metrics miss."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Validate gut decisions with post-hoc data** - Decide fast, measure after
2. **Dogfood heavily** - Surfaces what dashboards miss
3. **Question completion rate** - Engagement ≠ value

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Over-optimization kills discovery** - Efficiency and exploration trade off
2. **Podcasts are private** - Social features don't transfer from music
3. **Metrics miss qualitative experience** - Dashboards aren't complete

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Maya's antimemes share a structure: **the standard product assumptions (data-driven decisions, optimization improves outcomes, engagement equals value, audio products are similar) break down in podcast products specifically**. Metrics miss what dogfooding reveals. Optimization reduces discovery. Completion doesn't indicate value. Music patterns don't transfer. The pattern reveals that podcast product work requires questioning assumptions that seem universal but are actually domain-specific.

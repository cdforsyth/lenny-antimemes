## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Product Managers Are Becoming Vulnerable

**INSIGHT:** "Feature team product managers and backlog administrators face obsolescence through AI automation. Administrative PM roles lack defensible value. This reframes the PM crisis as a skill-level problem, not a role-validity problem."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Identity / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Identity Antimeme:** PM is a stable career.
- **System Antimeme:** All PM roles are valuable.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Follow PM best practices.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- PM careers seem stable
- All PM roles seem needed
- Best practices provide guidance
- The vulnerability is uncomfortable

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that PM is safe
- The belief that all PM roles are valuable
- The comfort of established practices

**Verification:**
> Marty argues that "feature team product managers" and backlog administrators face obsolescence through AI automation. Unlike empowered PMs who solve viability problems, administrative PM roles lack defensible value. This reframes the PM crisis as a skill-level problem, not a role-validity problem.

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Upgrade PM skills to avoid obsolescence** - Administrative roles are vulnerable
2. **Resist over-hiring** - Small teams produce more
3. **Question online PM advice** - 90% propagates wrong methodology

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **PMs are becoming vulnerable** - AI automates backlog administration
2. **Small teams produce more** - Over-hiring creates ridiculous roles
3. **Most online advice is wrong** - Mediocre company practices spread

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Marty's antimemes share a structure: **product leadership requires accepting uncomfortable truths about roles, scale, and conventional wisdom—product managers are becoming vulnerable because AI can automate administrative PM work, small teams produce more than large ones because over-hiring creates ridiculous roles, remote work has measurably harmed innovation despite being taboo to discuss, and most online product advice is wrong because mediocre companies propagate feature-team methodology**. Upgrade skills. Stay small. Question remote dogma. Ignore popular advice. The pattern reveals that decades of product leadership taught that effectiveness comes from accepting the uncomfortable—where some PM roles are vulnerable, less produces more, remote harms innovation, and popular advice is wrong.

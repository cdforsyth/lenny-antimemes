## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Address indecision not competition** - 40-60% are no-decision
2. **Address FOMU not FOMO** - Fear of messing up is the blocker
3. **Make recommendations** - Sharing risk increases close rates

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **The indecision crisis** - Most deals lost to paralysis not competition
2. **The FOMU paradox** - FOMO backfires 87% of the time
3. **Omission bias trumps status quo** - Active losses feared more

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Matt's antimemes share a structure: **sales effectiveness requires recognizing that the real blockers are psychological not competitive—most deals fail to indecision not competition, FOMU matters more than FOMO because customers fear blame for failed decisions, omission bias is more powerful than status quo bias because active losses trigger deeper fear, and the delegation effect shows that recommendations reduce anxiety by sharing risk**. Address indecision. Reduce FOMU. Understand omission bias. Make recommendations. The pattern reveals that 2.5 million sales conversations taught that closing requires addressing hidden psychological barriers—where paralysis beats competition, blame-fear beats missing-out-fear, active-loss-fear beats inertia, and recommendations beat neutrality.

# Antimeme Analysis: Jules Walter on Lenny's Podcast

**Source:** Lenny's Podcast - Jules Walter Interview (Product Leader, ex-Slack, YouTube)
**Analyzed:** 2026-01-20

---

## Stage 1: Popular vs. Rare Concepts

### Popular Takeaways (80-90% of listeners would mention)

1. **Two skill buckets** - Intellectual (execution, strategy) and EQ (communication, leadership)
2. **Focus on one skill at a time** - Not simultaneous improvement
3. **Make smallest ask possible** - When seeking mentors
4. **Work backward from outcomes** - Not passive learning
5. **Circle back to show application** - Mentor relationship building
6. **Enthusiastic gratitude for criticism** - Signals openness

### Rare/Antimemetic Insights (1-5% would notice)

1. **EQ requires building muscles** - Not intellectual understanding
2. **Subjective feedback is most valuable** - But rarely offered
3. **"What people say you're good at but you think is no big deal"** - Strength diagnostic
4. **Email first, not meetings** - Specific questions over open-ended asks
5. **Demonstrate value before asking more** - Implementation sharing
6. **Natural strengths worth amplifying** - Rather than fixing weaknesses

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Subjective Feedback Is Most Valuable But Rarely Offered

**INSIGHT:** He stresses enthusiastic gratitude when receiving criticism to encourage people to share "honest, subjective feedback about interpersonal impact—the most valuable but rarely offered type."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Subjective feedback is uncomfortable.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Objective feedback is more useful.
- **System Antimeme:** Facts are actionable; opinions aren't.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Subjective feels like opinion
- Interpersonal impact is sensitive
- People avoid giving it
- The value isn't obvious

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that objective feedback is best
- The belief that facts are more actionable
- The comfort of avoiding subjective territory

**Verification:**
> "Honest, subjective feedback about interpersonal impact—the most valuable but rarely offered type."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Find your dismissed strength** - "Good at but no big deal"
2. **Start with smallest ask** - Email questions, not meetings
3. **Show gratitude enthusiastically** - Unlocks subjective feedback

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Subjective feedback is most valuable** - But rarely offered
2. **Natural strengths deserve amplification** - Not weaknesses
3. **EQ requires muscle building** - Not just understanding

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Jules's antimemes share a structure: **career acceleration requires pursuing the counterintuitive, small, and subjective approaches that ambitious people dismiss—subjective feedback about interpersonal impact is most valuable but rarely offered, natural strengths that seem "no big deal" deserve amplification over weaknesses, EQ skills require muscle building rather than intellectual understanding, and the smallest ask possible works better than ambitious meeting requests**. Seek subjective feedback. Amplify dismissed strengths. Practice EQ repeatedly. Start tiny. The pattern reveals that PM career acceleration comes from the humble, iterative, interpersonal work that ambitious professionals skip—where subjective feedback, natural strengths, practice over understanding, and tiny asks outperform the objective, weakness-fixing, knowledge-focused, big-ask approaches that seem more impressive.

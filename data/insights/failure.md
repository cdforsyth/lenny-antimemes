Execution excellence matters less than psychological safety when implementing organizational shifts."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Build trust before pushing change** - Credibility precedes execution
2. **Design conclusive failures not ambiguous tests** - Definitive beats incremental
3. **Check the math before perfecting execution** - Economics can't be iterated out of

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Trust precedes change** - Bringing people along matters more than being right
2. **Conclusive failure beats ambiguous setbacks** - Well-designed failure eliminates questions
3. **The math trumps execution brilliance** - Broken economics can't be fixed by product

### Pattern Across Antimemes

This failure compilation's antimemes share a structure: **learning from failure requires accepting counterintuitive truths about trust, finality, and visibility—trust precedes change because execution excellence matters less than bringing people along, conclusive failure is more valuable than ambiguous setbacks because well-designed failure eliminates future iterations, the math trumps execution brilliance because no product can overcome broken unit economics, and public failure can become social capital because spectacular recovery beats quiet mediocrity**. Build trust first. Fail conclusively. Check the math. Embrace visibility. The pattern reveals that these failure stories taught that growth comes from inversions that feel risky—where trust precedes action, big failures help, math beats product, and public failure builds credibility.

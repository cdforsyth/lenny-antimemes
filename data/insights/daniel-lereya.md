## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Goal Magnitude Changes Approach Type

**INSIGHT:** "Reducing timelines from 'four months to three months' often means people work longer hours, whereas 'transformative goals' drive structural innovation and process redesign." Incremental improvement gets incremental effort; transformative goals get transformed approaches.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Stretch goals motivate harder work.
- **System Antimeme:** Continuous improvement is sustainable.
- **Social Antimeme:** Impossible goals demoralize.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Stretch goals are the playbook
- The magnitude distinction isn't taught
- Continuous improvement is safe
- "Impossible" seems unfair

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that stretch goals just stretch effort
- The belief that improvement is linear
- The comfort of achievable-seeming targets

**Verification:**
> "Reducing timelines from 'four months to three months' often means people work longer hours, whereas 'transformative goals' drive structural innovation."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Set transformative, not stretch goals** - Magnitude changes approach type
2. **Be relentless about impact validation** - Not just shipping
3. **Display metrics pre-IPO** - Transparency creates partnership

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Goal magnitude changes thinking** - Stretch gets hours; transformative gets innovation
2. **Infrastructure as competitive advantage** - Multi-year investment, not cost center
3. **Relentless impact validation** - The PM job continues after launch

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Daniel's antimemes share a structure: **shipping velocity at scale requires counterintuitive investments in transparency, infrastructure, and goal-setting that seem to slow things down but actually enable speed—transformative goals change thinking not just effort, infrastructure investment creates competitive advantage not cost, pre-IPO transparency creates partnership not risk, and great PMs are relentless about impact not just launches**. Set impossible goals. Invest in infrastructure. Share everything. Validate impact. The pattern reveals that Monday's velocity comes from commitments that seem like they would slow execution but actually enable it through structural change rather than incremental effort.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- PM career paths emphasize strategy, not support

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that PM skills come from PM activities
- The belief that strategic work teaches more than operational work
- The comfort of the standard PM career path

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Ops work as learning** - Spend time in support/operations to understand the business deeply
2. **Trust intuition in low-volume contexts** - Don't force experimentation when sample sizes can't support it
3. **Signup-based review cadence** - Let teams control when they present; limit slots to ensure quality

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Finding the kernel of truth** - Most signals are noise; the job is aggressive filtering
2. **Vertical integration complexity** - Tech companies systematically underestimate operational depth
3. **Never as good, never as bad** - Emotional calibration as leadership technique

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Brian's antimemes share a structure: **the rigorous-looking approach often fails, and the "unrigorous" approach is actually more rigorous**. Data-driven experimentation fails at low volume; intuition works. Comprehensive product reviews create politics; signup-based cadence creates accountability. Strategic PM thinking misses the business; support ticket work reveals it. Software company approaches miss vertical complexity; ops-first thinking captures it. The pattern reveals that the signals of rigor (data, comprehensive analysis, frequent oversight) often obscure actual rigor, which lives in constraint recognition, operational depth, and knowing when standard approaches don't apply.

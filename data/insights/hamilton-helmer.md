### Rare/Antimemetic Insights (1-5% would notice)

## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Benefit Without Barrier Isn't Power

**INSIGHT:** "You have to have something that you do that gives you a better outcome than your competitors, lower cost or higher price, AND something that makes it impossible for somebody else to mimic that." Both required. Benefit alone isn't power.

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Epistemic / Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Epistemic Antimeme:** Advantages are called "competitive" regardless of barriers.
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** "We're better" feels like power.
- **Social Antimeme:** Pitches emphasize benefits, not barriers.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Advantages feel sufficient
- Barrier analysis is uncomfortable
- "We're better" is easier to claim
- The AND requirement is strict

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that being better is enough
- The belief that advantages are automatically defensible
- The comfort of focusing only on benefits

**Verification:**
> "You have to have something... lower cost or higher price, AND then something that makes it impossible for somebody else to mimic that."

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Require both benefit AND barrier** - Incomplete claims aren't power
2. **Remove three powers early** - Branding, process, resource-based come later
3. **Assess network effect materiality** - Modest isn't enough

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Operational excellence rarely constitutes power** - Opacity condition rarely met
2. **Benefit without barrier isn't power** - AND requirement
3. **Founders overestimate advantages** - Default is overclaiming

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Hamilton's antimemes share a structure: **the claims that sound like power (benefits, great teams, network effects, brand) often fail the strict definition (benefit + barrier, material impact, opacity)**. The framework isn't about identifying power - it's about eliminating false claims of power. Founders overclaim because the strict definition disqualifies most advantages. The pattern reveals that strategic power is much rarer than strategic rhetoric - most "moats" aren't.

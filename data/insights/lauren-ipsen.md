## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Big Names Don't Equal Best Fit

**INSIGHT:** "The most experienced recruiters deliberately avoid 'shiny objects'—famous executives from Google or YouTube. These leaders are often 'far from the work' and require building entire teams beneath them."

**CLASSIFICATION:** Paradigm

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y

- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Recognition: 2
- Implication: 5
- Specificity: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / System

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 9

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Famous names signal quality.
- **System Antimeme:** Big company experience transfers.
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Hire impressive resumes.

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Famous names are valued
- Big company experience seems valuable
- Impressive resumes attract
- The "far from work" problem is hidden

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The assumption that fame equals capability
- The belief that big company experience transfers
- The comfort of impressive credentials

**Verification:**
> The most experienced recruiters deliberately avoid "shiny objects"—famous executives from Google or YouTube. These leaders are often "far from the work" and require building entire teams beneath them, making them poor choices for resource-constrained early-stage companies.

## Stage 3: Summary

### Most Actionable Antimemes

1. **Avoid shiny objects** - Famous executives are far from work
2. **Don't hide short tenures** - Omissions are bigger red flags
3. **Invest in long courtships** - 7 months beats immediate offers

### Most Paradigm-Shifting Antimemes

1. **Big names don't equal best fit** - Build teams beneath them
2. **Recruiters ignore resumes** - Stories trump documents
3. **Extended courtship yields better outcomes** - Relationships over speed

### Pattern Across Antimemes

Lauren's antimemes share a structure: **executive recruiting requires accepting counterintuitive truths about credentials, honesty, and timelines—big names don't equal best fit because famous executives are far from the work, hiding short tenures backfires because omissions are bigger red flags, recruiters ignore resumes at executive level because stories trump documents, and the 7-month courtship works because relationships beat fast offers**. Avoid famous names. Be transparent. Focus on story. Invest in relationships. The pattern reveals that executive recruiting taught that hiring success comes from inversions that feel risky—where unknown beats famous, transparency beats curation, story beats resume, and slow beats fast.
